# Level 8: Measuring the unreachable

*Measurement · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

Some things can never be reached with a ruler: the size of the Earth, the distance to Mars or to a star, the exact
area under a curve, the loudness of a sound or the strength of an earthquake. In this level geometry, light, limits and
logarithms measure them anyway. You follow Eratosthenes' shadow, time radio signals to Mars, measure stars by parallax,
trap a curved area between rectangles, see how the SI units are now built from constants of nature, and learn to read
the scales where each step means "ten times".

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Eratosthenes measures the Earth](01-eratosthenes-earth.md) | Explain his shadow method with parallel rays, compute a circumference from an angle and a distance, and judge how good his answer was |
| [Distances in space](02-distances-in-space.md) | Use the astronomical unit and the light-year, and explain why a signal to Mars takes 3 to 22 minutes |
| [Parallax](03-parallax.md) | Find distances from a baseline and a small angle in radians, and use parsecs and arcseconds for the stars |
| [Areas of curved regions](04-curved-areas.md) | Bound areas with grids and rectangles, prove the area under $y = x^2$ is $\tfrac13$, and follow Archimedes' parabola |
| [The SI system](05-si-system.md) | Name the seven base units, build derived units, and explain how the metre and kilogram are defined by fixed constants |
| [Logarithmic measures](06-logarithmic-measures.md) | Move between decibels, earthquake magnitudes and pH and the quantities they measure, and compare readings as ratios |
