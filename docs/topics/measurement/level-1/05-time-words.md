# Time words

*Measurement · level 1, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

Here is a riddle that sounds impossible.

"Today is the day that, yesterday, you called **tomorrow**."

Is that true? Think about it. Yesterday, you were looking ahead to the next day, and you called it "tomorrow". Then
you went to sleep, woke up, and that day came. Now you call it "**today**"! The day did not change. The **word**
changed, because you moved along in time.

Time is strange. You cannot see it, hold it, or put it on a balance. But you can still measure it and compare it.
In this lesson you will learn the words we use for time, and how to tell which things take longer.

## The idea

### The parts of a day

Every day has the same parts, in the same order.

- **Morning**: the Sun comes up. You wake up, eat breakfast, and go to school.
- **Afternoon**: after lunch. The Sun has been high in the sky.
- **Evening**: the Sun goes down. You eat supper and get ready for bed.
- **Night**: it is dark. You sleep.

Then morning comes again, and a new day starts. Why does this happen every day? The Earth is a huge ball that
**spins**, slowly, all the time. As it turns, the place where you live turns to face the Sun (morning), then faces it
fully (the middle of the day), then turns away from it (evening and night). One whole spin of the Earth takes **one
day**.

### Before, after, first, next, last

Time words help us say **the order** things happen in.

- **First** I put on my socks, **next** I put on my shoes, **last** I tie the laces.
- I eat breakfast **before** school. I play **after** school.

The order matters! Shoes before socks does not work. Many things in life have to happen in the right order, and time
words are how we talk about it.

### Yesterday, today, tomorrow

- **Today** is the day it is right now.
- **Yesterday** was the day just before today.
- **Tomorrow** is the day just after today.

These words are like a little window that slides along. Every morning, the window moves one box to the right. What
was "tomorrow" becomes "today", and what was "today" becomes "yesterday". That is why the riddle at the top is true.

Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday. Then Monday again! The days go **round and round** like
a circle, which is why the picture is a circle. Seven days make **one week**. Saturday and Sunday (the green ones) are
called the **weekend**. On many calendars in Canada the week is drawn starting on Sunday, and on others it starts on
Monday, but the order round the circle is always the same.

This is a **repeating pattern**, just like red, blue, red, blue. After Sunday comes Monday; the pattern starts again.
So if today is Friday, tomorrow is Saturday, and yesterday was Thursday.

### Which takes longer?

Some things take a short time and some take a long time.

- **Short**: blinking, clapping once, a sneeze, jumping once.
- **A bit longer**: brushing your teeth (about as long as singing a short song twice), putting on your shoes.
- **Long**: a night's sleep, a car trip to another city, a whole school day.

To compare two things that happen at **the same time**, start them together and see which one finishes first. Two
children start building towers when you say "go". The one who finishes first took a **shorter** time. This is like
lining up the ends of two pencils: the start is the starting line!

To compare things that happen at **different times**, count while they happen. Clap slowly and steadily, or count
"one elephant, two elephants...". Brushing your teeth might take about 100 slow counts. Putting on shoes might take
about 20. The bigger count took longer. Just like hand spans, counts are fair only if you count at the **same speed**
each time.

### A day on Mars

Mars spins too, so Mars also has mornings, afternoons, evenings and nights. A day on Mars is called a **sol**. Mars
spins a little more slowly than Earth, so a sol is a bit longer than our day: about 40 minutes longer. That is about
as long as a short cartoon film. If you lived on Mars, your bedtime would slip later and later compared with Earth
clocks!

## Worked examples

**Example 1. Put the day in order.** Put these in order: eat lunch, wake up, go to bed, eat breakfast.

1. Which happens first in the morning? **Wake up**.
2. Next comes **eat breakfast**, still in the morning.
3. Then, in the middle of the day, **eat lunch**.
4. Last, at night, **go to bed**.

**Example 2. Days of the week.** Today is Wednesday. What day was yesterday? What day will it be tomorrow? What
about the day after tomorrow?

1. Find Wednesday on the circle.
2. One step back is **Tuesday**: that was yesterday.
3. One step forward is **Thursday**: that is tomorrow.
4. Two steps forward is **Friday**: the day after tomorrow.

**Example 3. Which took longer?** Mia and Leo start a jigsaw puzzle each at the same moment. Mia finishes while Leo
still has 5 pieces left. Who took longer?

1. They started at the same time, so the start is fair, like lining up two pencils.
2. Mia finished first. Leo was still working.
3. So **Leo took longer**, and Mia took a shorter time.

## Try it

1. Do you eat breakfast in the morning, the afternoon or the night?
2. Which takes longer: a sneeze or a night's sleep? Eating an apple or blinking?
3. Today is Saturday. What day was yesterday? What day is tomorrow?
4. Say the days of the week starting from Thursday, all the way round to Wednesday.
5. Put in order: put on a coat, go outside, open the door.
6. Sam says, "I clapped 20 times while my sister tied her shoes, and 30 times while I tied mine. So I am faster."
   Is he right?
7. Today is Monday. In 7 days, what day will it be? Why?
8. How many days are there from Friday to the next Friday?

??? note "Answers"
    1. In the morning (breakfast is the first meal of the day).
    2. A night's sleep takes much longer than a sneeze. Eating an apple takes longer than a blink.
    3. Yesterday was **Friday**. Tomorrow is **Sunday**.
    4. Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday. That is all 7 days.
    5. Put on a coat, open the door, go outside. (You could also open the door first, then put on the coat, but you
       must open the door before you go outside.)
    6. No. More claps means **more** time, so Sam took **longer**. His sister was faster. (And the claps must be the
       same speed both times for the test to be fair.)
    7. **Monday** again. There are 7 days in a week, so 7 steps around the circle bring you all the way back to where
       you started.
    8. **7 days**. Count the steps: Saturday, Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday. That is one whole
       week.

## Common mistake

**Mixing up yesterday and tomorrow.** "Yester" goes **back**; "tomorrow" goes **forward**. A trick: tomorrow is the
day you are waiting for. Yesterday is already gone.

**Thinking more counts means faster.** If you count while something happens, a **bigger** count means it took
**longer**, not shorter.
