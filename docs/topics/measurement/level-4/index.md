# Level 4: Area and volume

*Measurement · level 4 of 8 · rough age 11-12 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 3 you found the area of rectangles by counting rows of squares. In this level you find the area of
parallelograms, triangles and trapezoids by cutting and moving pieces until they become rectangles, so each formula
comes with a reason you can see. Then you build shapes out of shapes, step up from squares to cubes to measure
volume, join volume to litres, and finish by measuring the size of Mars itself.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Area of a parallelogram](01-parallelogram-area.md) | Show why a parallelogram's area is base × height, and pick the true height instead of the slanted side |
| [Area of a triangle](02-triangle-area.md) | Show why a triangle is half a parallelogram, find the area of any triangle (obtuse ones too), and choose the right height |
| [Area of a trapezoid](03-trapezoid-area.md) | Use two copies to explain the trapezoid formula, and find a trapezoid's area or a missing side |
| [Composite shapes](04-composite-shapes.md) | Find the area of L-shapes, rooms and gardens by adding pieces or subtracting holes |
| [Volume by counting cubes](05-volume-cubes.md) | Measure volume in cubic centimetres, fill a box layer by layer, and explain length × width × height |
| [Capacity and volume together](06-capacity-volume.md) | Use 1 mL = 1 cm³ and 1 L = 1000 cm³ to find how many litres a tank holds, and measure odd shapes by water |
| [How big is Mars?](07-how-big-mars.md) | Compare the sizes of Mars, Earth and the Moon with numbers, a ruler and a ball model |
