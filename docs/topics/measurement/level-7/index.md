# Level 7: Precision, scale and dimension

*Measurement · level 7 of 8 · rough age 15-16 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

Up to now a measurement was a number and a unit. In this level it becomes a number, a unit **and an honest statement
of how well it is known**. You learn how precise an instrument can be, what the digits you write promise, and how
errors grow when measurements are combined. Then you treat units as algebra to check formulas and convert anything,
see how the square-cube law shapes animals and planets, and finish by estimating things nobody has measured.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [No measurement is exact](01-no-exact-measurement.md) | Give the precision and greatest possible error of a reading, write it as $x \pm \Delta x$ and as an interval, and tell precision from accuracy |
| [Significant figures](02-significant-figures.md) | Count significant figures, explain why 3.0 m and 3 m differ, and round sums and products honestly |
| [Errors that grow](03-errors-that-grow.md) | Combine errors in sums, differences, products, quotients and powers, and find percent error |
| [Dimensional analysis](04-dimensional-analysis.md) | Check a formula by its dimensions, convert units with factors equal to 1, and find a formula's form from dimensions alone |
| [The square-cube law in nature](05-square-cube-law.md) | Scale lengths, areas and volumes by $k$, $k^2$ and $k^3$, and explain thick elephant legs, cold shrews and slow-falling mice |
| [Fermi estimates](06-fermi-estimates.md) | Break an impossible-looking question into guessable pieces, bound unknowns with the geometric mean, and give the order of magnitude |
