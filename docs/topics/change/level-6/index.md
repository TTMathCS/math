# Level 6: Speed at an instant

*Change and motion · level 6 of 8 · rough age 13-14 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

A speedometer shows your speed at this very instant, yet speed is distance divided by time, and an instant has no
time in it. This level solves that old puzzle, the one that started calculus. You shrink the interval until the
average speeds settle on one number, watch secant lines turn into a tangent, estimate rates from real data, discover
that exponential growth has a rate proportional to its size, and find where change is fastest and where it stops.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [The problem of instant speed](01-instant-speed.md) | Explain why $0 \div 0$ is meaningless, and why instant speed is defined by where average speeds are heading |
| [Shrinking the interval](02-shrinking-interval.md) | Find an exact instant speed with tables and algebra, such as 19.6 m/s for the falling stone at 2 s |
| [From secant to tangent](03-secant-tangent.md) | Find the tangent line at a point as the limit of secant lines, and see why a smooth curve looks straight close up |
| [Estimating instantaneous rates from data and graphs](04-estimating-rates.md) | Draw a tangent by eye, and use forward, backward and central estimates from a table, knowing which to trust |
| [Rates in exponential change](05-exponential-rates.md) | Show that exponential growth and decay have a rate proportional to the amount, and use it for bacteria, savings and cooling |
| [Where is change fastest?](06-fastest-change.md) | Find the steepest point of an S-curve, and the highest and lowest points of a curve where the rate is zero |
