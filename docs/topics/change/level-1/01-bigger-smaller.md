Most people get this right very quickly: **C, then A, then B**. A plant starts as a seed, grows a little, then grows
a lot. But **how** did you know? Nobody told you which photo was taken first. You used an idea that is so useful that
we will spend the whole lesson on it: some things only grow **one way**.

## The idea

### Change over time

Things **change over time**. That means that if you look at something now, and then look at it again later, it may
not be the same. The word **time** here just means "now, a bit later, and later still", like the ticking of a clock
or the days on a calendar.

Here are some things that get **bigger** over time:

- a baby, who becomes a child, and then a grown-up;
- a puppy, who becomes a dog;
- a bean plant, from a seed to a tall green plant;
- a pile of leaves you are raking up;
- the pile of books you have read this year.

And here are some things that get **smaller** over time:

- a candle that is burning;
- a pencil that you keep sharpening;
- a snowman on a sunny day;
- a bar of soap that you use every day.

### Clues that tell you the order

When you put pictures in order from first to last, you are being a **time detective**. A detective looks for clues.
Here are the best clues for change.

**Clue 1: some things only get bigger.** A puppy does not turn back into a smaller puppy. A seed that has sprouted
does not go back into a seed. So for a growing thing, the **smallest** picture comes **first** and the **biggest**
picture comes **last**.

**Clue 2: some things only get smaller.** A burning candle never grows back. So for a shrinking thing, the
**biggest** picture comes **first** and the **smallest** comes **last**. This is the same clue turned around.

**Clue 3: other things change too, not only size.** A baby's hair grows longer. A banana turns from green to yellow
to brown with spots. A leaf turns from green to orange and falls off the tree. These changes also only go one way,
so they are clues too.

### Why the clues work

Why can you be so sure that a puppy picture with a **small** puppy came first? Because a puppy grows a tiny bit every
day. If it was small in one photo and big in another, then all the days of growing must have happened **between**
the two photos. The small photo had to be taken before those days, and the big photo after them.

A grown-up would say the puppy's size is a **record of time**: the bigger it is, the more time has gone by. That is
why a doctor can look at a child's bones and tell roughly how old the child is, and why the rings inside a tree trunk
tell how many years the tree has been growing (one new ring each year).

### When the clue does not work

Be careful. "Bigger means later" only works for something that **only grows**. Some things go up **and** down:

- A balloon can be blown up, then let down, then blown up again.
- A puddle can grow when it rains and shrink when the sun comes out, and then grow again in the next rain.
- The water in a glass goes down when you drink it, and up when you fill it again.

If you see two pictures of a puddle, a big one and a small one, you **cannot** be sure which came first. It might have
dried up, or it might have rained. A good detective says: "I need another clue." Maybe the ground around it is wet in
one picture and dry in the other, or maybe one picture shows rain.

### First, next, last

When you tell the story of a change, use **order words**:

- **first** (at the start),
- **next** or **then** (in the middle),
- **last** or **at the end**.

For the bean plant: "**First** there was only a seed in the soil. **Then** a small shoot came up with two leaves.
**At the end** it was a tall plant with lots of leaves."

Telling the story in order is the same as putting the pictures in order. Both are ways of saying **what happened,
and when**.

## Worked examples

**Example 1. Growing up.** Three photos show the same girl: in one she is a baby in a pram, in one she is riding a
bike, and in one she is a grown-up driving a car. Put them in order.

1. A person only grows up, never down. So this change goes one way.
2. The baby is the youngest, so that photo comes **first**.
3. Riding a bike comes **next**: she is a child.
4. Driving a car comes **last**: she is a grown-up.

Order: **baby, bike, car**.

**Example 2. A shrinking snowman.** Three pictures of a snowman: one tall with a hat and a scarf, one shorter and
leaning, one just a small lump of snow with the hat lying on top. The sun is out in all three.

1. On a sunny day snow melts, so the snowman only gets smaller.
2. The biggest snowman comes **first**.
3. The leaning one comes **next**.
4. The lump with the hat on top comes **last**.

Order: **tall, leaning, lump**. The hat did not change at all. Only the snow did.

**Example 3. Two pictures of a balloon.** One picture shows a big round balloon, the other a small balloon. Which came
first?

1. A balloon can get bigger (you blow it up) or smaller (air leaks out).
2. So size alone is **not enough**. We need another clue.
3. Suppose in the small picture the balloon is wrinkly and lying on the floor. Wrinkles appear when a balloon has
   been blown up and then lost air. So that one is probably **later**.

The answer is "big first, then small", but only because of the wrinkle clue.

**Example 4. A banana.** Three pictures: a yellow banana, a green banana, a brown banana with spots. Order?

1. A banana ripens one way: green, then yellow, then brown.
2. Order: **green, yellow, brown**.

Here the clue is colour, not size. The banana stays about the same size the whole time.

## Try it

1. Put in order from first to last: a big apple tree, an apple seed, a small tree as tall as you.
2. Put in order: a long new pencil, a very short pencil, a pencil half as long.
3. A cat in three photos: a tiny kitten, a big cat, a middle-sized cat. Which photo is **last**?
4. Name two things in your home that get **smaller** over time.
5. A cup of juice is full in one picture and half full in another. Can you be sure which came first? What clue
   would help?
6. A boy builds a tower of blocks. Picture A has 3 blocks, picture B has 7 blocks, picture C has 5 blocks. If he
   only ever adds blocks, what is the order?
7. Tell the story of a melting ice lolly using "first", "then" and "at the end".
8. Grown-up question: why can the rings inside a tree trunk tell you how old the tree is?

??? note "Answers"
    1. **Seed, small tree, big tree.** A tree only grows, so the smallest comes first.
    2. **Long, half, very short.** A pencil gets shorter each time you sharpen it, and it never grows back.
    3. The **big cat**. A kitten grows into a cat, so the biggest picture comes last.
    4. Many answers, for example: soap, toothpaste in the tube, a candle, a crayon, a roll of toilet paper, the
       cereal in the box.
    5. **No, not just from these pictures.** Someone could have drunk half (full first), or someone could be filling
       the cup (half first). A clue might be a jug pouring in one picture, or a child drinking with a straw.
    6. If he only adds blocks, the number only grows: **A (3), C (5), B (7)**.
    7. For example: "First the ice lolly was whole and cold. Then it started to drip and got smaller. At the end there
       was only the stick and a puddle of juice."
    8. A tree grows one new ring each year, and the old rings stay inside. So the number of rings is a record of how
       many years it has grown. Counting rings is counting years.

## Common mistake

**Thinking "bigger" always means "later".** That is only true for things that only grow, like children, trees and
puppies. Things that can go up **and** down, like balloons, puddles and the juice in a cup, need another clue.

**Forgetting the things that did not change.** When a snowman melts, the hat stays the same. Noticing what did **not**
change is just as clever as noticing what did. You will practise this in lesson 3.
