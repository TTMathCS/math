# How fast are things?

*Change and motion · level 4, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

Put these in order, slowest to fastest:

- a cheetah sprinting,
- the Curiosity rover driving on Mars,
- the sound of a clap travelling across a room,
- a person walking,
- a car on a highway at 100 km/h.

Easy? Here is the catch. The facts come in different units. The rover's top speed is about **4 centimetres per
second**. Sound travels about **343 metres per second**. The car does **100 kilometres per hour**. You cannot compare
4, 343 and 100 when they are counting different things. One of the surprises waiting for you: the cheetah and the
highway car are almost exactly as fast as each other.

## The idea

### Same units first

A speed is a rate: a distance per a time. Two speeds can only be compared fairly when they use the **same distance
unit** and the **same time unit**. So the first job is always to **convert**: change one speed into the other's units.

The two most common speed units are:

- **metres per second (m/s)**: good for people, animals, sound;
- **kilometres per hour (km/h)**: good for cars, bikes, trains.

### Why 1 m/s is 3.6 km/h

Something moving at 1 m/s goes 1 metre every second. How far in an hour?

- One hour is 60 minutes, and each minute is 60 seconds: $60 \times 60 = 3600$ seconds.
- So in an hour it goes 3600 metres.
- 1000 metres make a kilometre, so 3600 metres is $3600 \div 1000 = 3.6$ km.

So **1 m/s = 3.6 km/h**. That gives two rules:

- **m/s to km/h: multiply by 3.6.** (Each m/s is worth 3.6 km/h.)
- **km/h to m/s: divide by 3.6.**

A check that you used the right one: the km/h number is always the **bigger** number, because a kilometre is a big
unit but an hour is a long time, and the long time wins. 10 m/s is 36 km/h, not 2.8 km/h.

### Other units

You can convert to anything by the same thinking: ask "how far in one of the new time units?", then change the
distance unit.

- **cm/s to m/s:** 100 cm make a metre, so divide by 100. 4 cm/s is 0.04 m/s.
- **cm/s to km/h:** 4 cm/s is 0.04 m/s, and $0.04 \times 3.6 = 0.144$ km/h.
- **km/s to km/h:** an hour has 3600 seconds, so multiply by 3600.

### A table of speeds

Here are typical speeds, all in m/s and in km/h. Some are measured exactly; others (like the cheetah) vary from animal
to animal, so they are rough.

| Thing | m/s | km/h |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human hair growing (roughly 1 cm a month) | about 0.000 000 004 | about 0.000 000 014 |
| Curiosity rover on Mars, top speed | 0.04 | 0.144 |
| Person walking | about 1.4 | about 5 |
| Usain Bolt's 100 m world record (average) | about 10.4 | about 37.6 |
| Car on a highway | about 27.8 | 100 |
| Cheetah sprinting (short bursts) | roughly 28 | roughly 100 |
| Passenger jet cruising | about 250 | about 900 |
| Sound in air at 20 °C | about 343 | about 1235 |
| Earth moving round the Sun | about 30 000 | about 107 000 |
| Light | about 300 000 000 | about 1 080 000 000 |

Some of these deserve a closer look.

- **Usain Bolt** ran 100 m in 9.58 seconds in 2009. His average speed was $100 \div 9.58$, about 10.44 m/s. His top
  speed during the race was higher, because he started from standing still.
- **The cheetah** really is about as fast as a car on the highway, but only for a short sprint of a few hundred
  metres. A car can keep it up for hours.
- **Sound** depends on the air: in warmer air it is a little faster, in colder air a little slower. 343 m/s is the
  usual figure for 20 °C.
- **Light** is the fastest thing there is. In one second it could go round the Earth more than 7 times.

On this ladder, walking and the rover are about one and a half rungs apart (a walker is 35 times faster), and light is
more than six rungs above sound (almost a million times faster). Writing such huge and tiny numbers neatly is the job
of [Scientific notation](../../numbers/level-5/05-scientific-notation.md).

### Comparing: how many times faster?

To compare two speeds in the same units, you can **subtract** (how much faster) or **divide** (how many times as
fast). Dividing is usually more telling for very different speeds.

- Walker and rover: $1.4 \div 0.04 = 35$. A walker is 35 times as fast.
- Cheetah and walker: $28 \div 1.4 = 20$. A sprinting cheetah is about 20 times as fast as you walk.
- Light and sound: $300\,000\,000 \div 343$, about 870,000. Light is nearly a million times faster.

## Worked examples

**1. Walking in km/h.** A person walks at 1.4 m/s. In km/h: $1.4 \times 3.6 = 5.04$, about 5 km/h. That is why people
say "an hour's walk is about 5 km".

**2. Cheetah against Bolt.** In m/s, a cheetah sprints at roughly 28 and Usain Bolt averaged about 10.4. In km/h:
$28 \times 3.6$ is about 100, and $10.4 \times 3.6$ is about 37.5. The cheetah is about $28 \div 10.4$, roughly 2.7
times as fast as the fastest human ever timed.

**3. How far away is the storm?** You see lightning, then hear the thunder 6 seconds later. Light reaches you almost
instantly (in a few millionths of a second), but sound takes time. In 6 seconds, sound goes $343 \times 6 = 2058$
metres. The storm is about **2 km** away. A handy rule: **every 3 seconds is about 1 km**, because
$343 \times 3 = 1029$ metres.

**4. The rover's long drive.** At its top speed of 4 cm/s, how long would Curiosity take to drive 1 km without
stopping?

1 km is 100,000 cm. $100\,000 \div 4 = 25\,000$ seconds. In hours: $25\,000 \div 3600$, about 6.9 hours. In practice
it drives much more slowly than its top speed, stopping often to check its path and take pictures, and it has
travelled only a few tens of kilometres in many years on Mars.

**5. A message to Mars.** When Mars is 225 million km away, how long does a radio message (travelling at the speed of
light, about 300,000 km/s) take to get there?

$225\,000\,000 \div 300\,000 = 750$ seconds, which is 12.5 minutes. Depending on where the two planets are in their
orbits, the delay is anything from about 3 minutes to about 22 minutes. That is why nobody can drive a Mars rover
live with a joystick.

## Try it

1. Change 90 km/h into m/s.
2. Change 15 m/s into km/h.
3. Which is faster: 72 km/h or 18 m/s?
4. You hear thunder 9 seconds after the lightning. About how far away is the storm?
5. Walking at 1.4 m/s, how long does it take to walk 3 km? Give your answer in minutes.
6. Which is faster, a jet at 900 km/h or sound at 343 m/s? By how many km/h?
7. Sunlight travels about 150 million km to reach Earth. How long does it take, at 300,000 km/s?
8. Challenge: hair grows about 1 cm a month. How many millimetres per day is that (use 30 days in a month)? About how
   many times faster is a walker (1.4 m/s) than growing hair (about 0.000 000 004 m/s)?

??? note "Answers"
    1. $90 \div 3.6 = 25$ m/s.
    2. $15 \times 3.6 = 54$ km/h.
    3. $72 \div 3.6 = 20$ m/s, which is more than 18 m/s. So **72 km/h** is faster.
    4. $343 \times 9 = 3087$ metres: about **3 km** (9 seconds is 3 lots of 3 seconds).
    5. 3 km is 3000 m. $3000 \div 1.4$ is about 2143 seconds. $2143 \div 60$ is about **35.7 minutes**, roughly 36
       minutes. (Check with 5 km/h: 3 km takes $\frac{3}{5}$ of an hour, which is 36 minutes. ✓)
    6. Sound: $343 \times 3.6$, about 1235 km/h. Sound is faster, by about $1235 - 900 = 335$ km/h. (A jet that flew
       faster than sound would be "supersonic".)
    7. $150\,000\,000 \div 300\,000 = 500$ seconds, which is 8 minutes 20 seconds. The sunlight you see left the Sun
       about 8 minutes ago.
    8. 1 cm is 10 mm, so $10 \div 30$, about **0.33 mm per day**. Walker against hair: $1.4 \div 0.000\,000\,004$,
       which is $1.4 \times 250\,000\,000 = 350\,000\,000$. About **350 million** times faster. (With a more exact
       hair speed, about 0.000 000 003 9 m/s, it is about 360 million.)

## Common mistake

**Multiplying when you should divide.** To change km/h into m/s, divide by 3.6; to change m/s into km/h, multiply.
Check your answer: the km/h number must be **bigger** than the m/s number for the same speed.

**Comparing numbers without units.** "The rover does 4 and a walker does 1.4, so the rover is faster" is wrong: the
rover's 4 is centimetres per second, only 0.04 m/s, and the walker is 35 times faster. Convert to the same units
first, then compare.
