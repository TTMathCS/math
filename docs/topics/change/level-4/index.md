# Level 4: Rate of change

*Change and motion · level 4 of 8 · rough age 11-12 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 3 you found rates by sharing a total over a time. In this level the rate becomes a precise tool: **change
in output divided by change in input**, with units, for any table or graph. You learn why a constant rate always
draws a straight line, read distance as the area under a speed-time graph, tell steady change from growth that feeds
on itself, and convert speeds so that a Mars rover, a cheetah and a beam of light can be compared fairly.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Rate of change from a table](01-rate-from-table.md) | Find a rate of change between any two rows, with units, and tell it apart from "total ÷ input" |
| [Constant rate means a straight line](02-constant-rate-line.md) | Explain why a constant rate gives a straight line, and write and use the rule "start value + rate × input" |
| [Speed-time graphs](03-speed-time-graphs.md) | Read speeding up, slowing down and steady speed from a speed-time graph, and find distance as an area |
| [Linear and non-linear change](04-linear-nonlinear.md) | Tell linear from non-linear change in tables and graphs, with savings and the area of a square |
| [Reading rates from graphs](05-rates-from-graphs.md) | Find the fastest part of a journey and the biggest change in an hour, using rise ÷ run and the axis scales |
| [How fast are things?](06-how-fast.md) | Convert between m/s and km/h, and compare speeds from a growing hair to light |
