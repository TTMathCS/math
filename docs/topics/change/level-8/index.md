# Level 8: Calculus

*Change and motion · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

Level 7 built the derivative. This level finishes the toolkit and puts it to work. You learn the rules for products,
quotients and functions inside functions, and the derivatives of $e^x$, $\ln x$, $\sin x$ and $\cos x$, each with a
reason. Then you use derivatives to sketch any curve, find the best possible answer to real problems, and link rates
that change together. Finally you meet the second half of calculus, areas and the fundamental theorem, and take a first
step into differential equations, where calculus becomes the language of science.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [The product, quotient and chain rules](01-product-quotient-chain.md) | Prove each rule (the product rule from a growing rectangle) and differentiate mixed functions |
| [Derivatives of $e^x$, $\ln x$, $\sin x$ and $\cos x$](02-exp-log-trig.md) | Explain why $e^x$ is its own derivative, prove $\frac{\sin h}{h} \to 1$, and differentiate exponentials, logarithms and trig functions |
| [Curve sketching](03-curve-sketching.md) | Use $f'$ and $f''$ to find turning points, concavity, inflection points and asymptotes, and sketch cubics and rational functions |
| [Optimization](04-optimization.md) | Find the largest box, the cheapest can and the shortest path, checking critical points and endpoints |
| [Related rates](05-related-rates.md) | Link rates with the chain rule: sliding ladders, growing balloons, filling cones |
| [Areas under curves and the fundamental theorem](06-area-fundamental-theorem.md) | Use Riemann sums, prove the area under $x^2$ from 0 to 1 is exactly $\frac13$, and integrate with antiderivatives |
| [Differential equations: a first look](07-differential-equations.md) | Solve $\frac{dy}{dt} = ky$ and Newton's law of cooling, read slope fields and use Euler's method |
