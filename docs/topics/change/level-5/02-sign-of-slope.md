# Positive, negative, zero and changing rates

*Change and motion · level 5, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

A hot-air balloon is coming down to land. Its height above the field drops by 2 metres every second. Its rate of
change of height is $-2$ metres per second: **minus** two.

Can a speed be negative? Can you go slower than standing still? Something is odd here. A minus sign is telling us
something, but what? This lesson is about reading the **sign** of a rate (plus, minus or zero) as part of a story,
and about what happens when the rate itself keeps changing.

## The idea

### The sign of the rise

In the last lesson, the slope of a line was rise over run. When time (or any other input) goes across from left to
right, the run is always positive: we move forward in time. So the sign of the slope is decided by the **rise**:

- If the up quantity gets **bigger**, the rise is positive, and the slope is **positive**. The line goes up from left
  to right.
- If the up quantity gets **smaller**, the rise is negative (an end value minus a bigger start value), and the slope
  is **negative**. The line goes down.
- If the up quantity **stays the same**, the rise is 0, and the slope is $0 \div \text{run} = 0$. The line is flat.

The bigger the number, ignoring the sign, the steeper the line and the **faster** the change. A slope of $-6$ is
steeper than a slope of $+2$; it is just steep going down.

What about an upright line? Its run is 0, and we can never divide by 0, so its slope has no value at all: we say it is
**undefined**. On a distance-time graph an upright line would mean being at many places at the same instant, or
moving with no time passing. No real object can do that, so a real distance-time graph never has an upright piece.

### What the sign means in a story

The sign always answers the question "is the up quantity growing or shrinking?". What that means depends on the
story.

| Graph | Positive slope | Zero slope | Negative slope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| distance from home against time | walking away from home | standing still | walking back towards home |
| water in a tank against time | filling | neither filling nor emptying | draining |
| bank balance against weeks | saving | no change | spending more than earning |
| temperature against hours | warming | steady | cooling |
| height of a balloon against time | rising | hovering | coming down |

Now the balloon puzzle. The rate of change of **height** is $-2$ m/s. That does not mean the balloon goes "slower
than stopped". It means the height is getting **smaller**, by 2 metres each second. The balloon's speed (how fast,
ignoring direction) is 2 m/s; the minus sign tells the **direction**: down.

Scientists have a word for a rate that keeps the sign: **velocity**. The velocity of the balloon is $-2$ m/s (taking
up as positive). Its **speed** is 2 m/s, which is the size of the velocity without the sign. Speed is never negative;
velocity can be.

sign says "towards home", the size says "fast".

### Changing rates: when the graph curves

A straight line has one slope everywhere: the rate is **constant**. A curve has a slope that changes as you move
along it: the rate is **changing**. There are four basic shapes, and each tells a different story.

To read a curve, ask **two** questions:

1. **Is it going up or down?** That is the sign of the rate.
2. **Is it getting steeper or flatter?** That says whether the change is speeding up or slowing down.

Some examples:

- A snowball rolling downhill, its mass against time: **up, faster and faster** (the bigger it is, the more snow it
  picks up).
- A cup of hot cocoa's temperature: **down, but slowing down**. It cools fast at first, then more and more gently as it
  gets close to room temperature.
- A sprinter's distance in the first two seconds of a race: **up, faster and faster** (she is speeding up).
- A child's height against age, from 18 to 25: **up, but slowing down** until it is almost flat.
- Water in a cone-shaped funnel draining out of the bottom: the level goes **down, faster and faster** near the end,
  because the narrow part holds little water.

### Average and changing: a careful word

On a curve, "the rate" has no single value. If someone says "the cocoa cooled at 3 degrees per minute", they must mean
either an **average** over some time, or the rate at one particular moment. Lesson 3 is about the first; level 6 is
all about the second.

## Worked examples

**1. A bank balance.** Leo has 240 dollars on 1 March and 168 dollars on 1 April (31 days later). Find the rate of
change, and say what it means.

Rate $= \dfrac{168 - 240}{31} = \dfrac{-72}{31} \approx -2.32$ dollars per day. Negative: the balance went **down**,
by about 2.32 dollars a day on average.

**2. Night temperatures.** At 8 p.m. it is 14 °C; at 2 a.m. it is 5 °C. Find the rate of change. If it keeps cooling
at that rate, what is the temperature at 6 a.m.?

1. From 8 p.m. to 2 a.m. is 6 hours. Rate $= (5 - 14) \div 6 = -9 \div 6 = -1.5$ °C per hour.
2. From 2 a.m. to 6 a.m. is 4 hours: change $= -1.5 \times 4 = -6$ °C. Temperature $= 5 - 6 = -1$ °C.

**3. An elevator.** An elevator is at floor height 36 m. It moves for 12 seconds and ends at 6 m. Find its velocity
and its speed.

Velocity $= (6 - 36) \div 12 = -30 \div 12 = -2.5$ m/s (going down). Speed $= 2.5$ m/s.

**4. Maya's whole trip.** Using the graph above, what was Maya's average velocity for the whole 35 minutes, and her
average speed?

- She ends where she started: change in distance from home $= 0 - 0 = 0$. Average velocity $= 0 \div 35 = 0$ m/min.
- But she really walked $800 + 400 + 1200 = 2400$ m. Average speed $= 2400 \div 35 \approx 68.6$ m/min.

The two averages answer different questions: "how far from home did I end up?" against "how much ground did I
cover?". That is why the sign matters.

**5. Which shape?** A bath is being filled, but the tap is slowly turned down. Sketch water against time.

The water keeps going **up** (positive rate), but each minute adds less than the minute before. So the curve is
**up, but slowing down**: the second shape in the picture.

## Try it

1. Give the sign (positive, negative or zero) of the rate in each story: (a) a candle's height while it burns; (b) a
   parked car's distance from home; (c) the number of people in a stadium as fans arrive.
2. A diver's depth below the surface goes from 3 m to 18 m in 10 s. Using "depth below the surface" as the up
   quantity, what is the rate? Using "height above the surface" (so the diver is at $-3$ m, then $-18$ m), what is
   it?
3. A tank holds 500 L and drains at a steady 25 L/min. Write the rate with the right sign. When is it empty?
4. In Maya's graph, which part had the gentlest non-zero slope? What was her speed in it, in km/h?
5. Which of these is the steepest: slope $+3$, slope $-5$, slope $0.5$, slope $-1$?
6. A ball is thrown straight up and caught at the same height. Describe its height-against-time graph using the four
   curve shapes. Where is the rate zero?
7. Draw (or describe) a graph of the water level in a bathtub for this story: the tap runs steadily, then someone
   gets in, then the tap is turned off, then the plug is pulled and the water drains, slowly at the end.
8. A town's population went from 12 400 to 11 650 in 5 years. Find the average rate of change. What would the
   population be after 3 more years at the same rate?

    1. (a) negative (it gets shorter); (b) zero (no change); (c) positive.
    2. Depth: $(18 - 3) \div 10 = +1.5$ m/s (depth growing). Height: $(-18 - (-3)) \div 10 = -15 \div 10 = -1.5$ m/s.
       Same motion, opposite sign: the sign depends on which way you call "up". Always say what you measure.
    3. Rate $= -25$ litres per minute. Empty after $500 \div 25 = 20$ minutes.
    4. Part C, $+40$ m/min. In an hour that is $40 \times 60 = 2400$ m, so **2.4 km/h**.
    5. Slope $-5$: its size, 5, is the biggest. It is steep going down.
    6. Going up the curve is "up, but slowing down" (the ball slows as it rises); at the top it is flat for an

    7. Steady rise (straight line up); a sudden jump up when the person gets in (the level rises at once though no

## Common mistake

**Thinking negative means slow.** A slope of $-120$ is fast: it is the **size** that tells how fast, and the **sign**
that tells which way. A slope near 0, like $+0.5$ or $-0.5$, is the slow one.

**Mixing up "going down" with "slowing down".** A graph going **down** has a negative rate. A graph getting
**flatter** has a rate getting closer to zero, which means slowing down. A curve can go up while slowing down (a plant
near its full height), or go down while speeding up (a stone falling, measured as height).
