# Level 7: Limits and the derivative

*Change and motion · level 7 of 8 · rough age 15-16 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 6 you closed in on the speed at an instant by shrinking time intervals. This level turns that idea into the
real tools of calculus. You learn what a limit is and when a function is continuous, define the derivative as a
limit and work it out from first principles, discover the power rule that makes it quick, read the derivative off a
graph, and use it to describe motion: position, velocity and acceleration.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Limits](01-limits.md) | Find limits from tables, pictures and algebra, use one-sided limits and the limit laws, and spot limits that do not exist |
| [Continuity](02-continuity.md) | Say exactly what continuous means, classify holes, jumps and asymptotes, and use the intermediate value theorem to find roots |
| [The derivative from first principles](03-first-principles.md) | Use $f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}$ to differentiate $x^2$, $x^3$, $\frac1x$ and $\sqrt{x}$, and see where derivatives fail |
| [The power rule](04-power-rule.md) | Prove the power rule, differentiate any polynomial and power, and find tangent and normal lines |
| [The derivative as a function](05-derivative-function.md) | Sketch $f'$ from the graph of $f$ and back again, and match a function with its derivatives |
| [Velocity and acceleration](06-velocity-acceleration.md) | Use $s$, $s'$ and $s''$ to analyse a throw on Earth and on Mars, braking cars and particles that turn round |
