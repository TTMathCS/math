# Level 8: Secret codes and open questions

*Number theory · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

The last level puts the whole topic to work. You will see why old letter-swapping codes break, and how RSA and
Diffie-Hellman use Euler's theorem and fast powers to keep messages secret even when the method is public. Then the
level turns from what is known to what is not: how the primes are spread out, which famous questions are still open
(and how Fermat's last theorem was finally settled), and which numbers are squares on a prime clock.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Old codes and why they break](01-old-codes.md) | Use Caesar and affine ciphers as arithmetic mod 26, and break them by brute force and frequency analysis |
| [RSA: a lock anyone can close](02-rsa.md) | Make an RSA key, encrypt and decrypt with it, and explain why it works (Euler) and why it is safe (factoring) |
| [Diffie-Hellman key exchange](03-diffie-hellman.md) | Agree on a shared secret in public with powers mod $p$, and explain the discrete logarithm problem |
| [How the primes are spread out](04-spread-of-primes.md) | Use the prime counting function and the prime number theorem, and build prime gaps as long as you like |
| [Famous open problems](05-open-problems.md) | State the twin prime and Goldbach conjectures and the Riemann hypothesis, and prove Fermat's last theorem for $n = 4$ |
| [Quadratic residues](06-quadratic-residues.md) | Find the squares mod $p$, prove there are $(p-1)/2$, use Euler's criterion, and show when $-1$ is a square |
