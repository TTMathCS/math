# Level 3: Counting with care

*Counting and combinations · level 3 of 8 · rough age 9-10 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 2 you learned to count by multiplying along a tree. In this level you learn to count **carefully**: to notice
when a choice cannot be repeated, to deal with the awkward part of a question first, and to count the things you do
**not** want when that is easier. You will count every square on a chessboard, find out when turning a table makes two
seatings "the same", and see that counting subsets and counting factors are the same idea.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Repeats allowed or not](01-repeats-or-not.md) | Count codes and words with and without repeats, and explain why each slot then has one choice fewer |
| [Fussy parts first](02-fussy-parts-first.md) | Count numbers with rules (even, no 0 in front, different digits) by filling the fussiest slot first and splitting into cases |
| [Counting what you do not want](03-counting-the-rest.md) | Count "at least one" by taking the complement away from the total, and explain why simple adding over-counts |
| [Squares on a chessboard](04-chessboard-squares.md) | Count squares of every size (204 on a chessboard) and rectangles by choosing lines (1296) |
| [Counting by symmetry](05-counting-by-symmetry.md) | Count seatings at a round table and necklaces, and know when dividing by the number of turns works |
| [Factors and subsets](06-factors-and-subsets.md) | Count subsets with "in or out" ($2^n$) and count the factors of a number from its primes |
