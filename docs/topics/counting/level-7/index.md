# Level 7: The binomial theorem

*Counting and combinations · level 7 of 8 · rough age 15-16 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 5 the numbers $\dbinom{n}{k}$ counted teams and filled Pascal's triangle. In this level they turn up somewhere
unexpected: in algebra, as the numbers you get when you multiply out $(a + b)^n$. That one link, the binomial theorem,
turns counting facts into algebra facts and algebra facts back into counting facts. Along the way you meet your first
real **proofs by counting**, where an identity is proved by counting one set in two different ways.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Expanding by choosing](01-expanding-by-choosing.md) | Multiply out any product of brackets by choosing one term from each, and explain why the coefficients of $(a + b)^n$ are choose numbers |
| [The binomial theorem](02-binomial-theorem.md) | State the theorem with $\Sigma$ notation, prove it, and find any single term of an expansion such as the $x^5$ term of $(2x - 3)^8$ |
| [Identities from the theorem](03-identities-from-theorem.md) | Get row sums, alternating sums and other identities by putting numbers in for $a$ and $b$, and explain each one by counting too |
| [Patterns in Pascal's triangle](04-pascal-patterns.md) | Prove the hockey-stick identity, find the triangular and Fibonacci numbers in the triangle, and explain the Sierpinski picture of its odd entries |
| [Proof by counting](05-double-counting.md) | Prove identities such as $k\dbinom{n}{k} = n\dbinom{n-1}{k-1}$ and Vandermonde's identity by counting one set in two ways |
| [Multinomial coefficients](06-multinomial-coefficients.md) | Count arrangements of words like AABBBC, expand $(a + b + c)^n$, and find any coefficient as $\dfrac{n!}{p!\,q!\,r!}$ |
