# Level 5: Choosing, combinations

*Counting and combinations · level 5 of 8 · rough age 12-13 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 4 you arranged things in order. In this level the order stops mattering: you choose teams, pizzas, routes
and card hands, where only **who is in** counts. One idea, dividing by the orders that look the same, gives the
"choose" numbers $\dbinom{n}{r}$, and they turn out to fill Pascal's triangle. The level ends with committees that come
with conditions and a full count of every poker hand.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Choosing, not arranging](01-choosing-not-arranging.md) | Tell a combination from a permutation, and count combinations with $\binom{n}{r} = {}^nP_r / r!$, explaining why the division is exact |
| [Choosing who is in is choosing who is out](02-in-or-out.md) | Use $\binom{n}{r} = \binom{n}{n-r}$ to save work, find the biggest choose number, and split people into named or unnamed groups |
| [Pascal's triangle](03-pascals-triangle.md) | Build the triangle, prove Pascal's rule by asking "is this person in or out?", and explain why row $n$ adds to $2^n$ |
| [Paths on a grid](04-grid-paths.md) | Count shortest routes on a grid by choosing which steps go north, including routes through or around a point or a closed street |
| [Committees with conditions](05-committees-conditions.md) | Count groups with "exactly", "at least", special roles and people who refuse to work together, and avoid the overcounting trap |
| [Cards and hands](06-cards-hands.md) | Count 5-card hands, four of a kind, full houses and every other poker hand, and check that they add up to $\binom{52}{5}$ |
