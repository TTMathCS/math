# Level 1: How many ways?

*Counting and combinations · level 1 of 8 · rough age 5-6 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

Counting one, two, three is easy. Counting **ways** is a puzzle: how many outfits, how many line-ups, how many
paths? In this level you count them with your hands, with cut-out pictures and with tidy lists, and for every answer
you learn how to be **sure** that nothing is missing and nothing is counted twice. You also meet the question that
runs through this whole topic: does the **order** matter?

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Dressing a teddy](01-dressing-teddy.md) | Lay out every outfit in a box of rows and columns, and explain why 2 shirts and 3 pants make 6 outfits, not 5 |
| [Ice-cream towers](02-ice-cream-towers.md) | Tell when the order matters (towers) and when it does not (cups), and see why towers are double the cups |
| [Lining up](03-lining-up.md) | Find all 6 line-ups of 3 friends by keeping the front person still, and use that to get 24 for 4 friends |
| [Handshakes](04-handshakes.md) | Count handshakes with dots and lines, explain why "each shakes 3, so 12" counts everything twice |
| [Paths to the playground](05-playground-paths.md) | Trace every right-and-up path on a small grid, and count paths fast with the corner trick |
| [Being organised](06-being-organised.md) | Make a tidy list in a fixed order, use it to find a missing or repeated item, and check a count two ways |
