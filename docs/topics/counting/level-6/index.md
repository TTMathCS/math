# Level 6: Counting with overlaps and stars

*Counting and combinations · level 6 of 8 · rough age 13-14 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

Level 5 gave you the choose numbers. This level gives you the tools that real counting problems need on top of them:
fixing double counting when groups overlap, sharing out identical things with stars and bars, deciding which rule a
problem needs, guaranteeing coincidences with the pigeonhole principle, and matching a hard collection with an easy
one. It ends by using counting to measure chances, from lottery tickets to passwords.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Inclusion-exclusion](01-inclusion-exclusion.md) | Count the union of two or three overlapping sets, prove why the three-set rule counts everything once, and count things in none of the sets |
| [Stars and bars](02-stars-and-bars.md) | Share identical things among people, count whole-number solutions of $a + b + c = n$, with minimums and maximums |
| [Mixed problems: which rule?](03-mixed-problems.md) | Decide whether order matters, repeats are allowed and objects are alike, and pick the right one of the four basic counts |
| [The generalised pigeonhole principle](04-generalised-pigeonhole.md) | Use $\lceil n/k \rceil$ to guarantee coincidences, answer "how many to be sure", and prove the party problem |
| [Counting by a bijection](05-counting-bijection.md) | Count by pairing one collection perfectly with another: subsets and strings, sums and gaps, crossings and sets of four points |
| [Counting outcomes for probability](06-counting-for-probability.md) | Find chances by counting equally likely outcomes, analyse a lottery fully, and explain why long random passwords are strong |
