# Level 3: Averages

*Statistics and data · level 3 of 8 · rough age 9-10 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 2 you counted answers, made tallies and drew bar graphs. In this level you collect your own data with a plan,
and then you learn to sum up a whole list of numbers with **one** number: the mean, the median or the mode. You find
out **why** each one works, and why one number is never the whole story, so you also measure how spread out the data
are. The level ends with two new graphs: one for comparing two groups, and one for things that change over time.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Collecting your own data](01-collecting-data.md) | Tell primary data from secondary data, and write a data plan before you start |
| [The mean](02-the-mean.md) | Find the mean by levelling towers or by adding and dividing, and explain why it need not be one of the values |
| [The median and the mode](03-median-and-mode.md) | Find the middle value of odd and even lists, and the most common value, even when there are two or none |
| [The range](04-the-range.md) | Measure how spread out data are, and explain why two groups with the same mean can be very different |
| [Stem-and-leaf plots](05-stem-and-leaf.md) | Build a stem-and-leaf plot, read the median from it, and compare two groups back to back |
| [Double bar graphs and broken-line graphs](06-double-bar-line.md) | Compare two groups with a double bar graph, show change over time with a line graph, and know when to join the points |
