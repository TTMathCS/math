# Level 7: Distributions and regression

*Statistics and data · level 7 of 8 · rough age 15-16 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 6 you measured spread with the standard deviation and drew lines through scatter plots by eye. In this level
those ideas become exact tools. You read the shape of a distribution, model data with the normal curve, compare
anything with z-scores, measure a straight-line pattern with the correlation coefficient, and find **the** best line by
least squares, with a proof of why it is best. The level ends with curved models and with the practical work of
organising and cleaning large data sets.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Shapes of distributions](01-distribution-shapes.md) | Describe a distribution by shape, outliers, centre and spread, and explain why a tail pulls the mean but not the median |
| [The normal curve as a model of data](02-normal-curve.md) | Use the 68–95–99.7 rule, explain why the bell curve appears so often, and check whether data are close to normal |
| [z-scores and percentiles](03-z-scores-percentiles.md) | Standardise values, compare results on different scales, and move between values, z-scores and percentiles |
| [The correlation coefficient r](04-correlation-coefficient.md) | Compute r by hand, prove it lies between −1 and 1, and explain why it sees only straight-line patterns |
| [Least-squares regression](05-least-squares.md) | Derive and use the least-squares line, interpret residuals, and read r² as the share of variation explained |
| [Non-linear models](06-non-linear-models.md) | Fit exponential, quadratic and power models with technology, and choose between models with a residual plot |
| [Organising large data sets](07-large-data-sets.md) | Store data as cases and variables, name the kind of each variable, and clean messy data with a log |
