# Level 8: Reading data honestly

*Statistics and data · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 7 you described and modelled data. In this level you learn how far a sample lets you see beyond itself, and
how to tell an honest claim from a misleading one. You find out why averages of samples are steadier than single
values, decode "accurate within 3 points, 19 times out of 20", run a hypothesis test and read a p-value correctly,
see why only experiments show cause, untangle Simpson's paradox and question the numbers in the news. The topic ends
with a complete investigation from question to conclusion.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Sampling distributions](01-sampling-distributions.md) | Describe how sample means and proportions vary, and explain the σ/√n law and the central limit theorem |
| [Margin of error and confidence intervals](02-confidence-intervals.md) | Compute a margin of error and a 95% interval, and say exactly what the 95% does and does not mean |
| [Hypothesis tests](03-hypothesis-tests.md) | Set up null and alternative hypotheses, find and interpret a p-value, and explain why 0.05 is a convention |
| [Experiments versus observational studies](04-experiments-studies.md) | Design a randomised, controlled, blinded experiment and explain why only random assignment shows cause |
| [Simpson's paradox](05-simpsons-paradox.md) | Explain how a comparison can reverse when groups are combined, using weighted averages, and decide which comparison to trust |
| [Statistics in the news](06-statistics-news.md) | Turn relative risks into absolute ones, and spot percentage-point confusion, cherry-picked ranges and survivorship bias |
| [A full investigation](07-full-investigation.md) | Carry out the whole cycle: question, plan, data, analysis and a conclusion that states its limits |
