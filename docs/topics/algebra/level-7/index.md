# Level 7: Quadratics and functions

*Algebra · level 7 of 8 · rough age 15-16 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 6 you multiplied and factored polynomials and solved pairs of straight-line equations. In this level the
straight line bends. You meet the parabola, the curve of every thrown ball, learn its three forms, and solve any
quadratic equation, finishing with a formula you derive yourself. Then the idea of a **function** takes centre stage:
what it is, how to move and stretch its graph, how to undo it, and the first function whose unknown lives in the
exponent.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Quadratic relations and the parabola](01-quadratic-relations.md) | Read the vertex, axis of symmetry and zeros from the standard, factored and vertex forms, and explain why the axis lies halfway between the zeros |
| [Completing the square](02-completing-the-square.md) | Turn $ax^2 + bx + c$ into vertex form with a square picture, and find the greatest height of a ball or the largest area of a fence |
| [Solving quadratic equations](03-solving-quadratics.md) | Solve by factoring, by completing the square and by the quadratic formula, derive the formula, and count the roots with the discriminant |
| [Functions](04-functions.md) | Use $f(x)$ notation, find domain and range, and decide with mapping diagrams and the vertical line test whether a relation is a function |
| [Transformations of functions](05-transformations.md) | Sketch $y = af(k(x - d)) + c$ from $x^2$, $\sqrt{x}$, $1/x$ and $\lvert x \rvert$, in the right order, with a mapping rule |
| [Inverse functions](06-inverse-functions.md) | Find an inverse by swapping $x$ and $y$, draw it as a reflection in $y = x$, and restrict a domain so that an inverse exists |
| [Exponential functions](07-exponential-functions.md) | Evaluate rational exponents like $8^{2/3}$, graph $y = a \cdot b^x$ with its asymptote, model growth and decay, and solve $2^x = 32$ |
