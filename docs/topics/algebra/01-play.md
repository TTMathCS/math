# Play: Mystery numbers and balance scales

*Algebra · step 1 of 5*

## A question to start

"I'm thinking of a number. I add 4 to it, and I get 11. What is my number?"

You probably said **7** quite quickly. But how did you know? You did not try every number. You thought something
like: *the number was 4 less than 11*, and 11 take away 4 is 7. You **worked backwards**. That one move, undoing what
was done to a mystery number, is the heart of a whole branch of mathematics called **algebra**.

## The idea

### Mystery numbers

A **mystery number** puzzle tells you what happened to a hidden number and what came out at the end. Your job is to
find what went in. There are two good ways to do it.

### Way 1: work backwards

Every action has an **opposite** that undoes it.

| If the puzzle says ... | ... undo it by |
| --- | --- |
| add 4 | taking away 4 |
| take away 3 | adding 3 |
| double it (times 2) | halving it (divide by 2) |
| divide by 5 | multiplying by 5 |

To find the mystery number, start at the **end** and undo each action, going in the **opposite order**. It is like
taking off your shoes and socks: you put your socks on first, but you take your shoes off first.

### Way 2: keep the scale balanced

Picture a see-saw scale. On the left pan is a closed bag with a mystery number of marbles inside, and 3 loose
marbles. On the right pan are 7 loose marbles. The scale is **balanced**: both sides weigh the same.

How many marbles are in the bag? Here is the golden rule of the balance:

> **Whatever you do to one side, do the same to the other side, and the scale stays balanced.**

Take 3 marbles off the left. To keep it balanced, take 3 marbles off the right too. Now the bag sits alone on the
left, and 4 marbles sit on the right. The scale still balances, so the bag holds **4 marbles**.

### Always check

When you think you have found the mystery number, put it back into the puzzle and see if it works. The bag holds 4:
4 marbles and 3 more make 7. Yes! Checking takes a few seconds and catches most mistakes.

## Worked example

**The puzzle:** "I think of a number. I double it, then add 1, and I get 15. What is my number?"

1. **Write the steps in order.** Start with the mystery number, then *double it*, then *add 1*, and the result is 15.
2. **Undo them in the opposite order.** The last thing done was *add 1*, so undo it first: 15 take away 1 is 14.
3. **Undo the next one.** Before that the number was *doubled*, so halve: half of 14 is 7.
4. **Answer.** The mystery number is **7**.
5. **Check.** Double 7 is 14, and add 1 is 15. It works.

**The same puzzle on a balance.** On the left: two identical bags (the number doubled) and 1 marble. On the right:
15 marbles. Take 1 marble off each side: two bags balance 14 marbles. Two equal bags balance 14, so share the 14
into two equal piles: each bag balances 7. Same answer, a different way to see it.

### A mind-reading trick

Try this on a friend. "Think of a number. Double it. Add 10. Halve the answer. Now take away the number you first
thought of." Then say, very slowly, as if reading their mind: "Your answer is ... **5**."

It always works, whatever number they choose. Why? Imagine their number as a bag. Doubling gives two bags. Adding 10
gives two bags and 10 marbles. Halving gives one bag and 5 marbles. Taking away the number they thought of takes
away the bag. Five marbles are left, every time. In the next step you will write this trick in a short, neat way.

## Try it

1. I think of a number and take away 6. I get 9. What is my number?
2. I think of a number and multiply it by 4. I get 28. What is my number?
3. I think of a number, double it, then take away 3, and I get 11. What is my number?
4. A balanced scale has three equal bags on one side and 12 marbles on the other. How many marbles are in each bag?
5. A balanced scale has one bag and 5 marbles on the left, and 2 marbles and 9 marbles on the right. How many marbles
   are in the bag?
6. Change the mind-reading trick so that the answer is always 8. (Hint: what should you add?)

??? note "Answers"
    1. 15. Undo "take away 6" by adding 6: 9 and 6 make 15. Check: 15 take away 6 is 9.
    2. 7. Undo "times 4" by dividing by 4: 28 shared into 4 is 7. Check: 7 times 4 is 28.
    3. 7. Undo "take away 3" first: 11 and 3 make 14. Then undo "double": half of 14 is 7. Check: double 7 is 14, take
       away 3 is 11.
    4. 4 in each bag. Three equal bags balance 12, so share 12 into three equal piles: 4 each.
    5. 6. The right side holds 2 and 9, which is 11 marbles. Take 5 off both sides: the bag balances 6. Check: 6 and 5
       make 11.
    6. Add 16 instead of 10. Two bags and 16 marbles, halved, is one bag and 8 marbles; take away the bag and 8 are
       left. (Whatever you add, the answer is half of it.)

## Common mistake

**Undoing in the wrong order.** In "double it, then add 1, and get 15", some people halve first: half of 15 is 7 and
a half, take away 1 is 6 and a half. Check it: double 6 and a half is 13, add 1 is 14, not 15. Wrong! The last thing
done must be the **first** thing undone, just like shoes before socks.
