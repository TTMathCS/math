# The equal sign as a balance

*Algebra · level 2, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

What number goes in the box?

**8 + 4 = ? + 5**

Many children quickly write **12**. They see 8 + 4, they know it makes 12, and they write 12 straight after the equal
sign. It feels right. But it is **wrong**. In this lesson you will see why, find the right answer two different ways,
and learn a trick that finds it without adding up at all.

## The idea

### What the sentence really says

Remember from level 1: the equal sign means **"is the same amount as"**. It is a picture of a level balance scale.
Whatever is on the left side must weigh the same as whatever is on the right side.

So **8 + 4 = ? + 5** says:

> "8 and 4 is the same amount as **some number** and 5."

The box is not "the answer to 8 + 4". The box is a number that sits **with** the 5 on the right side. The whole right
side, box and 5 together, has to match the whole left side.

### Why 12 is wrong

Put 12 in the box and check both sides.

- Left side: 8 + 4 = **12**.
- Right side: 12 + 5 = **17**.

12 is not the same as 17. The right side is too heavy, and the scale tips. Here are the two sides as bars: the right
bar is longer than the left bar.

So 12 makes the sentence **false**. The mistake comes from reading = as "the answer is". When there is something
after the box, like **+ 5**, the box cannot be the answer to the left side.

### Way 1: work out the side you can

The left side has no box, so we can work it out: 8 + 4 = 12. Now the sentence says

**12 = ? + 5**

This is a missing-number puzzle from [lesson 1](01-bigger-missing-numbers.md). The whole is 12, one part is 5, and the
box is the other part. 12 − 5 = **7**.

Check: left side 8 + 4 = 12. Right side 7 + 5 = 12. Both 12 ✓. The scale is level.

### Way 2: compare, without adding up

Look at the bars again. Both sides have two parts. Compare them part by part.

- The second parts: 4 on the left and **5** on the right. The right one is **1 more**.
- So, to keep the sides the same, the first part on the right must be **1 less** than 8. One less than 8 is **7**.

**Why does this work?** Imagine two towers of blocks. The left tower is 8 blue blocks and 4 orange blocks. To build
the right tower, take the left tower and move **one** block from the blue part to the orange part. The tower is just
as tall (no block was added or taken away), but now the parts are 7 and 5. So **8 + 4 = 7 + 5**.

This idea is called **keeping the balance**: if one part goes **up** by some amount, the other part must go **down**
by the same amount, and the total stays the same. It is very useful, because you do not need to work out the totals
at all. It also works with big numbers that would be hard to add, like **38 + 25 = 40 + ?**: 40 is 2 more than 38, so
the box is 2 less than 25, which is **23**.

### The box can be on either side

The box can be on the left: **? + 6 = 9 + 3**. Way 1: the right side is 12, so ? + 6 = 12 and the box is **6**. Way 2:
6 is 3 more than 3, so the box is 3 less than 9: **6**. Same answer. Check: 6 + 6 = 12 and 9 + 3 = 12 ✓.

### More than two numbers

Sometimes one side has three numbers: **5 + 5 + 2 = 6 + ?**. The left side is 10 + 2 = 12. So 6 + ? = 12 and the box
is **6**. Check: 6 + 6 = 12 ✓.

### Taking away on both sides

The equal sign works the same way with take-away. **10 − 4 = 9 − ?**. The left side is 6. So 9 − ? must also be 6.
9 take away what leaves 6? Count down from 9 to 6: three steps. The box is **3**. Check: 9 − 3 = 6 ✓. (In
[lesson 5](05-true-or-false.md) you will learn a no-adding trick for take-away too, and find out that it is a little
different from the adding trick.)

## Worked examples

**1. The starting puzzle.** **8 + 4 = ? + 5**.

- Way 1: left side 12. So 12 = ? + 5 and the box is 12 − 5 = **7**.
- Way 2: 5 is 1 more than 4, so the box is 1 less than 8: **7**.
- Check: 8 + 4 = 12 and 7 + 5 = 12 ✓.

**2.** **9 + 6 = 10 + ?**. Way 2 is quick: 10 is 1 more than 9, so the box is 1 less than 6: **5**. Check: 9 + 6 = 15
and 10 + 5 = 15 ✓. This is how many people add 9 + 6 in their heads: make the 9 into a 10, and take 1 from the 6.

**3.** **7 + 7 = 5 + ?**. 5 is 2 less than 7, so the box is 2 more than 7: **9**. Check: 7 + 7 = 14 and 5 + 9 = 14 ✓.

**4.** **? + 20 = 15 + 25**. Way 1: the right side is 40, so ? + 20 = 40 and the box is **20**. Way 2: 20 is 5 less than
25, so the box is 5 more than 15: **20**. Check: 20 + 20 = 40 and 15 + 25 = 40 ✓.

**5. Spotting the trap.** **6 + 3 = ? + 1**. Someone writes 9. Check: 9 + 1 = 10, not 9. Wrong! The right answer:
1 is 2 less than 3, so the box is 2 more than 6: **8**. Check: 8 + 1 = 9 ✓.

## Try it

1. **5 + 4 = ? + 3**
2. **10 + 2 = 6 + ?**
3. **? + 8 = 7 + 9**
4. **9 + 9 = 10 + ?**
5. **3 + 4 + 5 = ? + 2**
6. **12 − 2 = 15 − ?**
7. **48 + 17 = 50 + ?** (try Way 2!)
8. Mia writes **6 + 7 = 13 + 2**, because "6 + 7 is 13, and then I added 2". Is her sentence true? How should she
   write what she did?

??? note "Answers"
    1. **6**. Left side 9, so ? + 3 = 9 and the box is 6. Or: 3 is 1 less than 4, so the box is 1 more than 5. Check:
       6 + 3 = 9 ✓.
    2. **6**. Left side 12, so 6 + ? = 12 and the box is 6 (a double). Check: 6 + 6 = 12 ✓.
    3. **8**. Right side 16. So ? + 8 = 16 and the box is 8 (a double). Or: 8 is 1 less than 9, so the box is 1 more
       than 7. Check: 8 + 8 = 16 and 7 + 9 = 16 ✓.
    4. **8**. 10 is 1 more than 9, so the box is 1 less than 9. Check: 9 + 9 = 18 and 10 + 8 = 18 ✓.
    5. **10**. Left side 3 + 4 + 5 = 12. So ? + 2 = 12 and the box is 10. Check: 10 + 2 = 12 ✓.
    6. **5**. Left side 10. So 15 − ? = 10, and 15 take away 5 leaves 10. Check: 15 − 5 = 10 ✓.
    7. **15**. 50 is 2 more than 48, so the box is 2 less than 17. Check: 48 + 17 = 65 and 50 + 15 = 65 ✓.
    8. **False.** The left side is 13 and the right side is 15. They are not the same. She used = to mean "and then".
       She should write two sentences: **6 + 7 = 13**, then **13 + 2 = 15**. Or one true sentence:
       **6 + 7 + 2 = 15**.

## Common mistake

**Writing the answer to the left side in the box.** In **8 + 4 = ? + 5**, writing 12 ignores the "+ 5". The box must
make the **two sides** match. Check every answer by working out both sides.

**Moving the wrong way in Way 2.** If one part goes **up**, the other part must go **down**. In
**9 + 6 = 10 + ?**, the 9 went up to 10, so the 6 must go down to 5, not up to 7. A quick check with the totals will
show you if you moved the wrong way.
