# Level 8: The limits of logic

*Logic and puzzles · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 7 you learned how proofs work. In this level you turn logic on itself: what exactly can be said in symbols,
what we must assume before we prove anything, and what proof and computation can **never** do. You meet the
discoveries of the early twentieth century that changed mathematics: Russell's paradox, Gödel's incompleteness
theorems and Turing's halting problem. The level ends with the most famous open question in computer science.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Predicate logic](01-predicate-logic.md) | Translate statements about objects into symbols with $\forall$ and $\exists$, and negate complicated statements step by step |
| [Axioms](02-axioms.md) | Explain Euclid's postulates, why changing the parallel postulate gives new geometries, and how the Peano axioms build the whole numbers |
| [Russell's paradox and sets](03-russells-paradox.md) | Follow Russell's paradox, explain why "the set of all sets" causes trouble, and say how careful axioms avoid it |

| [Turing and the halting problem](05-halting-problem.md) | Explain in plain words why no program can decide for every program whether it stops |
| [P versus NP](06-p-versus-np.md) | Tell checking an answer from finding one, recognise problems in NP, and explain what the open question asks |
