# Level 3: And, or, not, if

*Logic and puzzles · level 3 of 8 · rough age 9-10 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 2 you sorted things with rules and solved puzzles by ticking and crossing. In this level you look closely at
the four small words that hold every argument together: **not**, **and**, **or** and **if**. You use them to catch
liars on an island, to write short computer programs with loops, to solve real 9-by-9 Sudoku, and to find a fake coin
with only two weighings. Every lesson asks the same question: how can we be **sure**?

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Not, and, or](01-not-and-or.md) | Decide when an *and* or an *or* sentence is true, and write the opposite of a sentence correctly |
| [If ... then](02-if-then.md) | Say when an *if ... then* sentence is broken, and explain why "the ground is wet" does not prove it rained |
| [Knights and knaves](03-knights-and-knaves.md) | Solve truth-teller and liar puzzles by trying both cases and crossing off the impossible one |
| [Coding with loops](04-coding-with-loops.md) | Use *repeat* to draw shapes with short code, find the pattern that shortens code, and put a loop inside a loop |
| [9-by-9 Sudoku moves](05-sudoku-moves.md) | Use the "only place" and "only number" moves, and explain why a good puzzle has exactly one answer |
| [Weighing puzzles](06-weighing-puzzles.md) | Find one heavy coin among 9 with two weighings, and explain why splitting into threes is the secret |
