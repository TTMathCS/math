# Level 4: Endless sums and fractals

*Infinity and strange math · level 4 of 8 · rough age 11-12 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 3 you met Zeno's race, where infinitely many stages fitted into a few seconds. In this level you learn to
**add up infinitely many numbers**: some endless sums settle on an exact total (like
$\frac12 + \frac14 + \frac18 + \cdots = 1$, or $0.999\ldots = 1$), while others grow past every number, even though
their pieces shrink. Then you
build shapes by repeating a step forever, the **fractals**: a triangle with no area left, and a snowflake with an
endless edge around a finite area. The level ends back at Hilbert's hotel, with infinitely many buses.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Halves that add up to 1](01-halves-add-to-one.md) | Prove with a picture and with partial sums that $\frac12 + \frac14 + \frac18 + \cdots = 1$, and add other endless sums the same way |
| [Does 0.999... equal 1?](02-does-point-nine.md) | Give three different arguments that $0.999\ldots = 1$, and explain why it feels wrong |
| [Sums that blow up](03-sums-blow-up.md) | Prove that $1 + \frac12 + \frac13 + \frac14 + \cdots$ grows without limit, and use it to stack books out past a table edge |
| [The Sierpinski triangle](04-sierpinski-triangle.md) | Count the triangles at each step, and show that the area left shrinks to zero |
| [The Koch snowflake](05-koch-snowflake.md) | Show that the snowflake's edge grows without limit while its area settles at $\frac85$ of the first triangle |
| [Hilbert's hotel with infinitely many buses](06-infinitely-many-buses.md) | Give every passenger of infinitely many buses a room using powers of primes, and see why that makes a set countable |
