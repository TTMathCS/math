# Level 8: At the edge of mathematics

*Infinity and strange math · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

This last level takes infinity to the places where modern mathematics was built and where it still meets its limits.
You will tame "as close as you like" with the exact $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition of a limit, test infinite sums,
meet a horn you can fill but never paint, and learn why one simple question about infinity can never be answered from
the usual axioms. Then you will count past infinity with ordinals, see infinitely small numbers made rigorous, and
finish with infinity as a point you can draw.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [The formal definition of a limit](01-limit-definition.md) | Use $\varepsilon$ and $N$ for sequences and $\varepsilon$ and $\delta$ for functions, and write proofs such as $\lim_{x \to 3}(2x + 1) = 7$ |
| [Convergence tests](02-convergence-tests.md) | Decide whether a series converges with the divergence, comparison, ratio and alternating series tests, and prove that $\sum 1/n^2$ converges |
| [Gabriel's horn](03-gabriels-horn.md) | Show that the horn has finite volume $\pi$ but infinite surface area, and resolve the painter's paradox |
| [The continuum hypothesis](04-continuum-hypothesis.md) | State the continuum hypothesis, explain what independence from the axioms means, and describe what Gödel and Cohen proved |
| [Ordinals and cardinals](05-ordinals-cardinals.md) | Count past infinity with $\omega$, $\omega + 1$ and $\omega \cdot 2$, tell ordinals from cardinals, and run a Goodstein sequence |
| [Infinitely small](06-infinitely-small.md) | Explain Leibniz's infinitesimals, Berkeley's objection, how limits replaced them and how Robinson's hyperreals made them rigorous |
| [The point at infinity](07-point-at-infinity.md) | Explain vanishing points, use homogeneous coordinates, and find where parallel lines and conics meet the line at infinity |
