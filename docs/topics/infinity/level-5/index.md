# Level 5: Limits

*Infinity and strange math · level 5 of 8 · rough age 12-13 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 4 you added up endless sums and drew shapes that never stop growing. This level asks what "endless" really
means, and answers with the idea of a **limit**: the number that a never-ending list closes in on. With it you can
settle Zeno's race to the exact second, prove that $0.333\ldots$ is exactly $\frac13$, see why $\infty$ is not a
number and why we never divide by zero, and spot the traps in some famous paradoxes.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Sequences that settle down](01-settling-sequences.md) | Explain what a limit is with the closeness game, find limits of simple sequences, and show that some sequences have none |
| [Zeno answered](02-zeno-answered.md) | Add up Zeno's infinitely many stages to find exactly when Achilles passes the tortoise, and say where Zeno's argument goes wrong |
| [Infinity is not a number](03-infinity-not-a-number.md) | Show why $\infty - \infty$ and $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ have no single answer, and why $6 \div 0$ has no answer and $0 \div 0$ has every answer |
| [Approaching asymptotes](04-approaching-asymptotes.md) | Find the horizontal and vertical asymptotes of curves like $y = \frac1x$, and show that a curve can cross its asymptote |
| [Repeating decimals and limits](05-repeating-decimals.md) | Explain why an endless decimal means a limit, prove $0.999\ldots = 1$, and turn any repeating decimal into a fraction |
| [Paradoxes of the infinite](06-paradoxes.md) | Analyse Thomson's lamp, the Ross-Littlewood urn and the staircase, and say exactly why each answer depends on the setup |
