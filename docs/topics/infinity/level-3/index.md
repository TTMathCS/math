# Level 3: Hilbert's hotel

*Infinity and strange math · level 3 of 8 · rough age 9-10 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 2 you matched groups without counting them, and met the puzzle of whether there are "more" counting numbers
than even numbers. In this level you check into a hotel with infinitely many rooms, where "full" does not mean "no
room", and you learn why: an infinite collection can be paired with a part of itself. Then you look **between**
numbers and find there is always another one, follow decimals that never stop, and meet Zeno's 2,500-year-old puzzle
that seems to prove a runner can never catch a tortoise.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [A full hotel with room for one more](01-room-for-one.md) | Find room for new guests in a full infinite hotel with a "move from room n to room n + 1" rule, and explain why it fails in an ordinary hotel |
| [A bus of infinitely many guests](02-bus-of-guests.md) | Use the "move to room 2n" rule to free all the odd rooms, and give every bus passenger a room of their own |
| [The part can be as big as the whole](03-part-as-big.md) | Pair the counting numbers one to one with the even numbers, the multiples of 10 and the square numbers, and explain Galileo's puzzle |
| [Numbers between numbers](04-numbers-between.md) | Find a fraction between any two fractions, and explain why there is no "next" fraction after 0 |
| [Decimals that never stop](05-decimals-never-stop.md) | Use long division to find repeating decimals like 0.333... and 0.142857142857..., and explain why they must repeat |
| [Zeno's race](06-zenos-race.md) | Tell Zeno's story of Achilles and the tortoise, explain why it seems to prove motion is impossible, and find when Achilles really passes |
