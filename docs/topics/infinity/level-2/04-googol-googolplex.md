# Googol and googolplex

*Infinity and strange math · level 2, lesson 4 of 6*

## A question to start

Here is a number with a funny name: a **googol**. It is a 1 followed by **one hundred** zeros.

That is more than the number of grains of sand on every beach on Earth. It is more than the number of drops of water
in all the oceans. Scientists think it is even more than the number of **atoms** (the tiny bits everything is made of)
in all the stars and planets we can see, put together.

So is a googol infinite? And if not, can you think of a number that is even bigger? (You already know a trick for that
from [level 1](../level-1/01-biggest-number-game.md).)

## The idea

### Zeros make numbers grow fast

Every time you put a 0 on the end of a whole number, it becomes **ten times as big**.

- 1 with one zero is **10** (ten).
- 1 with two zeros is **100** (a hundred): ten tens.
- 1 with three zeros is **1000** (a thousand): ten hundreds.

**Why does a zero make it ten times bigger?** Think of place value. In 30, the 3 is in the tens place: 3 tens. Put a
zero on the end and you get 300, where the 3 has moved to the hundreds place: 3 hundreds. Every digit slides one place
to the left, and each place is worth ten times the place before. So the whole number is ten times as big.

### The zeros ladder

Here are some numbers with names that you may have heard.

| Name | Written out | Zeros after the 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ten | 10 | 1 |
| hundred | 100 | 2 |
| thousand | 1,000 | 3 |
| million | 1,000,000 | 6 |
| billion | 1,000,000,000 | 9 |
| trillion | 1,000,000,000,000 | 12 |

The commas are there to help your eyes: they split the zeros into groups of three. A million is a thousand
thousands, so it has $3 + 3 = 6$ zeros. A billion is a thousand millions: $6 + 3 = 9$ zeros.

### Meet the googol

A **googol** has **100** zeros. Here it is, written out: a 1, then ten rows of ten zeros.

```
1
0000000000
0000000000
0000000000
0000000000
0000000000
0000000000
0000000000
0000000000
0000000000
0000000000
```

It fits on a page easily. You could write it in a minute or two. But it is unbelievably big. A trillion has only 12
zeros. A googol has 100. Every one of those 88 extra zeros makes the number **ten times** bigger again.

The story goes that the name was made up about a hundred years ago by a nine-year-old boy. His uncle, an American
mathematician called **Edward Kasner**, asked him for a name for a 1 followed by a hundred zeros, and the boy said
"googol". Kasner put the name in a book, and it stuck.

Grown-ups sometimes write a googol in a short way as $10^{100}$: "ten, multiplied by itself, with a hundred tens". The
little 100 counts the zeros. You do not need this short way yet, but now you know what it means if you see it.

### Bigger than the universe (in a way)

Scientists have estimated how many **atoms** there are in the whole part of the universe we can see: all the stars,
all the planets, all the gas between them. Their best guess is a number with about **80 zeros** after the 1. Nobody has
counted, of course; it is a careful estimate.

A googol has **100** zeros: about 20 more zeros than that. Each extra zero is ten times as big. So a googol is far,
far bigger than the number of atoms we think there are. If every atom in all the stars you can see were a sticker, you
would not have nearly enough stickers to count to a googol.

### How long to count to a googol?

The universe is very old: scientists think about 13.8 billion years. Counting one number every second since the
universe began, you would have counted to a number with only **18 digits** by now. A googol has **101** digits (the 1
and its hundred zeros). So counting to a googol would take far, far longer than the universe has existed.

### But a googol is still a number

Here is the important part. A googol is enormous, but it is **finite**. It is one particular number. It ends. And you
already know how to beat it:

> A googol and **one** is bigger than a googol.

A googol is not even close to infinity. Infinity is not "a really big number". It is the fact that the numbers **never
end**, and a googol is just one of them, with lots more numbers after it.

### Googolplex

Here is a much, much bigger number. A **googolplex** is a 1 followed by **a googol zeros**. Not a hundred zeros: a
googol of them.

Can you write a googolplex down? You would need to write a googol zeros. Even if you wrote each zero on a single atom,
there are not enough atoms in all the stars and planets we can see: about 80 zeros' worth of atoms, but you need a
googol of zeros. **Nobody can ever write a googolplex out in full.** There is not enough stuff in the universe.

And yet a googolplex is **still finite**. It is a real number, with a clear rule that says exactly what it is. A
googolplex and one is bigger. Infinity is still infinitely far away.

## Worked examples

**1. How many zeros?** A thousand thousands is a million. Count the zeros: a thousand has 3, so a thousand thousands
has $3 + 3 = 6$ **zeros**: 1,000,000.

**2. Ten times a googol.** A googol has 100 zeros. Ten times a googol puts **one more zero** on the end, so it has
**101 zeros**. It is a 1 followed by 101 zeros.

**3. How many digits?** A googol is a 1 followed by 100 zeros. How many digits is that? The 1 is one digit and the zeros
are 100 more: $1 + 100 = 101$ **digits**.

**4. Which is bigger?** A trillion trillions, or a googol? A trillion has 12 zeros, so a trillion trillions has
$12 + 12 = 24$ zeros. A googol has 100 zeros. More zeros (with a 1 in front) means bigger, so **a googol** is much
bigger.

**5. Writing time.** You write one zero every second. How long to write a googol? 100 zeros take **100 seconds**, which
is a minute and 40 seconds. How long for a googolplex? A googol seconds: much longer than the age of the universe.

## Try it

1. How many zeros does a hundred have? A million?
2. What do you get if you put a zero on the end of 45? How many times bigger is it?
3. A billion is a thousand millions. How many zeros does it have? Explain using the zeros of a million and a thousand.
4. What is one more than a googol? Is it bigger than a googol?
5. A googol times ten times ten: how many zeros?
6. Kai says: "A googolplex is so big that it must be infinity." Explain why Kai is wrong.
7. Which number has more zeros: a million millions, or a billion? How many zeros does each have?

??? note "Answers"
    1. A hundred has **2** zeros. A million has **6** zeros.
    2. **450**. It is **ten times** as big: the 4 moved from the tens place to the hundreds place, and the 5 from the
       ones place to the tens place.
    3. A million has 6 zeros and a thousand has 3, so a thousand millions has $6 + 3 = 9$ **zeros**:
       1,000,000,000.
    4. A googol and one is a 1, then 99 zeros, then a 1 at the end. It **is** bigger than a googol, by one. So a
       googol is not the biggest number.
    5. Each "times ten" adds one zero: $100 + 1 + 1 = 102$ **zeros**.
    6. A googolplex is a particular number with a clear rule (a 1 followed by a googol zeros). It is finite: a
       googolplex **and one** is bigger. Infinity is not a number at all; it is the fact that the numbers never end.
    7. A million millions has $6 + 6 = 12$ zeros (it is a trillion). A billion has 9 zeros. So **a million millions**
       has more zeros, and it is the bigger number.

## Common mistake

**Thinking a googol (or a googolplex) is infinity.** They are huge, but each one is a number with a next number after
it. Infinity is not at the end of the ladder of big numbers, because the ladder has no end.

**Adding instead of putting zeros on.** Ten times a googol is a 1 with 101 zeros, not "a googol and ten". Putting a
zero on the end multiplies by ten.
