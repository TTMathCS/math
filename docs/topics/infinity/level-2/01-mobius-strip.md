# The Möbius strip

*Infinity and strange math · level 2, lesson 1 of 6*

## A question to start

A sheet of paper has two sides, a front and a back. You could colour the front red and the back blue, and the two
colours would never touch, except at the edges.

What if you could make a loop of paper with **only one side**? An ant could walk over every part of it, on "both
sides", without ever crossing an edge. And if you cut it down the middle, it would **not** fall into two pieces.

It sounds impossible. You can make one in two minutes with a strip of paper and some tape. Get them ready: this
lesson is **try first, then explain**.

## The idea

### What you need

- A few long strips of paper, about 3 centimetres wide. Cut them from the long side of a sheet of paper, or tape two
  strips end to end to make them longer.
- Sticky tape.
- A crayon or felt pen.
- Scissors (ask a grown-up to help if you need it).

### Making the strip

Hold a strip flat. Mark the corners: **A** at the top left and **B** at the bottom left. At the other end, mark **B**
at the top right and **A** at the bottom right.

Now bring the two ends together as if to make a ring. Before you tape, **turn one end over**. That is called a **half
twist**: half of a full turn. Now the A's meet and the B's meet. Tape them together, all the way across.

The loop you have made is called a **Möbius strip** (say "MER-bee-us"). It is named after **August Möbius**, a German
mathematician. He and another German mathematician, **Johann Listing**, each found it in 1858.

Make an **ordinary ring** too, from another strip, with **no** twist. You will need it to compare.

### Test 1: how many sides?

Take the ordinary ring. Put your crayon on the **outside**, in the middle of the strip, and draw a line along it
without lifting the crayon. You go round once and meet your line. The **inside** is still clean. The ring has two
sides, the inside and the outside, and you only coloured one.

Now do the same on the Möbius strip. Put the crayon in the middle and draw, turning the strip as you go. Keep going.
Do not lift the crayon and do not cross an edge.

What happens? You go round once and you are **not** back at the start: your crayon is on the "other side" of the paper,
just under where you began. Keep going. After going round **twice**, you meet the start of your line. Now look: there
is a line on **every** part of the strip, on both "sides". There was only ever **one side**.

### Test 2: how many edges?

An **edge** is where the paper stops. The ordinary ring has two edges: the top rim and the bottom rim. Run your finger
along the top rim: you go round once and come back, and you never touch the bottom rim.

Run your finger along the edge of the Möbius strip. Go round once ... and your finger is on the "other" edge! Keep
going, and after going round **twice**, you are back where you started. The Möbius strip has only **one edge**.

| | Sides | Edges |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary ring | 2 | 2 |
| Möbius strip | 1 | 1 |

### Why one side? The two-lane road

Here is a way to understand what the half twist does. Pretend the strip is a **road with two lanes**: an orange lane
along the top half and a blue lane along the bottom half, on the front of the paper.

On the ordinary ring, the orange lane joins up with itself, and the blue lane joins up with itself. Two separate
lanes.

On the Möbius strip, the half twist turns one end over. So at the tape, the **orange** lane at one end meets the
**blue** lane at the other end. Drive along the orange lane all the way round, and when you cross the tape you are in
the blue lane. Drive along the blue lane all the way round, and you are back in the orange lane.

So the two lanes are really **one long lane**, twice as long as the strip. That is why the crayon line needed two
trips round, and why it ended up on "both sides": there is only one road, and it visits every part of the paper.

### Try it: cut down the middle

Now the big surprise. Take the scissors, poke a hole in the middle of the strip, and cut along your crayon line, all
the way round.

- **Ordinary ring:** you get **two** thin rings, the same length as before. No surprise.
- **Möbius strip:** you get **one** loop, **twice as long** and half as wide! Try it, and see.

**Why?** Use the two lanes. Cutting down the middle splits the orange lane from the blue lane. On the ordinary ring
each lane is its own loop, so you get two loops. But on the Möbius strip, the orange lane and the blue lane are joined
end to end into one long lane. Cutting them apart from each other does not cut that long lane anywhere. So you get
one long loop, twice as long. (Look closely: the new loop has more twist in it, and if you test it with a crayon, it
has **two** sides. Cutting changed it.)

### Try it: cut one third of the way in

Make a new Möbius strip. This time, start cutting **one third** of the way in from the edge, not in the middle. Keep
the scissors the same distance from the edge, and keep cutting. You will go round **twice** before you get back to
the start.

What do you get? **Two loops, linked together like a chain**: a thin Möbius strip, the same length as the one you
started with, and a long loop, twice as long, hooked through it.

**Why?** Now the road has **three lanes**: top, middle and bottom. The half twist swaps top and bottom, but the
**middle** lane stays in the middle. So:

- the middle lane joins up with itself after one trip: that is a thin Möbius strip, as long as the original;
- the top lane joins the bottom lane, and the bottom lane joins the top lane: together they make one loop, twice as
  long.

The two loops are linked because the long loop runs right alongside the middle one, on both of its "sides".

## Worked examples

**1. One trip or two?** An ant walks along the middle of a Möbius strip that is 30 centimetres long. How far does it
walk before it is back where it started, standing the right way up?

One trip round is 30 centimetres, but after one trip the ant is on the "other side" of the paper, upside down under
its starting spot. It needs a second trip. So it walks $30 + 30 = 60$ centimetres.

**2. The edge.** The strip is 30 centimetres long. How long is the one edge of the Möbius strip?

Your finger went round **twice** along the edge before it came back. Each trip is 30 centimetres, so the edge is
$30 + 30 = 60$ centimetres long. (An ordinary ring of the same strip has two edges, each 30 centimetres long.)

**3. More twists.** Make a loop with **two** half twists (one full twist). How many sides does it have?

Use the lanes. The first half twist swaps the lanes, and the second half twist swaps them **back**. So the orange lane
meets the orange lane again: **two separate lanes, two sides**. It is a twisted ordinary ring. If you cut it down the
middle you get **two** loops, but they are linked together.

**4. Three half twists.** Swap, swap back, swap again: the lanes end up swapped. So a loop with **three** half twists
has **one side**, like a Möbius strip.

The rule: **an odd number of half twists gives one side; an even number gives two sides.** Each half twist swaps the
lanes, and you just need to know whether they end up swapped or not.

## Try it

1. How many sides and how many edges does an ordinary ring have? A Möbius strip?
2. A Möbius strip is made from a strip 20 centimetres long. How far does a crayon line along the middle go before it
   meets its start?
3. You cut an ordinary ring down the middle. What do you get?
4. You cut a Möbius strip down the middle. What do you get? How long is it, if the strip was 20 centimetres long?
5. Will a loop with 4 half twists have one side or two? What about 5 half twists?
6. Make a Möbius strip and colour the "outside" red. Where does the red stop?
7. Cut a Möbius strip one third of the way in. Describe what you get and explain it with three lanes.

??? note "Answers"
    1. Ordinary ring: **2 sides and 2 edges**. Möbius strip: **1 side and 1 edge**.
    2. It goes round **twice**: $20 + 20 = 40$ centimetres.
    3. **Two** thin rings, each as long as the first ring.
    4. **One** loop, twice as long: $20 + 20 = 40$ centimetres long (and half as wide).
    5. 4 half twists: swap, back, swap, back. The lanes end up **not** swapped, so **two sides**. 5 half twists: they
       end up swapped, so **one side**. (Even number: two sides. Odd number: one side.)
    6. The red **never stops at an edge**: if you keep colouring without crossing an edge, the whole strip turns red.
       There is no "inside" to keep clean.
    7. You get **two linked loops**: a thin Möbius strip, as long as the original, and a loop twice as long. The middle
       lane stays in the middle at the twist, so it joins itself after one trip: the short Möbius strip. The top and
       bottom lanes swap at the twist, so they join each other into one long loop.

## Common mistake

**Guessing instead of trying.** Almost everyone guesses "two loops" for the cut down the middle, and "two loops, not
linked" for the cut at one third. Make the strips and cut them: the paper is the best judge. Then use the lanes to see
**why**.

**Stopping the crayon too early.** After one trip round, the line seems to have "missed". Keep going for a second trip,
and the line meets itself.
