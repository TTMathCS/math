# Explore: Ratios, unit rates and scale

*Ratio and rates · step 2 of 5*

## A question to start

Earth is about 12 742 km across. Mars is about 6 779 km across. If you made a model of Earth the size of a football,
22 cm across, how big should the model of Mars be? Would a tennis ball do, or a marble, or a grape? To answer, you
need to compare sizes with a **ratio**, and then use the same ratio at a different **scale**. (Answer below: about
11.7 cm, roughly a grapefruit.)

## The idea

### Writing a ratio

The "for every" of [Play](01-play.md) has a short way of writing. "2 orange beads for every 3 blue beads" is

$$\text{orange} : \text{blue} = 2 : 3$$

read "2 to 3". The **order matters**: blue to orange is $3 : 2$, a different ratio. A ratio can compare more than two
things: a mortar mixed from 1 part cement, 3 parts sand and 1 part water is $1 : 3 : 1$.

### Equivalent ratios and simplest form

Multiplying or dividing **every** part of a ratio by the same number gives an **equivalent ratio**, one that
describes the same mixture:

$$2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 10 : 15 = 20 : 30.$$

A ratio is in **simplest form** when the parts are whole numbers with no common factor except 1. To simplify, divide
every part by the **greatest common factor** (the biggest number that divides them all): $18 : 24$, both divisible by
6, becomes $3 : 4$. If a ratio has fractions or decimals, multiply up first: $1.5 : 2 = 3 : 4$.

### Parts and wholes

A ratio $2 : 3$ compares **part to part**. The whole has $2 + 3 = 5$ parts, so orange beads are $\tfrac25$ of the
necklace and blue beads are $\tfrac35$. Fractions compare **part to whole**. Mixing these up is the most common ratio
error of all, so always ask: "Is this a part compared with a part, or a part compared with the whole?"

### Sharing in a ratio

To share an amount in the ratio $a : b$:

1. Add the parts: $a + b$.
2. Find the size of one part: divide the amount by $a + b$.
3. Multiply: the shares are $a$ parts and $b$ parts.

This is exactly the "rounds" method from Play, written compactly.

### Unit rates and the best buy

A **rate** compares two different kinds of quantity: dollars per kilogram, kilometres per hour, heartbeats per
minute. A **unit rate** is a rate for **one** unit of the second quantity: "4 dollars per 1 kilogram". To find it,
divide. Unit rates let you compare offers fairly.

### Scale

A **scale** is a ratio between a drawing (or model) and the real thing, usually written with 1 first:

$$\text{map} : \text{real} = 1 : 50\,000$$

means 1 cm on the map is 50 000 cm on the ground. Since 1 km = 1000 m = 100 000 cm, that is 1 cm to 0.5 km.
The units must be the **same** on both sides of a scale ratio, which is why the numbers get so big.

- Map to real: **multiply** by the scale number.
- Real to map: **divide** by it.

## Worked examples

**1. Sharing.** Share 40 dollars between Kim and Lee in the ratio $3 : 5$.

- Parts: $3 + 5 = 8$.
- One part: $40 \div 8 = 5$ dollars.
- Kim: $3 \times 5 = 15$ dollars. Lee: $5 \times 5 = 25$ dollars. Check: $15 + 25 = 40$, and $15 : 25 = 3 : 5$.

**2. The best buy.** A pack of 6 juice boxes costs 4.20 dollars. A pack of 10 costs 6.50 dollars. Which is better
value?

- Small pack: $4.20 \div 6 = 0.70$ dollars per box.
- Big pack: $6.50 \div 10 = 0.65$ dollars per box.
- The big pack is cheaper per box, by 5 cents (as long as you will drink them all).

**3. A map.** On a map with scale $1 : 25\,000$, two towers are 6 cm apart. How far apart are they really?

- Real distance: $6 \times 25\,000 = 150\,000$ cm.
- $150\,000 \text{ cm} = 1500 \text{ m} = 1.5$ km.

**4. Earth and Mars.** The ratio of diameters is

$$\text{Earth} : \text{Mars} = 12\,742 : 6\,779.$$

Dividing both by 6 779 gives about $1.88 : 1$: Earth is a little less than twice as wide as Mars. For a 22 cm
football Earth, the Mars model is $22 \div 1.88 \approx 11.7$ cm across. A grape would be far too small; a
grapefruit is about right. On this model, how far apart should the two balls be? At their closest, Earth and Mars are about
55 million km apart. That is about $55\,000\,000 \div 12\,742 \approx 4300$ Earth-widths, so the Mars ball would be
$4300 \times 22$ cm $\approx 950$ m away, nearly a kilometre. Space is mostly empty.

## Try it

1. Write $18 : 24$ in simplest form.
2. Share 56 in the ratio $3 : 4$.
3. A map has scale $1 : 50\,000$. A road on the map is 6 cm long. How long is the real road, in km?
4. Which is better value: 500 g of cereal for 3.40 dollars, or 750 g for 4.95 dollars?
5. A class has 12 girls and 15 boys. Write the ratio of girls to boys in simplest form. What fraction of the class
   are girls?
6. A recipe uses flour and sugar in the ratio $3 : 2$. How much sugar goes with 7.5 cups of flour?

??? note "Answers"
    1. The greatest common factor is 6: $18 \div 6 = 3$ and $24 \div 6 = 4$, so $3 : 4$.
    2. Parts: $3 + 4 = 7$. One part: $56 \div 7 = 8$. Shares: $3 \times 8 = 24$ and $4 \times 8 = 32$.
    3. $6 \times 50\,000 = 300\,000$ cm $= 3000$ m $= 3$ km.
    4. Compare price per 100 g. Small: $3.40 \div 5 = 0.68$ dollars. Large: $4.95 \div 7.5 = 0.66$ dollars. The 750 g
       box is slightly better value.
    5. $12 : 15 = 4 : 5$ (dividing by 3). The class has 27 pupils, so girls are $\dfrac{12}{27} = \dfrac{4}{9}$ of the
       class. Notice it is $\tfrac49$, not $\tfrac45$.
    6. 7.5 cups of flour is $7.5 \div 3 = 2.5$ "parts", so sugar is $2 \times 2.5 = 5$ cups.

## Common mistake

**Confusing a part-to-part ratio with a fraction.** If boys to girls is $2 : 3$, it is tempting to say "$\tfrac23$ of
the class are boys". But the class has $2 + 3 = 5$ parts, so boys are $\tfrac25$ of the class. The ratio compares
boys with girls; the fraction compares boys with everybody.
