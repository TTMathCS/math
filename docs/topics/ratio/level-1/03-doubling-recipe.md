# Doubling a recipe

*Ratio, rates and money · level 1, lesson 3 of 5*

## A question to start

Grandpa's banana cake needs **2 cups of flour** and **1 cup of sugar**. Today it is a big birthday party, so you want
to bake **2 cakes** that taste just like Grandpa's.

Your friend says: "Easy! Use 4 cups of flour. And keep 1 cup of sugar, because sugar is not flour."

Will your friend's cakes taste the same as Grandpa's? Or will something go wrong?

Think about it for a moment, then read on. By the end of this lesson you will know what to do with **every**
ingredient when you make more, and you will be able to explain **why** to your friend.

## The idea

### What a recipe is

A **recipe** is a list of what goes into a food, and how much of each. The things on the list are called
**ingredients**. Here is a simple recipe for one small cake.

| Ingredient | For 1 cake |
| --- | --- |
| Flour | 2 cups |
| Sugar | 1 cup |
| Eggs | 3 eggs |
| Milk | 1 cup |

A recipe is a "for every" list. It says: **for every cake**, use 2 cups of flour, 1 cup of sugar, 3 eggs and 1 cup of
milk. One cake is one group.

### Doubling means making the recipe twice

To **double** something means to have two of it. To double a recipe means to make the recipe **twice**.

Imagine two bowls side by side. Into each bowl, you put one full recipe.

Now pour both bowls into one giant bowl. What is in the giant bowl?

- Flour: 2 cups and 2 cups make **4 cups**.
- Sugar: 1 cup and 1 cup make **2 cups**.
- Eggs: 3 eggs and 3 eggs make **6 eggs**.
- Milk: 1 cup and 1 cup make **2 cups**.

| Ingredient | For 1 cake | For 2 cakes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flour | 2 cups | 4 cups |
| Sugar | 1 cup | 2 cups |
| Eggs | 3 eggs | 6 eggs |
| Milk | 1 cup | 2 cups |

**Every** ingredient doubled. Not just the flour. Every single one.

### Why every ingredient must double

Here is the reason, and it is a good one.

Each bowl on its own makes a perfect cake, because each bowl has exactly Grandpa's recipe. When you pour the two
bowls together, nothing new gets added and nothing gets taken away. The giant bowl is just **two perfect cakes'
worth** of batter. Cut the giant bowl's batter into two cake pans, and each pan gets a perfect cake.

So the doubled recipe must be exactly what two bowls hold. And two bowls hold double of **everything**.

### What goes wrong if you forget one

Now back to your friend, who used 4 cups of flour but only 1 cup of sugar.

Think of the giant bowl again. To make it match two of Grandpa's bowls, it needs 2 cups of sugar. Your friend's
bowl only has 1 cup. So the sugar is spread over twice as much batter. Each bite has only **half** the sugar it
should have. The cakes will taste much **less sweet**. They will not taste like Grandpa's.

The same thing happens with anything you forget to double:

- Forget to double the eggs, and the cake may not hold together.
- Forget to double the milk, and the batter will be too dry and thick.

The ingredients are a team. When one grows, they all have to grow together.

### Same taste, bigger amount

When two mixtures taste the same, we say they are mixed in the same **way**. Double the recipe and the cake tastes
the same, there is just more of it. Later in this topic you will learn the grown-up word for "mixed in the same
way". It is **ratio**. But you already know the idea: keep the groups the same and just make more groups.

### Tripling and more

You can make the recipe **3 times** too. That is called **tripling**. Use three bowls, or count by groups like in the
lesson ["For every"](02-for-every.md).

| Ingredient | 1 cake | 2 cakes | 3 cakes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flour (cups) | 2 | 4 | 6 |
| Sugar (cups) | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Eggs | 3 | 6 | 9 |
| Milk (cups) | 1 | 2 | 3 |

Look down the flour row: 2, 4, 6, counting by twos. The eggs row: 3, 6, 9, counting by threes. Every row counts by
its own number, the number for one cake.

## Worked examples

**Example 1. Pancakes for two batches.** One batch of pancakes needs 1 cup of flour, 1 egg and 2 cups of milk. How
much do you need for 2 batches?

1. Two batches means making the recipe twice.
2. Flour: 1 cup and 1 cup make **2 cups**.
3. Eggs: 1 egg and 1 egg make **2 eggs**.
4. Milk: 2 cups and 2 cups make **4 cups**.
5. Check: did every ingredient double? 1 became 2, 1 became 2, 2 became 4. Yes.

**Example 2. Fruit salad for three.** One bowl of fruit salad has 2 apples, 1 banana and 5 strawberries. How much
fruit do you need for 3 bowls?

1. Three bowls means the recipe 3 times. Count by groups for each fruit.
2. Apples: 2, 4, 6. So **6 apples**.
3. Bananas: 1, 2, 3. So **3 bananas**.
4. Strawberries: 5, 10, 15. So **15 strawberries**.

**Example 3. Spotting the mistake.** Lily wants to double this playdough recipe: 2 cups of flour, 1 cup of salt,
1 cup of water. She uses 4 cups of flour, 2 cups of salt and 1 cup of water. What will happen?

1. Doubling means every ingredient doubles.
2. Flour: 2 doubles to 4. Lily used 4. Good.
3. Salt: 1 doubles to 2. Lily used 2. Good.
4. Water: 1 doubles to 2. Lily used only **1**. Not good.
5. So Lily's playdough has only half the water it needs. It will be **too dry and crumbly**. She should add 1 more
   cup of water.

**Example 4. Going back.** Dad made a double batch of cookies and used 6 cups of oats. How many cups of oats are in
the normal recipe?

1. A double batch is two normal batches put together.
2. So 6 cups of oats is two equal amounts. Share 6 into 2 equal parts: 3 and 3.
3. The normal recipe uses **3 cups of oats**.
4. Check: 3 and 3 make 6. Yes.

## Try it

1. Juice for one glass: 1 scoop of powder and 1 glass of water. How much for 2 glasses?
2. One sandwich needs 2 slices of bread and 1 slice of cheese. How much bread and cheese for 2 sandwiches?
3. One smoothie needs 1 banana and 3 strawberries. How many bananas and strawberries for 3 smoothies?
4. A bird feeder mix uses 2 cups of seeds and 1 cup of nuts. Ben doubles it and uses 4 cups of seeds and 4 cups of
   nuts. Is that right? What will the mix be like?
5. A cake recipe uses 3 eggs. Mum made a double batch. How many eggs did she use?
6. A double batch of muffins used 4 cups of flour. How much flour does one batch use?
7. Mia says: "To double the recipe, I add 1 more of everything." The recipe is 2 cups of flour and 1 cup of milk.
   Use her way and the right way. Do they give the same answer?

??? note "Answers"
    1. **2 scoops and 2 glasses of water.** Make the recipe twice: 1 and 1 make 2, for each ingredient.
    2. **4 slices of bread and 2 slices of cheese.** Bread: 2 and 2 make 4. Cheese: 1 and 1 make 2.
    3. **3 bananas and 9 strawberries.** Bananas: 1, 2, 3. Strawberries: count by threes, 3, 6, 9.
    4. **No.** The seeds doubled correctly (2 to 4). But the nuts should double from 1 cup to 2 cups, and Ben used 4
       cups. That is too many nuts: the mix will be much more nutty than the real one.
    5. **6 eggs.** A double batch is the recipe twice: 3 and 3 make 6.
    6. **2 cups.** The double batch is two equal batches. Share 4 into 2 equal parts: 2 and 2.
    7. **No, they are different.** Mia's way: flour 2 and 1 more is 3 cups, milk 1 and 1 more is 2 cups. The right
       way: flour 2 and 2 make 4 cups, milk 1 and 1 make 2 cups. The milk happens to come out right, but the flour
       does not. Adding 1 to everything is **not** doubling. Doubling means adding the **whole** amount again.

## Common mistake

**Doubling only the "main" ingredient.** People often double the flour, the biggest amount, and forget the rest.
Every ingredient must double, even the little ones like a spoon of salt.

**Adding the same amount to everything.** "One more cup of everything" is not doubling. To double, each ingredient
gets its **own** amount again: 2 cups of flour gets 2 more, 1 cup of milk gets 1 more.
