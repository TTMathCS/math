# Master: Percent

*Ratio and rates · step 4 of 5*

## A question to start

A shop raises the price of a 100 dollar jacket by 20%. A month later, it has a sale: 20% off. Is the jacket back to
100 dollars? Most people say yes. It is not: it costs 96 dollars. And if the shop had put on the sale first and the
rise second, it would *still* cost 96 dollars. Percent is everywhere (prices, taxes, test marks, news headlines) and
it is full of little traps like this one. This page gives you one tool, the **multiplier**, that handles all of them.

## The idea

### Percent means "out of a hundred"

**Percent** comes from the Latin *per centum*, "for each hundred". The sign $\%$ means "divided by 100":

$$35\% = \frac{35}{100} = 0.35.$$

A percent is a ratio with the second part fixed at 100, which makes different things easy to compare. Converting:

- percent to decimal: divide by 100 (move the point two places left): $7\% = 0.07$, $150\% = 1.5$;
- decimal to percent: multiply by 100: $0.625 = 62.5\%$;
- fraction to percent: divide, then multiply by 100: $\tfrac38 = 0.375 = 37.5\%$.

Some to know by heart: $\tfrac12 = 50\%$, $\tfrac14 = 25\%$, $\tfrac34 = 75\%$, $\tfrac15 = 20\%$,
$\tfrac1{10} = 10\%$, $\tfrac13 \approx 33.3\%$.

### Percent of an amount

"Of" means multiply. $35\%$ of 240 is $0.35 \times 240 = 84$. A mental route: 10% of 240 is 24, so 30% is 72, 5% is
12, and 35% is $72 + 12 = 84$.

### One amount as a percent of another

To write $A$ as a percent of $B$, divide and multiply by 100:

$$\frac{A}{B} \times 100\%.$$

18 out of 72 is $\tfrac{18}{72} \times 100\% = 25\%$.

### Multipliers: the one tool

To **increase** an amount by $p\%$, you keep the whole 100% and add $p\%$, so you multiply by $1 + \tfrac{p}{100}$.
To **decrease** by $p\%$, multiply by $1 - \tfrac{p}{100}$.

| Change | Multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| up 20% | 1.20 |
| up 5% | 1.05 |
| up 100% (doubled) | 2 |
| down 20% | 0.80 |
| down 15% | 0.85 |
| down 100% (all gone) | 0 |

Multipliers make three hard problems easy.

**Repeated changes multiply.** Up 20% and then down 20% is $1.20 \times 0.80 = 0.96$: a 4% loss overall. The order
does not matter, because $0.80 \times 1.20$ is also 0.96. The two 20%s are not percents of the same thing: the rise
is 20% of 100, the fall is 20% of 120.

**Reverse percent.** If the price *after* a 15% discount is 68 dollars, then $0.85 \times \text{original} = 68$, so
the original was $68 \div 0.85 = 80$ dollars. Divide by the multiplier to go back. (Taking 15% of 68 and adding it
back on is wrong, because the 15% was of the original price, not of 68.)

**Percent change.** To find the percent by which something changed, compare the change with the **original**:

$$\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}} \times 100\%.$$

A positive answer is an increase; a negative one is a decrease.

### Percent or percentage points?

If a bank's interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, it rose by **1 percentage point**, the plain difference. But it rose
by **25%**, because 1 is a quarter of 4. News reports sometimes use whichever sounds bigger. A careful reader asks
which one is meant.

### Percents bigger than 100

Nothing stops a percent from passing 100. Mars's surface gravity is about 38% of Earth's, so Earth's is about
$1 \div 0.38 \approx 2.63$ times Mars's, or 263% of it. Both statements describe the same two numbers; only the
"of what?" changed.

## Worked examples

**1. The jacket.** $100 \times 1.20 = 120$, then $120 \times 0.80 = 96$ dollars. Overall multiplier $0.96$, a 4%
decrease.

**2. Percent change.** A town grew from 250 thousand to 290 thousand people. By what percent?

- Change: $290 - 250 = 40$ thousand.
- $\dfrac{40}{250} \times 100\% = 16\%$ increase.
- Check with a multiplier: $250 \times 1.16 = 290$.

**3. Reverse percent with tax.** A bill including 13% sales tax comes to 50.85 dollars. What was the price before
tax?

- The multiplier for adding 13% is 1.13.
- $50.85 \div 1.13 = 45$ dollars before tax. The tax was $50.85 - 45 = 5.85$ dollars.

**4. Three changes in a row.** A share price rises 10%, then falls 10%, then rises 10%. Overall change?

- $1.10 \times 0.90 \times 1.10 = 1.089$.
- That is an 8.9% increase overall, not 10%.

## Try it

1. Find 35% of 240 without a calculator.
2. A price of 45 dollars goes up by 12%. What is the new price?
3. After a 25% increase, a ticket costs 75 dollars. What did it cost before?
4. A population falls from 8000 to 6800. What is the percent change?
5. An interest rate goes from 4% to 5%. Describe the change in percentage points and in percent.
6. A price is cut by 30% and then the new price is cut by another 20%. Is that the same as a 50% cut?

??? note "Answers"
    1. 10% is 24, so 30% is 72; 5% is 12. $72 + 12 = 84$.
    2. $45 \times 1.12 = 50.40$ dollars.
    3. $75 \div 1.25 = 60$ dollars. (Check: $60 \times 1.25 = 75$.)
    4. $\dfrac{6800 - 8000}{8000} \times 100\% = \dfrac{-1200}{8000} \times 100\% = -15\%$, a 15% decrease.
    5. Up 1 percentage point. As a percent of the old rate, $\dfrac{1}{4} \times 100\% = 25\%$ higher.
    6. No. $0.70 \times 0.80 = 0.56$, so the price is 56% of the original: a 44% cut, not 50%.

## Common mistake

**Using the new amount as the base.** Percent change is always measured against the **original**. Going from 80 to
100 is a 25% increase ($\tfrac{20}{80}$), but going back from 100 to 80 is a 20% decrease ($\tfrac{20}{100}$). The
same gap of 20 is a different percent of a different starting point. This is the whole secret of the jacket.
