# Earning, spending, saving

*Ratio, rates and money · level 2, lesson 6 of 6*

## A question to start

Noor wants a pair of roller skates that cost **30 dollars**. She gets **5 dollars a week** for helping at home. How
many weeks until she can buy the skates?

Now a second question. On Saturday Noor looks in her wallet and finds only 6 dollars. She is sure she had more. Where
did the money go?

The first question is about **saving**. The second is about **keeping track**. Grown-ups do both all their lives,
and many find it hard. In this lesson you will learn a simple tool, the **money diary**, that answers both questions,
and you will see why steady saving always gets you there.

## The idea

### Three things money can do

- **Earning** is getting money: an allowance, a gift, money for doing a job like raking leaves.
- **Spending** is giving money away to buy something.
- **Saving** is keeping money for later.

Some families also talk about **sharing**: giving some money to help others, like a food bank or a friend in need.
A popular idea is to keep **three jars**: one to spend, one to save and one to share. Each time you earn money, you
split it between the jars.

### The money diary

A **money diary** is a list of every time money comes in or goes out. Each line has the day, what happened, the money
**in**, the money **out**, and how much you **have** after it.

Here is Noor's diary for one week. She starts the week with **4 dollars**.

| Day | What happened | Money in | Money out | Money I have |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sunday | start of the week | | | 4 dollars |
| Monday | allowance | 5 dollars | | 9 dollars |
| Tuesday | snack after school | | 2 dollars | 7 dollars |
| Thursday | helped wash the car | 3 dollars | | 10 dollars |
| Saturday | comic book | | 4 dollars | 6 dollars |

Now we can answer the second question. Noor had 10 dollars on Thursday. Then she bought a comic book for 4 dollars.
10 take away 4 is 6. The money went on the comic! Without the diary she would just feel that money "disappears".

### Why the diary always works

Every line follows one simple rule:

- Money **in**: what you have goes **up** by that much.
- Money **out**: what you have goes **down** by that much.

Money never appears or vanishes by magic. Every change has a line in the diary. So if you start right and write every
line, the last number **must** be right.

You can also check the whole week at once. Noor earned 5 and 3, which make **8 dollars** in. She spent 2 and 4, which
make **6 dollars** out. She started with 4. Then 4 and 8 make 12, and 12 take away 6 is **6**. That matches the last
line of the diary. Two ways, one answer: the diary is right.

### Saving toward a goal

A **savings goal** is an amount you want to reach, for something you want to buy. Noor's goal is 30 dollars for the
skates.

Each week is one step up, and every step is the same height: 5 dollars. After **6 steps** she reaches the goal line.
So Noor needs **6 weeks**.

### Why the steps are equal

Noor saves the **same** amount every week: 5 dollars for every week. That is a "for every" rule, just like 2 lemons
for every 5 cups of water in [Ratio tables](05-ratio-tables.md). You can even write it as a ratio table:

| Weeks | Dollars saved |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 10 |
| 3 | 15 |
| 6 | 30 |

The 6-weeks row is double the 3-weeks row: twice as many weeks, twice as much money.

### Three ways to reach a goal sooner

Look at the staircase. There are only three ways to reach the goal line sooner.

1. **Start higher.** If Noor already has some money saved, her staircase starts partway up.
2. **Take bigger steps.** Save more each week, by earning more or spending less.
3. **Pick a lower goal.** A cheaper thing, or a used one.

Every money plan in the world, even a grown-up's plan to buy a house, uses these same three ideas.

## Worked examples

**Example 1. How many weeks?** Noor wants the 30-dollar skates and saves 5 dollars a week. She is starting with 0.

1. Count by fives, one jump per week: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30.
2. That is 6 jumps. **6 weeks.**

**Example 2. Starting higher.** What if Noor already has 10 dollars saved?

1. Start the count at 10, then add 5 each week: 15 (week 1), 20 (week 2), 25 (week 3), 30 (week 4).
2. **4 weeks.** The 10 dollars she already had are worth 2 weeks of saving, because 10 is 2 groups of 5.

**Example 3. Earning and spending.** Sam earns 8 dollars a week and spends 3 dollars of it on treats. He saves the
rest for a 30-dollar game. How many weeks?

1. Each week he saves what is left: 8 take away 3 is **5 dollars a week**.
2. Count by fives to 30: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30. **6 weeks.**
3. If Sam spent nothing on treats, he would save 8 a week: 8, 16, 24, 32. **4 weeks**, with 2 dollars over. The
   treats cost him 2 extra weeks of waiting.

**Example 4. Finding a missing number.** Ella's diary for one day is smudged. She started with 12 dollars, spent
something on a gift, and has 7 dollars left. How much was the gift?

1. The money went **down** from 12 to 7.
2. Count up from 7 to 12: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12. That is 5.
3. The gift cost **5 dollars**.
4. Check: 12 take away 5 is 7. Yes.

## Try it

1. You start with 2 dollars. You earn 5 dollars, then spend 3 dollars. How much do you have now?
2. You save 4 dollars a week for a 20-dollar puzzle, starting from 0. How many weeks?
3. Noor wants the 30-dollar skates. She has 15 dollars already and saves 5 a week. How many weeks?
4. Ben wants the 30-dollar skates too, but he can only save 3 dollars a week. How many weeks?
5. Kai earns 7 dollars a week and spends 2. He is saving for a 25-dollar helmet. How many weeks?
6. A diary says: start 10 dollars, then money in 6 dollars, then money out (smudged), and at the end 12 dollars. How
   much was the money out?
7. Plan A: save 5 dollars every week. Plan B: save 2 dollars in week 1, 4 in week 2, 6 in week 3, and 2 more each
   week after that. Which plan reaches 30 dollars first?
8. Ava saves 4 dollars a week for the 30-dollar skates. How many weeks? How much will she have left over after
   buying them?

## Common mistake

**Forgetting small spending.** A snack here, a sticker there: small amounts are easy to forget, and they are exactly
why money seems to "disappear". Write down **every** time money comes in or goes out, even 1 dollar.

**Counting earning as saving.** If you earn 8 dollars and spend 3, you did not save 8. You saved only what is left, 5.
Use the saved amount, not the earned amount, to count the weeks to your goal.
