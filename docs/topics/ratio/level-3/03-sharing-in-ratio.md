# Sharing in a ratio

*Ratio, rates and money · level 3, lesson 3 of 6*

## A question to start

Sam and Ria run a stall selling lemonade. Sam works for 3 hours and Ria works for 4 hours. At the end of the day they
have made 35 dollars.

Sam says: "Let's split it half and half. That's 17.50 dollars each."

Ria says: "That's not fair. I worked longer."

Ria has a point. But how much should each of them get, so that the money matches the work? If you share 35 dollars so
that **for every 3 dollars Sam gets, Ria gets 4**, every hour of work earns the same. How do you do that without
guessing?

## The idea

### Sharing in rounds

Imagine the 35 dollars as a pile of loonies. Share them out in **rounds**. In each round, Sam takes 3 loonies and Ria
takes 4. That uses $3 + 4 = 7$ loonies per round.

How many rounds until the pile runs out? $35 \div 7 = 5$ rounds.

- Sam took 3 loonies in each of 5 rounds: $3 \times 5 = 15$ dollars.
- Ria took 4 loonies in each of 5 rounds: $4 \times 5 = 20$ dollars.

Check: $15 + 20 = 35$. ✓ And $15 : 20$ divides by 5 to give $3 : 4$. ✓

Each of them earned $15 \div 3 = 5$ dollars per hour and $20 \div 4 = 5$ dollars per hour. Same pay for each hour:
that is why it is fair.

This "rounds" idea is the whole method. The rest of this lesson just gives it a quicker look.

### Bar models

A **bar model** is a drawing that shows the ratio as boxes of equal size. For a $3 : 4$ share, draw 3 boxes for Sam
and 4 boxes for Ria, all the same size.

All 7 boxes together are worth 35 dollars. Since the boxes are equal, one box is $35 \div 7 = 5$ dollars. Then Sam's 3
boxes are 15 dollars and Ria's 4 boxes are 20 dollars. One box in the bar model is one round in the loonie picture.

### The three steps

To share an amount in the ratio $a : b$:

1. **Add the parts** to find how many boxes there are: $a + b$.
2. **Find one part**: divide the amount by the number of boxes.
3. **Multiply**: one share is $a$ boxes, the other is $b$ boxes.

Then **check**: the shares add up to the amount, and they simplify to the ratio.

Step 2 is the key step. Almost every sharing problem is solved by finding the value of **one part** first.

### What if you know something else?

The bar model is useful because it works even when you are **not** given the total. You always find one box first,
using whatever you are told.

**You know one share.** Ali and Bea share stickers $3 : 5$, and Ali gets 12. Ali's 3 boxes are 12 stickers, so one box
is $12 \div 3 = 4$. Bea's 5 boxes are $5 \times 4 = 20$ stickers.

**You know the difference.** Ali and Bea share stickers $3 : 5$, and Bea gets 10 more than Ali. In the bar model,
Bea has 2 more boxes than Ali. Those 2 boxes are the 10 extra stickers, so one box is $10 \div 2 = 5$. Ali gets
$3 \times 5 = 15$ and Bea gets $5 \times 5 = 25$. Check: $25 - 15 = 10$. ✓

**Three people.** The same steps work for $a : b : c$. Add all three parts to count the boxes.

### Why the answer is always fair

Sharing $3 : 4$ means "Ria gets $\tfrac43$ of what Sam gets", and the bar model makes that true by building both
shares from the **same-size** box. You could also say Sam gets $\tfrac37$ of the total and Ria gets $\tfrac47$, since
there are 7 boxes. Indeed $\tfrac37 \times 35 = 15$ and $\tfrac47 \times 35 = 20$. This is the part-to-whole idea from
[lesson 1](01-writing-ratio.md) in action.

### When it does not divide evenly

With money, the one-part value may come out in cents. Share 50 dollars in the ratio $1 : 3$: there are 4 boxes, and
one box is $50 \div 4 = 12.50$ dollars. The shares are 12.50 and 37.50 dollars. That is fine.

But with things you cannot cut, like marbles or people, a share that is not a whole number tells you the ratio cannot
be met exactly. Sharing 20 marbles in the ratio $2 : 1$ gives one box $= 20 \div 3 = 6\tfrac23$ marbles. You cannot
do it exactly; the nearest is 13 and 7, or 14 and 6.

## Worked examples

**1. Share 56 dollars in the ratio $3 : 4$.**

- Boxes: $3 + 4 = 7$.
- One box: $56 \div 7 = 8$ dollars.
- Shares: $3 \times 8 = 24$ dollars and $4 \times 8 = 32$ dollars.
- Check: $24 + 32 = 56$ ✓, and $24 : 32 = 3 : 4$ (divide by 8) ✓.

**2. Three people.** Grandma gives 90 dollars to three grandchildren in the ratio of their ages, 4 : 5 : 6.

- Boxes: $4 + 5 + 6 = 15$.
- One box: $90 \div 15 = 6$ dollars.
- Shares: $4 \times 6 = 24$, $5 \times 6 = 30$, $6 \times 6 = 36$ dollars.
- Check: $24 + 30 + 36 = 90$ ✓.

**3. Knowing one share.** Flour and sugar are in the ratio $5 : 2$. A baker uses 600 g of sugar. How much flour, and
how much of the two together?

- Sugar is 2 boxes, so one box is $600 \div 2 = 300$ g.
- Flour is 5 boxes: $5 \times 300 = 1500$ g.
- Together: 7 boxes, $7 \times 300 = 2100$ g.

**4. Knowing the difference.** A bag has red and blue marbles in the ratio $2 : 7$. There are 35 more blue marbles than
red. How many marbles altogether?

- Blue has $7 - 2 = 5$ more boxes than red, and those are 35 marbles.
- One box: $35 \div 5 = 7$ marbles.
- Altogether: $2 + 7 = 9$ boxes, $9 \times 7 = 63$ marbles (14 red, 49 blue). Check: $49 - 14 = 35$ ✓.

**5. Back to the lemonade stall.** Sam (3 hours) and Ria (4 hours) earn 35 dollars. The fair split is $3 : 4$: 15
dollars for Sam and 20 dollars for Ria. Half-and-half would have given Sam 17.50 dollars, which is
$17.50 \div 3 \approx 5.83$ dollars per hour, while Ria would get only $17.50 \div 4 = 4.375$ dollars per hour, about
4.38. The ratio split gives both of them 5 dollars per hour.

## Try it

1. Share 40 dollars in the ratio $3 : 5$.
2. Share 72 stickers in the ratio $1 : 2$.
3. Share 100 dollars in the ratio $2 : 3 : 5$.
4. A school has teachers and pupils in the ratio $1 : 18$. There are 25 teachers. How many pupils?
5. Juice and water are mixed $2 : 3$ to make 1.5 litres of drink. How much juice is used?
6. Jo and Kai share money in the ratio $4 : 7$. Kai gets 21 dollars more than Jo. How much does each get?
7. Can 25 children be split into two teams in the ratio $2 : 3$? Can 26?
8. A bead pattern repeats 2 orange, 1 green, 3 blue. A necklace uses 48 beads with no part pattern left over. How many
   of each colour?

??? note "Answers"
    1. Boxes: 8. One box: $40 \div 8 = 5$. Shares: **15 and 25 dollars**. Check: $15 + 25 = 40$.
    2. Boxes: 3. One box: $72 \div 3 = 24$. Shares: **24 and 48 stickers**.
    3. Boxes: $2 + 3 + 5 = 10$. One box: 10 dollars. Shares: **20, 30 and 50 dollars**.
    4. Teachers are 1 box, so one box is 25. Pupils: $18 \times 25 =$ **450**.
    5. 1.5 litres is 1500 mL. Boxes: 5, so one box is $1500 \div 5 = 300$ mL. Juice is 2 boxes: **600 mL**.
    6. Kai has $7 - 4 = 3$ more boxes, worth 21 dollars, so one box is 7 dollars. Jo: $4 \times 7 =$ **28 dollars**;
       Kai: $7 \times 7 =$ **49 dollars**. Check: $49 - 28 = 21$.
    7. Boxes: 5. For 25 children one box is 5: teams of **10 and 15**, yes. For 26 children one box would be
       $26 \div 5 = 5.2$ children, which is not a whole number, so **no**.
    8. One repeat has $2 + 1 + 3 = 6$ beads, and $48 \div 6 = 8$ repeats. Orange $2 \times 8 = 16$, green
       $1 \times 8 = 8$, blue $3 \times 8 = 24$. Check: $16 + 8 + 24 = 48$.

## Common mistake

**Dividing by one part instead of the total.** To share 40 in the ratio $3 : 5$, some people work out $40 \div 3$ or
$40 \div 5$. But the 40 is spread over **all** $3 + 5 = 8$ boxes, so one box is $40 \div 8$. Draw the bar model and
you will not make this mistake.

**Forgetting to check.** The two shares must add back to the total. If they do not, something went wrong. It takes
five seconds and catches most errors.
