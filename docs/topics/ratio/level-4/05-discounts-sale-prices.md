# Discounts and sale prices

*Ratio, rates and money · level 4, lesson 5 of 6*

## A question to start

Two shops are having a sale.

- Shop A: **30% off everything.**
- Shop B: **20 dollars off everything.**

You want a jacket that costs 80 dollars at both shops. Which shop should you go to? Now your friend wants a shirt
that costs 50 dollars at both shops. Which shop should **she** go to?

Here is the surprise: the answers are different. One offer is better for the jacket and the other is better for the
shirt. There is even a price at which the two offers are exactly equal. Finding it is part of this lesson.

## The idea

### Words of a sale

- The **original price** (or regular price) is the price before the sale.
- The **discount** is the amount taken off. It may be given in dollars ("20 dollars off") or as a percent ("30%
  off").
- The **sale price** is what you pay: original price minus discount.

A percent discount is always a percent **of the original price**.

### Method 1: find the discount, then subtract

30% off an 80 dollar jacket:

- Discount: 10% of 80 is 8, so 30% is 24 dollars.
- Sale price: $80 - 24 = 56$ dollars.

### Method 2: find what you pay

If 30% is taken off, what is left? The whole price is 100%, so you pay $100\% - 30\% = 70\%$ of it.

So the sale price is 70% of 80: $0.70 \times 80 = 56$ dollars. Same answer, one step.

In general: **for $p\%$ off, multiply by $(100 - p)\%$ as a decimal.** 30% off is $\times 0.70$; 15% off is
$\times 0.85$; 25% off is $\times 0.75$; half price (50% off) is $\times 0.5$.

This is the same trick as sales tax in [lesson 4](04-sales-tax.md), where adding 13% was $\times 1.13$. A number you
multiply by to make a percent change is called a **multiplier**. Taking a percent **off** gives a multiplier **less
than 1**; adding a percent **on** gives a multiplier **more than 1**.

### Comparing a percent with an amount

Now the two shops. For the 80 dollar jacket:

- Shop A: 30% of 80 is 24 dollars off.
- Shop B: 20 dollars off.

Shop A saves more. For the 50 dollar shirt:

- Shop A: 30% of 50 is 15 dollars off.
- Shop B: 20 dollars off.

Shop B saves more. Why the switch? A **percent** discount grows with the price: the dearer the item, the bigger the
discount. A **fixed** discount is the same 20 dollars whatever the price. For cheap items the fixed discount wins; for
dear items the percent wins.

Where do they cross? When 30% of the price is exactly 20 dollars. 10% of that price would be a third of 20, about 6.67
dollars, so the price is about **66.67 dollars**. Below that, take the 20 dollars off; above it, take the 30%.

### "Buy one, get one" deals

Some deals are percents in disguise.

- **"Buy 2, get 1 free"**: you get 3 items for the price of 2. You pay $\tfrac23$ of the full price, so it is $\tfrac13$
  off, about **33.3% off**, but only if you wanted 3.
- **"Second one half price"**: two items for $1 + \tfrac12 = 1.5$ times the price, instead of 2 times. You pay
  $1.5 \div 2 = 0.75$ of the full price: **25% off** the pair.

### Discount and tax: does the order matter?

In a shop, the discount comes off first and then the tax is added. With multipliers, a 25% discount and 13% tax on a
120 dollar bike is

$$120 \times 0.75 \times 1.13 = 101.70 \text{ dollars}.$$

What if the tax were added first and then 25% taken off? $120 \times 1.13 \times 0.75$ is **also** 101.70 dollars,
because you can multiply numbers in any order. That is a nice fact to know when you check a receipt: the order does
not change the total.

### Reading sale signs carefully

- **"Up to 50% off"** means **some** items are 50% off. Many may be less.
- **"Save 40%"** is a percent of the **original** price.
- **"30% off, then take an extra 20% off"** is **not** 50% off. The extra 20% is taken from the already reduced
  price, so it is a smaller amount. On a 100 dollar item: $100 \times 0.70 = 70$, then $70 \times 0.80 = 56$. That is
  44% off, not 50%. Changes one after another are the subject of [level 6](../level-6/index.md).

### What percent off was it?

If you know the original and sale prices, find the discount in dollars and write it as a percent of the original, as
in [lesson 3](03-as-a-percent.md). A game that was 60 dollars and is now 45 dollars has 15 dollars off, and
$15 \div 60 = 0.25$, so it is **25% off**.

## Worked examples

**1. A simple discount.** Shoes cost 45 dollars. They are 15% off. What is the sale price?

- You pay 85%: $0.85 \times 45 = 38.25$ dollars.
- Check by method 1: 10% is 4.50, 5% is 2.25, so 15% is 6.75. $45 - 6.75 = 38.25$. ✓

**2. Half price and a quarter off.** A 64 dollar board game is 25% off. Another 64 dollar game is half price. Find
both sale prices.

- 25% off: $0.75 \times 64 = 48$ dollars.
- Half price: $0.5 \times 64 = 32$ dollars.

**3. Which offer?** A 90 dollar coat. Shop A: 25% off. Shop B: 20 dollars off.

- Shop A: 25% of 90 is 22.50 dollars off, so 67.50 dollars.
- Shop B: $90 - 20 = 70$ dollars.
- Shop A is cheaper by 2.50 dollars.

**4. A "buy 2, get 1 free" deal.** Notebooks cost 6 dollars each, buy 2 get 1 free. What is the price per notebook if
you buy 3?

- You pay for 2: 12 dollars for 3 notebooks.
- Per notebook: $12 \div 3 = 4$ dollars, instead of 6. That is 2 dollars off each, and $2 \div 6 \approx 0.333$, so
  about 33.3% off.

**5. Discount, then tax.** A 120 dollar bike is 25% off, and then 13% HST is added.

- Sale price: $0.75 \times 120 = 90$ dollars.
- With tax: $90 \times 1.13 = 101.70$ dollars.

**6. Finding the percent off.** A tablet was 250 dollars and is now 200 dollars.

- Discount: $250 - 200 = 50$ dollars.
- As a percent of the original: $50 \div 250 = 0.2$, so **20% off**.

## Try it

1. Find the sale price of a 35 dollar shirt at 20% off.
2. Find the sale price of a 65 dollar item at 40% off.
3. Use a multiplier to find the price of a 220 dollar bike at 15% off.
4. A hoodie was 50 dollars and is now 42 dollars. What percent off is that?
5. For a 45 dollar item, which is better: 20% off or 10 dollars off? For a 60 dollar item? At what price are the two
   offers the same?
6. A shop has "second item half price" on 30 dollar shirts. What do two shirts cost? What percent off is that for the
   pair?
7. A 96 dollar coat is 25% off, and then 13% HST is added. What do you pay?
8. Sam says: "30% off and then an extra 20% off means 50% off." Test his claim on a 100 dollar item.

??? note "Answers"
    1. $0.80 \times 35 = 28$ dollars. (20% of 35 is 7, and $35 - 7 = 28$.)
    2. $0.60 \times 65 = 39$ dollars.
    3. $0.85 \times 220 = 187$ dollars.
    4. Discount: $50 - 42 = 8$ dollars. $8 \div 50 = 0.16$, so **16% off**.
    5. On 45 dollars: 20% is 9 dollars off, so **10 dollars off** is better. On 60 dollars: 20% is 12 dollars off,
       so **20% off** is better. They are the same when 20% of the price is 10 dollars, at a price of **50 dollars**
       (10% would be 5, so the whole is 50).
    6. $30 + 15 = 45$ dollars instead of 60. Saving: 15 dollars, and $15 \div 60 = 0.25$, so **25% off** the pair.
    7. Sale price: $0.75 \times 96 = 72$ dollars. With tax: $72 \times 1.13 = 81.36$ dollars.
    8. $100 \times 0.70 = 70$, then $70 \times 0.80 = 56$ dollars. That is 44 dollars off, **44%**, not 50%. The extra
       20% was taken from 70 dollars, not from 100.

## Common mistake

**Taking the discount but forgetting to subtract it.** 30% of 80 is 24, but the sale price is not 24 dollars; that is
the discount. The sale price is $80 - 24 = 56$. Using the multiplier 0.70 avoids this.

**Adding percent discounts together.** Two discounts one after another do not add up, because the second one is a
percent of a smaller price. Multiply the multipliers instead.
