# Percent and percentage points

*Ratio, rates and money · level 6, lesson 3 of 7*

## A question to start

A bank raises its savings interest rate from 4% to 5%. Two newspapers report it.

- Newspaper A: **"Bank raises rate by 1%"**
- Newspaper B: **"Bank raises rate by 25%"**

One of them sounds like a tiny change and the other like a big one. Which is right?

In a way, both are trying to say something true, but newspaper A has used the wrong word. The rate went up by
**1 percentage point**, and that is a rise of **25 percent**. This lesson is about the difference, which matters every
time you read about interest rates, taxes, test scores, polls or health risks.

## The idea

### When the things being compared are already percents

Usually a percent compares an amount with a base: 18 out of 24 is 75%. But some quantities are **already percents**:
an interest rate, a tax rate, a test score, the share of votes a party gets. When a percent changes, there are two ways
to measure the change, exactly as in [level 5](../level-5/03-percent-change.md):

- The **absolute change** is the plain difference, new minus old. When the things being compared are percents, this
  difference is measured in **percentage points** (sometimes "points" for short). From 4% to 5% is
  $5 - 4 = 1$ percentage point.
- The **relative change** compares the difference with the old value, as a percent. From 4% to 5% is
  $\dfrac{5 - 4}{4} = \dfrac14 = 25\%$.

So:

$$4\% \to 5\%: \quad \text{up } 1 \text{ percentage point}, \quad \text{up } 25\%.$$

Newspaper A said "1%" when it meant "1 percentage point". Newspaper B is correct. A careful writer would say "the rate
rose by 1 percentage point, from 4% to 5%", which gives the reader everything.

### Why both are useful

**Percentage points** tell you the change in the actual thing. If you have 10 000 dollars in the bank, a rise from 4%
to 5% earns you 1 percentage point more, which is 100 dollars more a year. That is the number that reaches your
pocket.

**Percent** tells you how big the change is compared with where it started. Your interest goes up from 400 dollars to
500 dollars a year, a 25% rise in your interest. Both numbers are right; they answer different questions.

### Small bases make big percents

When the starting percent is small, a tiny change in points can be a huge change in percent. Suppose a rare side effect
of a medicine happens to 1 person in 10 000 (that is 0.01%), and a new medicine raises it to 2 in 10 000 (0.02%).

- A headline could say: **"New medicine doubles the risk!"** That is true: up 100%.
- But the change is only $0.02 - 0.01 = 0.01$ percentage points: 1 extra person in every 10 000.

In health, the percentage-point change is called the **absolute risk** change, and the percent change is the
**relative risk** change. Both are true. Only reporting the relative one can scare people; only reporting the absolute
one can hide a real danger in a big population. Good reporting gives both. (More on reading data honestly in
[Statistics](../../statistics/index.md).)

### Basis points

Banks talk about very small changes in rates, so they use an even smaller unit: a **basis point** is one hundredth of a
percentage point. A rise from 5.00% to 5.25% is 0.25 percentage points, or **25 basis points**.

### Percents bigger than 100

A percent can be more than 100. The trick is to keep straight "percent **of**" and "percent **more than**".

- **Of**: if this year's sales are 3 times last year's, they are **300% of** last year's.
- **More than**: the increase is 2 times last year's, so they are **200% more than** last year's.

Mars is a good example. Gravity at Mars's surface is about 3.71 m/s² and at Earth's about 9.81 m/s². So

- Mars's gravity is $\dfrac{3.71}{9.81} \approx 0.378$, about **38% of** Earth's;
- Earth's gravity is $\dfrac{9.81}{3.71} \approx 2.64$, about **264% of** Mars's, or **164% more than** Mars's.

Same two numbers, different base, very different-looking percents.

### A percent of a percent

When you take a percent of a group that is itself a percent of a bigger group, multiply. If 40% of a class take French
and 25% **of those** also take Spanish, then $0.40 \times 0.25 = 0.10$: 10% of the **whole class** take both. Not
$40\% - 25\% = 15\%$ and not $25\%$.

## Worked examples

**1. The bank.** A rate goes from 4% to 5%.

- Points: $5 - 4 = 1$ percentage point.
- Percent: $\dfrac{1}{4} = 25\%$ higher.

**2. A test score.** Sam's score goes from 60% to 75%.

- Points: $75 - 60 = 15$ percentage points.
- Percent: $\dfrac{15}{60} = 25\%$ better.

**3. Going down.** The unemployment rate falls from 6.0% to 5.4%.

- Points: $5.4 - 6.0 = -0.6$, down 0.6 percentage points.
- Percent: $\dfrac{-0.6}{6.0} = -0.10$, down 10%. (One in ten of the people who were unemployed now has a job,
  roughly speaking.)

**4. Risk.** A risk goes from 1 in 10 000 to 2 in 10 000.

- Relative: doubled, up 100%.
- Absolute: up 0.01 percentage points.

**5. Gravity.** Mars has about 38% of Earth's gravity. How do you describe Earth compared with Mars?

- $9.81 \div 3.71 \approx 2.64$: Earth's gravity is about **264% of** Mars's, which is about **164% more**.

**6. A percent of a percent.** 40% of a class take French; 25% of those take Spanish too.

- $0.40 \times 0.25 = 0.10$: **10%** of the class take both.

## Try it

1. An interest rate goes from 3% to 4.5%. Describe the change in percentage points and in percent.
2. A politician's approval falls from 52% to 39%. Describe the change both ways.
3. A test score goes from 80% to 88%. How many percentage points up? How many percent up?
4. A company's sales this year are 3 times last year's. Complete: this year's sales are ___% of last year's and
   ___% more than last year's.
5. A new safety rule lowers a risk from 4% to 3%. Give the absolute change and the relative change. Which sounds
   more impressive?
6. 60% of students in a school bike to school, and 30% of the bikers ride an e-bike. What percent of all the students
   ride an e-bike?
7. A loan rate drops from 5.25% to 5.00%. Give the change in percentage points, in basis points, and in percent.
8. A year on Mars is about 687 Earth days; an Earth year is about 365.25 days. A Mars year is about ___% of an Earth
   year, or ___% longer.

??? note "Answers"
    1. Up $4.5 - 3 = 1.5$ percentage points. In percent: $\dfrac{1.5}{3} = 50\%$ higher.
    2. Down $52 - 39 = 13$ percentage points. In percent: $\dfrac{13}{52} = 25\%$ lower.
    3. Up 8 percentage points. In percent: $\dfrac{8}{80} = 10\%$.
    4. **300%** of last year's, and **200%** more than last year's.
    5. Absolute: down 1 percentage point. Relative: $\dfrac{1}{4} = 25\%$ lower. "Risk cut by 25%" sounds more
       impressive than "risk cut by 1 point", though they describe the same change.
    6. $0.60 \times 0.30 = 0.18$, so **18%** of all students.
    7. Down 0.25 percentage points, which is 25 basis points. In percent: $\dfrac{0.25}{5.25} \approx 0.048$, about
       4.8% lower.
    8. $\dfrac{687}{365.25} \approx 1.88$: about **188%** of an Earth year, or about **88%** longer.

## Common mistake

**Saying "percent" when you mean "percentage points".** From 4% to 5% is up 1 percentage point, not up 1%. If you are
not sure, give both numbers and the before and after.

**Subtracting percents of different groups.** "40% take French and 25% of those take Spanish" means
$0.40 \times 0.25 = 10\%$ of the class, because the 25% is a percent of the French group, not of the class.
