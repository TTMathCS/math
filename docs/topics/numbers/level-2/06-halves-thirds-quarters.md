# Halves, thirds and quarters

*Numbers · level 2, lesson 6 of 7*

## A question to start

Two brothers share a sandwich. The older one cuts it "in half", but his cut is not in the middle. He takes the big
piece and says: "I'll have the bigger half!"

Is there such a thing as a **bigger half**? People say it all the time. But a mathematician would say the older
brother did not cut the sandwich in half at all. Why not?

And here is a second puzzle. Would you rather have **one third** of a pizza or **one quarter** of the same pizza? Four
is bigger than three... so is a quarter bigger? In this lesson you will find out what halves, thirds and quarters
really are, and the answers to both puzzles will be easy.

## The idea

### Equal parts

So far, every number in this topic has counted **whole** things: 3 apples, 12 cookies. But often we want **part** of
a thing: some of a sandwich, a piece of a pizza, part of a ribbon. Numbers for parts of a whole are called
**fractions**.

The most important word in this lesson is **equal**. To cut something into halves, thirds or quarters, every piece
must be **the same size**.

- **Halves**: the whole is cut into **2 equal** parts. Each part is **one half**.
- **Thirds**: the whole is cut into **3 equal** parts. Each part is **one third**.
- **Quarters** (also called **fourths**): the whole is cut into **4 equal** parts. Each part is **one quarter**.

The circle and the square are cut into halves: the two parts are exactly the same size. The rectangle is cut into
two parts, but they are **not equal**, so they are not halves.

That answers the sandwich puzzle. If the two pieces are different sizes, they are not halves. A "bigger half" is
impossible, because halves are equal by their very meaning.

**Why must the parts be equal?** Because a fraction is a promise about **how much**. If "a half" could be any piece,
then "half a pizza" might be a tiny sliver or nearly the whole thing, and the words would tell you nothing. Making the
parts equal is what makes "a half" mean one exact amount.

### How to check that parts are equal

- **Fold it.** Fold a paper square along the diagonal. If the two triangles fit exactly on top of each other, they
  are halves.
- **Turn it.** Cut a square from corner to corner. Turn one triangle around: it fits exactly on top of the other.
- **Count it.** If a chocolate bar has 12 little squares, each half must have 6 of them.

to cover any other exactly.)

### Writing fractions

We write one half as **1/2**, or stacked up like this: $\frac{1}{2}$. One third is 1/3, and one quarter is 1/4.

- The **bottom** number says **how many equal parts** the whole is cut into.
- The **top** number says **how many of those parts** we are talking about.

So 1/4 means "the whole is cut into 4 equal parts, and we have 1 of them". The bottom number is called the
**denominator** (it names the size of the parts). The top number is the **numerator** (it counts them).

We can also have more than one part. If a pizza is cut into 4 equal slices and you eat 3, you ate **3/4** (three
quarters). If a cake is cut into 3 equal pieces and two are gone, **2/3** (two thirds) are gone.

Look at the shaded pieces. **1/2 is bigger than 1/3, and 1/3 is bigger than 1/4.** The more pieces you cut the whole
into, the **smaller** each piece is.

That answers the pizza puzzle. A third of a pizza is **more** than a quarter. Think of sharing: if a pizza is shared
among 3 people, each gets more than if the same pizza is shared among 4 people. More people, smaller shares. The 4 in
1/4 is bigger, but it is the number of people sharing, not the size of the piece.

The strips show more:

- 2 halves make 1 whole. 3 thirds make 1 whole. 4 quarters make 1 whole.
- 2 quarters are the same length as 1 half. So **2/4 is the same amount as 1/2**.

### A fraction of a group

Fractions also work for a **group** of things. What is **half of 8 cookies**?

Half means 2 equal parts. So split the 8 cookies into 2 equal groups: share them between 2 people, as in
[lesson 5](05-fair-sharing.md). Each group has 4. So **half of 8 is 4**. Finding a fraction of a group is dividing:

- half of 8 is 8 ÷ 2 = **4**;
- a third of 12 is 12 ÷ 3 = **4**;
- a quarter of 12 is 12 ÷ 4 = **3**.

And for more than one part, take more than one group: three quarters of 12 is 3 groups of 3, which is **9**.

- Cut the stretch from 0 to 1 into **2** equal steps. The first step ends at **1/2**, exactly halfway.
- Cut it into **3** equal steps. The first step ends at **1/3**.
- Cut it into **4** equal steps. The first step ends at **1/4**.

A fraction with 1 on top, like 1/2, 1/3 or 1/4, is called a **unit fraction**: it is one single part. On the number
line, you can see again that 1/4 is closer to 0 than 1/3, and 1/3 is closer to 0 than 1/2. So 1/4 < 1/3 < 1/2.

## Worked examples

**1. Is it halves?** A rectangle is cut into two pieces. One piece is 5 squares and the other is 3 squares. Is it cut
into halves?

**No.** The pieces must be equal. Halves of 8 squares would have 4 squares each.

**2. Naming the fraction.** A ribbon is cut into 3 equal pieces. Ana takes 1 piece. What fraction of the ribbon does
she have?

3 equal pieces, 1 taken: **1/3**, one third.

**3. A fraction of a group.** What is a quarter of 20 stickers?

Quarter means 4 equal parts, so share 20 into 4 groups: 20 ÷ 4 = **5**. Check: 4 groups of 5 is 20. ✓

**4. Comparing.** Which is more: half of a chocolate bar, or a third of the same bar?

**Half.** Cutting into 2 equal pieces makes bigger pieces than cutting into 3.

**5. Two thirds of a group.** What is two thirds of 15 marbles?

One third of 15 is 15 ÷ 3 = 5. Two thirds is two of those groups: 5 + 5 = **10**.

## Try it

1. A pizza is cut into 4 equal slices. What is each slice called? How many slices make the whole pizza?
2. Draw a rectangle and cut it into thirds. How can you check the parts are equal?
3. What is half of 14? A third of 9? A quarter of 16?
4. Put in order from smallest to biggest: 1/3, 1/2, 1/4.
5. Tim says "I cut the cake into quarters" but he cut it into 4 pieces of different sizes. What would you tell him?
6. A day on Mars, a sol, is a little longer than an Earth day. If a rover sleeps for half a sol and drives for a
   quarter of a sol, is it sleeping or driving for longer?
7. What is three quarters of 8?
8. Is 2/4 of a pizza more, less or the same as 1/2 of the same pizza? Use the fraction strips to explain.

??? note "Answers"
    1. Each slice is **one quarter** (1/4). **4** slices (4 quarters) make the whole.
    2. Draw lines so that 3 parts are equal: for example, two lines going down, cutting the rectangle into 3 strips of
       equal width. Check by measuring each strip's width with a ruler, or by folding the paper in three.
    3. Half of 14: 14 ÷ 2 = **7**. A third of 9: 9 ÷ 3 = **3**. A quarter of 16: 16 ÷ 4 = **4**.
    4. **1/4, 1/3, 1/2**. More pieces means smaller pieces.
    5. They are not quarters. Quarters must be **4 equal** pieces. Unequal pieces are just "4 pieces".
    6. **Sleeping.** A half is bigger than a quarter (in fact, a half is two quarters, so it sleeps twice as long as it
       drives).
    7. A quarter of 8 is 8 ÷ 4 = 2. Three quarters is 3 groups of 2: **6**.
    8. **The same.** On the fraction strips, two quarter pieces put end to end are exactly as long as one half piece.

## Common mistake

**"4 is bigger than 3, so a quarter is bigger than a third."** The bottom number counts how many pieces the whole is
cut into. More pieces means **smaller** pieces. So 1/4 is smaller than 1/3.

**Unequal parts.** Cutting something into 4 pieces does not make quarters unless the pieces are **equal**. Always
check by folding, turning or counting.
