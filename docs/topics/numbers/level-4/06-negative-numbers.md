# Negative numbers

*Numbers · level 4, lesson 6 of 7*

## A question to start

What is $5 - 8$?

For a long time, many people said: "You can't take 8 from 5. It is impossible." Even some mathematicians a few
hundred years ago called answers like this "absurd". But a thermometer has no trouble with it. If it is 5 °C and the
temperature drops by 8 degrees, it is now **−3 °C**: three degrees below zero.

And here is a stranger one: what is $3 - (-2)$? Taking away a negative? By the end of this lesson you will know the
answer (it is 5) and, more importantly, **why**.

## The idea

### Numbers below zero

Some things can go below a starting point:

- **Temperature**: −10 °C is ten degrees below freezing. The average temperature on Mars is about −60 °C.
- **Height**: the shore of the Dead Sea is more than 400 m **below** sea level.
- **Floors**: in many buildings, the floor below the ground floor is floor −1.
- **Money**: if you owe 20 dollars, your balance is −20 dollars.

Each of these has a **zero** that is a starting point, not "nothing": 0 °C is when water freezes, sea level is a
chosen height. Numbers above it are **positive**, numbers below it are **negative**. We write a negative number with
a minus sign in front: −3, read "negative three". Positive numbers can be written with a plus sign (+3) but usually
are not. Zero is neither positive nor negative.

The whole numbers together with their negatives, ..., −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ..., are called the **integers**.

Extend the number line to the left of 0. The negative numbers are a mirror image of the positive ones.

**Opposites.** 4 and −4 are the same distance from 0 (4 steps) but on opposite sides. They are called
**opposites**. The opposite of 7 is −7; the opposite of −7 is 7; the opposite of 0 is 0. Writing a minus sign in
front of a number means "the opposite of", so $-(-7) = 7$: the opposite of the opposite takes you back where you
started, like turning around twice.

The distance of a number from 0 is called its **absolute value**, written with two bars: $|-4| = 4$ and $|4| = 4$.

### Comparing: further left is smaller

On the number line, numbers get **bigger to the right** and **smaller to the left**. So:

$$-7 < -3$$

even though 7 is bigger than 3. Think of temperature: −7 °C is colder than −3 °C. Or money: owing 7 dollars is worse
than owing 3 dollars. Among negative numbers, the one that is **further from zero** is the **smaller** one.

Every negative number is smaller than 0, and every positive number is bigger than every negative number:
$-1000 < -1 < 0 < 1$.

### Adding with chips

Here is a model that makes adding and subtracting integers easy to see. Use two kinds of chips:

- an orange chip (+) stands for **+1**,
- a blue chip (−) stands for **−1**.

One orange and one blue chip together make **0**: +1 and −1 cancel out. A pair like this is called a **zero pair**.
You can add or remove zero pairs whenever you like, because adding 0 changes nothing.

**Adding means putting chips together.**

- $5 + (-3)$: put 5 orange and 3 blue together. Make 3 zero pairs and remove them. 2 orange are left: **2**.
- $-4 + (-2)$: 4 blue and 2 blue make 6 blue: **−6**.
- $-5 + 3$: 5 blue and 3 orange. 3 zero pairs cancel, and 2 blue are left: **−2**.

So: adding two negatives gives a bigger negative. Adding numbers with different signs, the chips cancel, and whichever
kind has more chips "wins", by the difference.

### Subtracting with chips

**Subtracting means taking chips away.**

$6 - 2$: start with 6 orange, take away 2 orange, 4 are left. Nothing new.

$2 - 5$: start with 2 orange. We need to take away 5 orange, but there are only 2. So **add zero pairs** until there
are enough. Add 3 zero pairs: now there are 5 orange and 3 blue, which is still worth 2. Take away the 5 orange. Left:
3 blue, so $2 - 5 = -3$.

$3 - (-2)$: start with 3 orange. Take away 2 **blue**, but there are none. Add 2 zero pairs: now 5 orange and 2 blue
(still worth 3). Take away the 2 blue. Left: 5 orange. So $3 - (-2) = 5$.

Look at what happened. To take away 2 blue chips, we had to bring in 2 zero pairs, which also brought in 2 orange
chips. Taking away the blue left those 2 extra orange behind. So **taking away −2 has the same effect as adding +2**:

$$3 - (-2) = 3 + 2 = 5$$

The same thing happens with $2 - 5$: taking away 5 orange leaves 5 extra blue behind, so it is the same as adding
−5. In general:

$$a - b = a + (\text{the opposite of } b)$$

**Subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite.** It makes sense in real life too: if someone takes away a
debt of 2 dollars that you owed, you are 2 dollars better off.

### On the number line

Think of adding as **moving**:

- adding a positive number moves you **right**;
- adding a negative number moves you **left**;
- subtracting a number moves you the **opposite way** from adding it.

**Subtracting as a difference.** $a - b$ also answers "how far is it from $b$ up to $a$?" For $-3 - (-7)$: from −7
up to −3 is 4 steps to the right, so $-3 - (-7) = 4$. Check with the rule: $-3 + 7 = 4$. ✓ This is how we find the
change in temperature: from −7 °C to −3 °C is a rise of 4 degrees.

## Worked examples

**1. Compare.** Order from smallest: 2, −5, 0, −1, −12. On the number line from left to right: **−12, −5, −1, 0, 2**.

**2. Add.** $8 + (-12)$. 8 orange and 12 blue: 8 zero pairs cancel, 4 blue left. **−4**.

**3. Add.** $-15 + 7$. 7 zero pairs cancel; 8 blue left. **−8**.

**4. Subtract.** $-6 - 4$. Add the opposite: $-6 + (-4) = -10$. On the line: start at −6, move 4 left.

**5. Subtract a negative.** $-2 - (-9)$. Add the opposite: $-2 + 9 = 7$.

**6. Temperature on Mars.** Suppose a rover's thermometer reads −78 °C at dawn and −19 °C in the afternoon. How much
did it warm up? The change is $-19 - (-78) = -19 + 78 = 59$ degrees. Check on the line: from −78 up to 0 is 78, but
we stop 19 short of 0, so $78 - 19 = 59$. ✓ Big daily swings like this are normal on Mars, because the thin air holds
very little heat.

## Try it

1. Write the opposite of: (a) 9 (b) −15 (c) 0. What is $-(-6)$?
2. Use $<$ or $>$: (a) −8 ☐ −2 (b) −1 ☐ −10 (c) −3 ☐ 1.
3. Use chips to work out: (a) $4 + (-6)$ (b) $-3 + (-5)$ (c) $-7 + 7$.
4. Use chips (add zero pairs) to work out: (a) $1 - 4$ (b) $5 - (-3)$ (c) $-2 - (-6)$.
5. Work out using "add the opposite": (a) $-9 - 3$ (b) $10 - (-10)$ (c) $-4 - (-1)$.
6. A diver is at −18 m (18 m below the surface) and rises 7 m. Where is she now? Then she dives 12 m. Where now?
7. The temperature at midnight was −63 °C and at noon −18 °C. What was the rise?
8. Harder: find two integers whose sum is −2 and whose difference (bigger minus smaller) is 10.

??? note "Answers"
    1. (a) −9 (b) 15 (c) 0. $-(-6) = 6$.
    2. (a) $-8 < -2$ (b) $-1 > -10$ (c) $-3 < 1$.
    3. (a) 4 zero pairs cancel, 2 blue left: **−2**. (b) 8 blue: **−8**. (c) 7 zero pairs: **0**.
    4. (a) 1 orange; add 3 zero pairs (4 orange, 3 blue); take away 4 orange; left 3 blue: **−3**.
       (b) 5 orange; add 3 zero pairs; take away 3 blue; left 8 orange: **8**.
       (c) 2 blue; add 4 zero pairs (6 blue, 4 orange); take away 6 blue; left 4 orange: **4**.
    5. (a) $-9 + (-3) = -12$ (b) $10 + 10 = 20$ (c) $-4 + 1 = -3$.
    6. $-18 + 7 = -11$, so 11 m below the surface. Then $-11 - 12 = -23$: 23 m below.
    7. $-18 - (-63) = -18 + 63 = 45$ degrees.
    8. The two numbers are 10 apart and their middle is $-2 \div 2 = -1$. So they are 5 either side of −1: **4 and
       −6**. Check: $4 + (-6) = -2$ ✓ and $4 - (-6) = 10$ ✓.

## Common mistake

**Thinking −7 is bigger than −3 "because 7 is bigger than 3".** On the number line, −7 is further **left**, so it is
smaller. Ask: which is colder?

**Two minus signs in a row.** In $5 - (-3)$, the first sign means "subtract" and the second means "negative". Together
they make **adding 3**: the answer is 8, not 2. Rewrite as "add the opposite" before calculating.
