# Level 7: Building number systems

*Numbers · level 7 of 8 · rough age 15-16 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

Every family of numbers you know was invented to answer a question the older family could not. This level looks back
at that ladder (counting numbers, integers, fractions, real numbers) and then takes the last step of school
mathematics: a number $i$ whose square is $-1$. You will calculate with complex numbers, draw them as points on a
plane, use them to solve every quadratic equation, and finish with a careful look at distance on the real line:
intervals, absolute value and the triangle inequality.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Why each family of numbers was invented](01-why-new-numbers.md) | Explain which equation forced each new family, what "closed under an operation" means, and why $x^2 = -1$ needs something new |
| [The number i](02-the-number-i.md) | Use $i^2 = -1$, find any power of $i$ from the cycle of four, write square roots of negative numbers, and tell the cubic-equation story honestly |
| [Complex numbers a + bi](03-complex-arithmetic.md) | Add, subtract, multiply and divide complex numbers, and use the conjugate and its rules |
| [The complex plane](04-complex-plane.md) | Plot complex numbers, add them as arrows, find the modulus and the distance between two numbers, and see multiplying by $i$ as a quarter turn |
| [Every quadratic has roots](05-every-quadratic.md) | Solve any quadratic with real coefficients, explain why the roots come in conjugate pairs, and read the discriminant in the complex world |
| [Intervals and absolute value](06-intervals-absolute-value.md) | Write sets of numbers in interval notation, solve absolute value equations and inequalities as distance problems, and prove the triangle inequality |
