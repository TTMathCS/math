# Level 8: Complex numbers and beyond

*Numbers · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 7 you met the complex numbers as pairs $a + bi$ and as points on a plane. This level gives them their second
face: a length and an angle. With it, multiplying becomes "multiply the lengths, add the angles", powers and roots
become easy, and the five most famous numbers in mathematics meet in one line, $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$. The level ends at
the edge of school mathematics: the theorem that the complex numbers are enough for every polynomial, and the strange
four-dimensional numbers that come after them.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Polar form](01-polar-form.md) | Find the modulus and argument of any complex number, switch between $a + bi$ and $r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)$, and prove that multiplying multiplies lengths and adds angles |
| [De Moivre's theorem](02-de-moivre.md) | Raise complex numbers to high powers by hand, prove the theorem by induction, and derive the double- and triple-angle formulas |
| [Roots of unity](03-roots-of-unity.md) | Solve $z^n = 1$ and $z^n = w$, draw the roots as a regular polygon, and prove that the roots of unity add to zero |
| [Euler's formula](04-euler-formula.md) | Explain where $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ comes from using power series, use it to calculate, and say what $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$ does and does not mean |
| [The fundamental theorem of algebra](05-fundamental-theorem.md) | State the theorem, count roots with multiplicity, factor polynomials such as $x^4 + 1$ completely, and explain why real polynomials split into linear and quadratic factors |
| [Beyond the complex numbers](06-quaternions.md) | Calculate with quaternions, use them to rotate a point in space, and explain what is lost at each step up the ladder of number systems |
