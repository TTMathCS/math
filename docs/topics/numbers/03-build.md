# Build: Fractions, decimals and percentages

*Numbers · step 3 of 5*

## A question to start

Three friends share two pizzas equally. How much does each person get?

Not a whole pizza: there is not enough. Not half: that would only use one and a half pizzas. The answer is **two
thirds** of a pizza each, written $\tfrac23$. Whole numbers cannot answer this question. Fractions can, and they
are the first big new family of numbers you meet after the counting numbers.

## The idea

### What a fraction means

A **fraction** like $\tfrac23$ has two parts:

- the bottom number, the **denominator**, says how many equal pieces the whole is cut into (3);
- the top number, the **numerator**, says how many of those pieces you have (2).

There is a second meaning, just as important: **a fraction is a division**. $\tfrac23$ is $2 \div 3$, which is
exactly the pizza question: 2 pizzas shared among 3 people. Keep both meanings in mind; each one makes some
problems easy.

### Equivalent fractions

Cut each of the 3 pieces of a pizza in half. Now there are 6 pieces, and the 2 you had have become 4. You still have
the same amount of pizza, so

$$\frac23 = \frac46 = \frac69 = \frac{8}{12} = \cdots$$

Fractions that look different but are the same amount are **equivalent**. The rule: **multiply or divide the top
and the bottom by the same number** (not zero), and the fraction stays the same amount.

Dividing top and bottom by a common factor is called **simplifying**. To simplify $\tfrac{18}{24}$, notice that 6
goes into both: $\tfrac{18}{24} = \tfrac{18 \div 6}{24 \div 6} = \tfrac34$. A fraction is in **lowest terms** when
no whole number bigger than 1 divides both the top and the bottom.

### Comparing and adding

You can only add pieces of the **same size**. Half a pizza plus a third of a pizza is not "two fifths"; the pieces
are different sizes. First cut both into pieces of the same size, called a **common denominator**:

$$\frac12 + \frac13 = \frac36 + \frac26 = \frac56$$

Sixths work because 6 is a multiple of both 2 and 3. The smallest such number (the **least common multiple**) keeps
the numbers small, but any common multiple gives the right answer after simplifying.

Taking away works the same way: $\tfrac56 - \tfrac14 = \tfrac{10}{12} - \tfrac{3}{12} = \tfrac{7}{12}$.

Common denominators also let you **compare**. Is $\tfrac58$ bigger than $\tfrac23$? Use 24ths:
$\tfrac58 = \tfrac{15}{24}$ and $\tfrac23 = \tfrac{16}{24}$, so $\tfrac23$ is a tiny bit bigger.

### Multiplying

Multiplying is easier than adding: **multiply the tops, multiply the bottoms**.

$$\frac34 \times \frac25 = \frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 5} = \frac{6}{20} = \frac{3}{10}$$

Why? "$\tfrac34$ of $\tfrac25$" means: take two fifths of a chocolate bar, then take three quarters of that. Cutting
fifths into quarters makes twentieths, and you end up with 3 × 2 = 6 of them. The word **of** between fractions
means multiply.

### Dividing

To divide by a fraction, **flip it and multiply**. Flipping a fraction (top and bottom swap places) gives its
**reciprocal**: the reciprocal of $\tfrac18$ is $\tfrac81 = 8$.

$$\frac34 \div \frac18 = \frac34 \times \frac81 = \frac{24}{4} = 6$$

Why does flipping work? $\tfrac34 \div \tfrac18$ asks: *how many eighths fit into three quarters?* Each whole holds
8 eighths, so three quarters of a whole holds $\tfrac34 \times 8 = 6$ eighths. Dividing by $\tfrac18$ is the same
as multiplying by 8.

### Fractions, decimals and percentages are the same numbers

A fraction is a division, so you can turn any fraction into a decimal by dividing:

- $\tfrac58 = 5 \div 8 = 0.625$
- $\tfrac14 = 0.25$, $\tfrac12 = 0.5$, $\tfrac34 = 0.75$
- $\tfrac13 = 0.333\ldots$ The 3s go on forever. We call this a **repeating decimal** and write $0.\overline{3}$.

**Percent** means "out of a hundred". 25% is $\tfrac{25}{100} = \tfrac14 = 0.25$. To turn a decimal into a percent,
multiply by 100: $0.625 = 62.5\%$.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\tfrac12$ | 0.5 | 50% |
| $\tfrac14$ | 0.25 | 25% |
| $\tfrac15$ | 0.2 | 20% |
| $\tfrac{1}{10}$ | 0.1 | 10% |
| $\tfrac38$ | 0.375 | 37.5% |
| $\tfrac13$ | $0.\overline{3}$ | about 33.3% |

## Worked examples

**1. Add $\tfrac23 + \tfrac14$.**

1. The pieces are thirds and quarters, so find a common denominator. 12 is a multiple of 3 and of 4.
2. $\tfrac23 = \tfrac{8}{12}$ (top and bottom times 4). $\tfrac14 = \tfrac{3}{12}$ (top and bottom times 3).
3. Add the twelfths: $\tfrac{8}{12} + \tfrac{3}{12} = \tfrac{11}{12}$.
4. 11 and 12 have no common factor, so $\tfrac{11}{12}$ is in lowest terms.

**2. A recipe needs $\tfrac23$ of a cup of flour. You only have a $\tfrac19$ cup scoop. How many scoops?**

1. The question is "how many ninths fit into two thirds?", which is $\tfrac23 \div \tfrac19$.
2. Flip and multiply: $\tfrac23 \times \tfrac91 = \tfrac{18}{3} = 6$.
3. **Six scoops.** Check: 6 ninths is $\tfrac69 = \tfrac23$. ✓

**3. Lifting things on Mars.** Gravity on Mars pulls about 38% as hard as on Earth. A box of tools that would feel
like 64 kg on Earth: what does it feel like on Mars?

1. 38% is $0.38$, so the box feels like $0.38 \times 64 = 24.32$ kg.
2. A handy fraction for 38% is $\tfrac38 = 37.5\%$, very close. $\tfrac38$ of 64 is $64 \div 8 \times 3 = 24$ kg.
3. So the box feels like about **24 kg**: heavy, but liftable. The *amount of stuff* in the box (its mass) has not
   changed; only the pull of gravity on it has.

## Try it

1. Simplify $\tfrac{24}{36}$ to lowest terms.
2. Which is bigger, $\tfrac35$ or $\tfrac47$?
3. Work out $\tfrac56 - \tfrac13$.
4. Work out $\tfrac23 \times \tfrac{9}{10}$ and $\tfrac23 \div \tfrac49$.
5. Write $\tfrac{7}{20}$ as a decimal and as a percent.
6. A rover drove $\tfrac12$ of its planned route on Monday, $\tfrac13$ on Tuesday and $\tfrac16$ on Wednesday. Did
   it finish?

??? note "Answers"
    1. 12 divides both: $\tfrac{24 \div 12}{36 \div 12} = \tfrac23$.
    2. Use 35ths: $\tfrac35 = \tfrac{21}{35}$ and $\tfrac47 = \tfrac{20}{35}$. So $\tfrac35$ is bigger, by
       $\tfrac{1}{35}$.
    3. $\tfrac13 = \tfrac26$, so $\tfrac56 - \tfrac26 = \tfrac36 = \tfrac12$.
    4. $\tfrac23 \times \tfrac{9}{10} = \tfrac{18}{30} = \tfrac35$. And
       $\tfrac23 \div \tfrac49 = \tfrac23 \times \tfrac94 = \tfrac{18}{12} = \tfrac32$, which is $1\tfrac12$.
    5. $\tfrac{7}{20} = \tfrac{35}{100} = 0.35 = 35\%$. (Top and bottom times 5 makes hundredths.)
    6. Yes, exactly. In sixths: $\tfrac36 + \tfrac26 + \tfrac16 = \tfrac66 = 1$, the whole route.

## Common mistake

**Adding the tops and adding the bottoms.** $\tfrac12 + \tfrac13$ is **not** $\tfrac25$. Check with a picture:
half a pizza plus a third of a pizza is more than half a pizza, but $\tfrac25$ is less than half. Only add the tops
once the bottoms are the same. (Multiplying tops and bottoms is right for **multiplying**, which is why the two get
mixed up.)
