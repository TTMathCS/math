# Level 3: Chances as numbers

*Probability · level 3 of 8 · rough age 9-10 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 2 you described chances in words: unlikely, even chance, likely. In this level chances become **numbers**
from 0 to 1. You learn to list every possible outcome, to count the ones you want, and to turn the count into a
fraction. Then you test the numbers against real experiments, and find out when the fraction rule works and when it
does not.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Outcomes and events](01-outcomes-events.md) | List the sample space of an experiment and write any event as a group of outcomes |
| [Probability as a fraction](02-probability-fraction.md) | Find a probability as favourable outcomes over all outcomes, place it on the scale from 0 to 1, and compare chances |
| [The chance of not happening](03-not-happening.md) | Use $P(\text{not } A) = 1 - P(A)$, and say the opposite of an event correctly |
| [Two dice: 36 outcomes](04-two-dice.md) | Use the 6-by-6 table to find the chance of any total, and explain why 7 is the most likely |
| [Experimental and theoretical probability](05-experiment-theory.md) | Work out relative frequencies, and explain in plain words why they settle down as the number of tries grows |
| [Equally likely or not](06-equally-likely.md) | Tell when outcomes are equally likely, and find probabilities by splitting, measuring or experimenting when they are not |
