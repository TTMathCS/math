# Level 1: Maybe, sure or never

*Probability · level 1 of 8 · rough age 5-6 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

Probability is the math of chance: of things that might happen and might not. It starts with three small words:
**sure**, **maybe** and **never**. In this level you sort things into those three piles, find out why one colour comes
out of a bag more often than another, choose who goes first in a way that is fair to everyone, build your own
spinner, and flip coins and roll dice to see what chance really looks like. Every lesson has something to do with
your hands, and answers to check.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Sure, maybe, never](01-sure-maybe-never.md) | Sort things that could happen into sure, maybe and never, and say why each one goes where it goes |
| [More likely and less likely](02-more-or-less-likely.md) | Look inside a bag of blocks and say which colour is more likely to come out, and why |
| [Taking turns fairly](03-taking-turns-fairly.md) | Tell a fair way of choosing from an unfair one, and use coins, straws and rock-paper-scissors fairly |
| [Spinners](04-spinners.md) | Say where a spinner will land more often, and make a fair spinner of your own |
| [The coin flip experiment](05-coin-flip-experiment.md) | Flip a coin 20 times, keep a tally, and explain why 10 heads and 10 tails is not what usually happens |
| [Rolling a die](06-rolling-a-die.md) | Explain why each face of a die is just as likely, and tell which of two dice games wins more often |
