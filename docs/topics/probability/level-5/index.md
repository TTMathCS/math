# Level 5: Probability rules

*Probability · level 5 of 8 · rough age 12-13 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 4 you found chances by listing every outcome in a table or a tree. That works until the list gets too long.
In this level you learn the **rules** that let you combine chances without listing everything: when to add, when to
multiply, how to handle overlaps, and the trick of working out the opposite. Each rule comes with a reason, so you know
**when** it is allowed. The level ends with the most famous wrong idea about chance, and what independence really
means.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Mutually exclusive events and "or"](01-mutually-exclusive-or.md) | Tell whether two events can happen together, and add their chances when they cannot |
| [The general addition rule](02-general-addition-rule.md) | Find P(A or B) for any two events by taking away the overlap, using Venn diagrams and tables |
| [The multiplication rule](03-multiplication-rule.md) | Find P(A and B) for independent events, and for draws with and without replacement |
| ["At least one"](04-at-least-one.md) | Use the complement to find the chance of at least one success, and solve de Méré's dice puzzle |
| [Trees with unequal branches](05-unequal-branch-trees.md) | Draw and read a tree where the chances change from stage to stage, and check it adds to 1 |
| [The gambler's fallacy and the hot hand](06-gamblers-fallacy.md) | Explain why chance has no memory, why long streaks are normal, and when the past really does matter |
