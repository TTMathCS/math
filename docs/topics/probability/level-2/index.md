# Level 2: Fair or unfair?

*Probability · level 2 of 8 · rough age 7-8 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 1 you sorted things into sure, maybe and never, and found that more ways means more likely. In this level
the maybe pile opens up into a whole **chance line**, and you become a judge of games: you decide whether a game is
fair, and fix it when it is not. You play detective with a hidden bag, race horses with two dice, find out why a coin
cannot remember, and learn to make predictions that are close without being exact.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [The chance line](01-the-chance-line.md) | Place events on a line from impossible to certain, and explain why one event goes further along than another |
| [Fair games](02-fair-games.md) | Decide whether a game is fair by counting every way it can turn out, and fix an unfair game in more than one way |
| [Experiments and tallies](03-experiments-and-tallies.md) | Use many draws from a hidden bag to make a good guess about what is inside, and say how sure the guess is |
| [The dice sum race](04-dice-sum-race.md) | Find all 36 ways two dice can land, and explain why horse 7 wins the race more often than any other |
| [Chance has no memory](05-chance-no-memory.md) | Explain why the next flip after five heads is still an even chance, and when the past really does change the chances |
| [Making predictions](06-making-predictions.md) | Predict about how many times something will happen in many tries, and say how close the real answer is likely to be |
