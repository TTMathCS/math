# Level 4: Two things at once

*Probability · level 4 of 8 · rough age 11-12 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 3 you found chances for one experiment at a time. In this level two or more things happen together: a coin
and a die, two spinners, three flips in a row. Tables and tree diagrams show every outcome, and the multiplication
rule for independent events explains why the numbers come out as they do. When a problem is too hard to work out, you
simulate it; and you learn what an expected count does, and does not, promise.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Compound events with tables](01-compound-tables.md) | List the outcomes of two experiments in a table, explain why there are $m \times n$, and read "and" and "or" events from it |
| [Tree diagrams](02-tree-diagrams.md) | Draw a tree for several stages, multiply along paths and add across them, and show that P(exactly two heads in three flips) is 3/8 |
| [Fractions, decimals and percents](03-fraction-decimal-percent.md) | Change a probability between the three forms, compare chances, and explain what "40% chance of rain" means |
| [Independent events](04-independent-events.md) | Decide whether two events are independent, and use $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$ with a reason why it works |
| [Simulations](05-simulations.md) | Design a fair simulation with dice, coins or random digits, run it, and check it against an exact answer |
| [Expected counts](06-expected-counts.md) | Find an expected count $n \times p$, describe how far real counts usually land from it, and spot a result too odd to be chance |
