# Level 8: Continuous chance and Bayes

*Probability · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

The last level reaches first-year university probability. It starts with Bayes' theorem, the rule for learning from
evidence, and the reason most positive results for a rare disease are false alarms. Then chance becomes continuous:
probability as area under a curve, the normal distribution and its use for counts, and the two great theorems that
explain why averages settle down and why bell curves are everywhere. It ends with random processes that unfold step by
step, and with using random numbers to compute things that are not random at all.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Bayes' theorem](01-bayes-theorem.md) | Reverse a conditional probability with natural frequencies, a tree and the formula, and explain the base-rate fallacy |
| [Continuous random variables](02-continuous-random-variables.md) | Find probabilities as areas under a density, explain why $P(X = 3) = 0$, and use the uniform and exponential distributions |
| [The normal distribution](03-normal-distribution.md) | Use z-scores, tables or a calculator to find normal probabilities and work backwards from a probability to a value |
| [The normal approximation to the binomial](04-normal-approximation.md) | Approximate binomial probabilities with a normal curve and the continuity correction, and say when it works |
| [The law of large numbers and the central limit theorem](05-large-numbers-clt.md) | Prove Chebyshev's inequality and the law of large numbers, and use the CLT for sums and averages |
| [Random walks and Markov chains](06-random-walks-markov.md) | Analyse a random walk, solve the gambler's ruin by first-step analysis, and find the long-run behaviour of a Markov chain |
| [Monte Carlo methods](07-monte-carlo.md) | Estimate $\pi$, areas and integrals with random numbers, judge their accuracy, and prove Buffon's needle result $\tfrac{2}{\pi}$ |
