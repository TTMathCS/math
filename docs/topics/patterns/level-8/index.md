# Level 8: Recursion and induction

*Patterns and sequences · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 7 you found formulas for sums and sequences and checked them on many cases. In this level you **prove** them
for every whole number at once, with proof by induction, and learn its stronger form. Then you solve recurrences
exactly, deriving Binet's formula for the Fibonacci numbers, and watch what happens when a function is applied again
and again. The topic ends at the edge of what is known: a pattern so simple a child can follow it, which nobody has
ever proved.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Proof by induction](01-proof-by-induction.md) | Explain why induction is a valid proof, and write induction proofs for sums such as $1 + 3 + \cdots + (2n - 1) = n^2$ and the sum of the first $n$ cubes |
| [More induction](02-more-induction.md) | Prove divisibility facts and inequalities by induction, choose the right base case, and find the flaw in the "all horses are the same colour" proof |
| [Strong induction](03-strong-induction.md) | Use strong induction with enough base cases, to prove that every number from 2 up is a product of primes and that $F_n < 2^n$ |
| [Solving recurrences](04-solving-recurrences.md) | Solve rules like $a_n = 5a_{n-1} - 6a_{n-2}$ with the characteristic equation, and derive Binet's formula |
| [Iteration and fixed points](05-iteration-fixed-points.md) | Draw cobweb diagrams, find fixed points, and use the slope to tell when an iteration settles, runs away or cycles |
| [Unsolved patterns](06-unsolved-patterns.md) | Explore the Collatz $3n + 1$ problem, explain why huge computer checks are not a proof, and recognise patterns that broke |
