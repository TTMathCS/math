# Level 7: Series and sigma

*Patterns and sequences · level 7 of 8 · rough age 15-16 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

In level 6 you added up a geometric sequence with one clever subtraction. In this level adding up becomes the main
subject. You learn the sigma shorthand for long sums, derive the arithmetic series formula carefully, and add
**infinitely** many numbers when the terms shrink fast enough. Then you meet the golden ratio hiding in the Fibonacci
numbers, prove formulas for sums of squares and cubes, and finish by seeing every sequence as a function whose graph is
a row of dots on a line, a parabola or an exponential curve.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [Sigma notation](01-sigma-notation.md) | Read and write sums with $\Sigma$, count their terms, change the starting index, and split and simplify sums |
| [Arithmetic series, carefully](02-arithmetic-series.md) | Derive $S_n = \frac{n(a + l)}{2}$, and add terms that meet a condition, such as the multiples of 7 between 100 and 500 |
| [Infinite geometric series](03-infinite-geometric.md) | Explain why $a + ar + ar^2 + \cdots = \frac{a}{1 - r}$ when $\lvert r \rvert < 1$, spot when there is no sum, and find a bouncing ball's total distance |
| [Recursion and the golden ratio](04-golden-ratio.md) | Show why Fibonacci ratios approach $\varphi = \frac{1 + \sqrt5}{2}$ from $x^2 = x + 1$, and use and check Binet's formula |
| [Sums of squares and cubes](05-squares-cubes.md) | Find and prove $\sum k^2 = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$ and $\sum k^3 = \left(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)^2$, and use them |
| [Sequences as functions](06-sequences-functions.md) | Treat a sequence as a discrete function, link arithmetic, quadratic and geometric sequences to lines, parabolas and exponentials, and describe convergence |
