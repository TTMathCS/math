# Patterns you can hear and move

*Patterns and sequences · level 1, lesson 2 of 6*

## A question to start

Listen to a drum. It goes:

**boom**, tap, tap, **boom**, tap, tap, **boom**, tap, tap...

Can you turn that sound into a row of beads? And can you turn it into a dance with your body?

Here is the surprising part. The drum, the beads and the dance can all be **the same pattern**, even though one is a
sound, one is a thing you can hold and one is a way of moving. In this lesson you will find out what "the same
pattern" really means.

## The idea

### Patterns you can hear

A pattern does not have to be something you can see. You can make a pattern with **sounds**.

Try this. Clap your hands, then stamp your foot twice:

> clap, stamp, stamp, clap, stamp, stamp, clap, stamp, stamp

What is the core? The part that repeats is *clap, stamp, stamp*. It is 3 sounds long.

Sound patterns are everywhere. A clock goes *tick, tock, tick, tock*: that is an AB pattern. A song often has a part
that comes back again and again (grown-ups call it the **chorus**). A skipping rhyme has a beat that goes on and on,
and your feet land in time with it.

### Patterns you can move

You can also make a pattern with your **body**. Try this:

> jump, turn, turn, jump, turn, turn, jump, turn, turn

Many dances and games are movement patterns. When you skip, your feet go *hop-step, hop-step*. When you swim, your arms
go *left, right, left, right*. When you march, you go *left, right, left, right*. A grown-up may count
"one, two, one, two" to help everyone stay together.

- The sounds go *clap, stamp, stamp*.
- The beads go *orange, blue, blue*.
- The moves go *jump, turn, turn*.

They look and sound very different. But in every row, the first thing in the core is **one kind**, and the next two
are **another kind, twice**. So we can give all three the same letter name: **ABB**.

That is what "the same pattern" means. The pattern is not the claps or the beads or the jumps. The pattern is the
**rule**: *one of the first kind, then two of the second kind, and repeat*. You can dress that rule up in sounds, in
colours, in shapes or in moves. It is like a person wearing different clothes. The clothes change, but it is the same
person underneath.

A good way to check is to write the letters:

1. Call the first thing **A**.
2. Each time something new comes, give it the next letter: **B**, then **C**.
3. Each time something you have seen before comes again, use its old letter.

If two patterns get the same letters, they are the same pattern.

### Why the letters work

Why can we swap claps for beads and still have the same pattern? Because the rule only cares about two things:

- **How long the core is.** Here, 3.
- **Where the repeats are inside the core.** Here, the 2nd and 3rd are the same, and the 1st is different.

The rule never says "it must be a clap" or "it must be blue". So you can pick any two things, as long as you use one
of them where the A goes and the other where the B goes.

This idea, finding the **same thing underneath** two things that look different, is one of the most powerful ideas in
all of mathematics. Mathematicians do it all the time. When you know that 2 apples and 3 apples make 5 apples, you
also know that 2 cars and 3 cars make 5 cars. The things change, the counting stays the same.

Say it: *clap, clap, (shh), clap, clap, (shh)*. The core is *clap, clap, rest*: 3 beats long, an AAB pattern. If
you forget the rest, you get *clap, clap, clap, clap*, which is a different pattern.

So when you turn a sound pattern into beads, the rest needs a bead too. You could use an empty space, or a white bead.

Now you can **see** the sound. And you can play it back by looking at the picture: every blue square is a boom, every
orange circle is a tap. Musicians do something like this. They write music down with marks on paper, so other people
can play the same tune.

To turn it into a dance, choose a move for *boom* (maybe a big jump) and a move for *tap* (maybe a little clap).
Then: jump, clap, clap, jump, clap, clap. Same pattern, third set of clothes.

## Worked examples

**Example 1. From sound to colours.** Turn *clap, clap, snap* into a pattern of red and yellow blocks.

1. Find the core: *clap, clap, snap*. It is 3 long.
2. Give letters: clap is A, clap is A, snap is B. So it is **AAB**.
3. Choose a colour for A (red) and a colour for B (yellow).
4. The blocks go: **red, red, yellow, red, red, yellow, red, red, yellow.**

You could also choose yellow for A and red for B: *yellow, yellow, red*. That is still AAB, so it is still right.

1. Row (a): green, green, orange. Green is A, green is A, orange is B: **AAB**.
2. Row (b): triangle, square, square. Triangle is A, square is B, square is B: **ABB**.
3. Row (c): orange, blue, orange, blue: **AB**.
4. None of them have the same letters! So **no two match**. Row (a) and row (b) both have a core 3 long, but the pair
   is in a different place. In (a) the pair comes first; in (b) the pair comes last.

That was a trick question, and it shows why the letters help. Rows (a) and (b) **look** alike (both use two shapes,
both have cores of 3), but they are different patterns.

**Example 3. A long movement pattern.** The pattern is *touch your head, touch your toes*, again and again. What do
you touch on the 9th move? On the 10th?

1. The core is *head, toes*. It is 2 long. Toes is last in the core.
2. Toes comes on 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, ... (counting by 2s).
3. So the 10th move is **toes**. The 9th move is not in the count by 2s, so it is **head**.

**Example 4. A rest in a pattern.** A drummer plays *boom, rest, boom, rest, boom, rest*. Is this an AB pattern?

1. Boom is A. Rest is something different (no sound), so it is B.
2. Boom is A, rest is B... The letters go **A B A B**. Yes, it is an AB pattern, the same as *orange, blue, orange,
   blue*. The rest takes the place of the blue bead.

## Try it

1. Clap this pattern out loud: clap, clap, stamp, clap, clap, stamp. What is the core? What is the letter name?
2. Turn *tick, tock, tick, tock* into a pattern of circles and squares.
3. Turn the bead pattern *green, orange, orange, green, orange, orange* into a movement pattern. (Choose your own
   moves.)
4. Which pattern is the odd one out?
    - (a) clap, snap, clap, snap
    - (b) red, blue, red, blue
    - (c) hop, hop, sit, hop, hop, sit
    - (d) up, down, up, down
5. Give the letter name of *snap, clap, stamp, snap, clap, stamp*.
6. A pattern goes *clap, rest, rest, clap, rest, rest*. How many claps are there in the first 12 beats?
7. A dance goes *step left, step right, jump*, again and again. What is the 7th move? What is the 12th?
8. Make up a sound pattern that is ABB, and a movement pattern that is the same ABB pattern.

??? note "Answers"
    1. The core is *clap, clap, stamp*. Clap is A, clap is A, stamp is B: **AAB**.
    2. Tick is A and tock is B: AB. For example **circle, square, circle, square**. (Square, circle, square, circle
       is right too.)
    3. Green is A, orange is B: **ABB**. For example *jump, wave, wave, jump, wave, wave*. Any moves are fine, as long
       as the first move is different and the next two are the same.
    4. **(c)** is the odd one out. (a), (b) and (d) are all AB patterns. (c) is AAB: hop is A, hop is A, sit is B.
    5. Snap is A, clap is new so B, stamp is new so C: **ABC**.
    6. The core is 3 beats long and has 1 clap. 12 beats is 4 cores (3, 6, 9, 12), so there are **4 claps**. They come
       on beats 1, 4, 7 and 10.
    7. The core is 3 long, and *jump* is last: jumps come on 3, 6, 9, 12. So the 12th move is **jump**. The 6th move is
       a jump too, so the 7th starts a new core: **step left**.
    8. Many answers. For example: sound *stamp, clap, clap* and movement *sit, wave, wave*. Check that both give the
       letters A B B.

## Common mistake

**Thinking a pattern changes when its things change.** *Clap, stamp, stamp* and *red, blue, blue* are the same
pattern, ABB, even though one is loud and one is colourful. Write the letters to check.

**Thinking two patterns match because they use the same number of things.** *Green, green, orange* and *triangle,
square, square* both have a core of 3, but one is AAB and the other is ABB. The order matters, not just the count.
