Now think back to the toothpick triangles in [the last lesson](05-growing-shapes.md): 3, 5, 7, 9 toothpicks.

The dots and the toothpicks look nothing alike. One is dots in a V, the other is sticks in a zigzag. But they are
**the same pattern**. How can two such different things be the same? And how can you tell?

## The idea

### Four ways to show a pattern

A growing pattern can be shown in many **forms**. Here are the four most useful ones, all for the V shapes.

**1. Objects or a picture.** The dots above. You can see each shape and how it grows.

**2. A table.**

| Shape number | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dots | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 |

**3. A number list.** 3, 5, 7, 9, ...

**4. A sentence (the rule in words).** *Start at 3 and add 2 each time.*

All four describe exactly the same pattern. They are like one story told in four languages.

### What each form is good for

Why bother with four forms? Because each one is good at a different job.

- **The picture** shows **why** the pattern grows the way it does. In the V shapes, you can see that each new shape
  gets one more orange dot and one more blue dot: that is why it grows by **2**. And there is always just **one** green
  dot at the bottom. A list of numbers can never show you that.
- **The table** keeps the **shape number** next to the count. So you can see which shape has which count, and spot
  a rule that goes **straight** from the shape number to the count.
- **The number list** shows the **gaps** clearly: 3, 5, 7, 9 goes +2, +2, +2.
- **The sentence** is the easiest to **share**. You can say it on the phone, and the other person can make the whole
  pattern without seeing it.

### Translating between forms

Moving from one form to another is called **translating**, just as you might translate a word from English to French.
Here is how to do each move.

**Picture → table.** Count each shape, and write the count under its shape number.

**Table → number list.** Read the bottom row from left to right.

**Number list → sentence.** The first number is the start. Find the gaps; if they are all the same, the sentence is
*start at ... and add ... each time*. (That is what you did in [lesson 1](01-rules-in-words.md).)

**Sentence → picture.** This is the creative one. You have to **invent** a picture that grows by the right amount.
For *start at 3 and add 2 each time*, you need a first shape with 3 things, and each new shape must get 2 more.

### Why the V shapes and the triangles are the same pattern

Now we can answer the starting question. Translate both into tables:

| Shape number | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V-shape dots | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 |
| Triangle toothpicks | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 |

The tables are **identical**. So as patterns, they are the same, even though one is dots and the other is toothpicks.
It is just like *clap, stamp, stamp* and *orange, blue, blue* in [Level 1](../level-1/02-sounds-and-moves.md): same
pattern, different clothes.

And look at the pictures again. Both have **one special piece** (the green dot; the first green toothpick) and then
**two more pieces** for every shape (an orange and a blue). The same reason makes both grow the same way. Seeing the
same reason in two places is how mathematicians know two patterns really are the same, and not just by luck for the
first few shapes.

### Same numbers, different picture, same rule

Here is a second way to see why the same rule can have many pictures. *Start at 3 and add 2 each time* could be:

- V shapes of dots (above).
- A row of triangles from toothpicks (lesson 5).
- A plant that has 3 leaves on day 1 and grows 2 new leaves every day.
- Savings: a jar with 3 coins that gets 2 more coins each day.

Same table, same list, same sentence. Four different pictures.

**Example 3. Table → sentence, with a gap.** Find the missing number and the sentence.

| Shape number | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Count | 6 | 10 | ? | 18 | 22 |

1. From 6 to 10 is +4. From 18 to 22 is +4.
2. From 10 to 18 is +8, which is two gaps of 4. So the missing number is 10 + 4 = **14**.
3. Sentence: **start at 6 and add 4 each time.**

**Example 4. Picture → sentence, using the picture's reason.** A garden path has 1 flower pot at the gate, and then
each metre of path gets a pair of pots, one on each side. How many pots for 5 metres of path?

1. 1 metre: 1 + 2 = 3 pots. 2 metres: 5 pots. 3 metres: 7 pots.
2. Sentence: start at 3 and add 2 each time. (The same pattern as the V shapes!)
3. 5 metres: the gate pot, and 5 pairs: 1 + 10 = **11 pots**. (List: 3, 5, 7, 9, 11. ✓)

## Common mistake

**Thinking patterns that look different must be different.** V-shaped dots and toothpick triangles look nothing alike,
but their tables are the same. Compare the **numbers** (or the reason they grow), not the look.

**Losing the shape number.** A list like 3, 5, 7, 9 does not show which shape each number belongs to. When you need a
faraway shape (shape 20), use a table, so the shape number stays next to its count.
