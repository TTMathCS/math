# Level 6: Pythagoras and circles

*Geometry · level 6 of 8 · rough age 13-14 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

This level is about measuring what you cannot reach with a ruler. You prove the Pythagorean theorem three different
ways and use it for ladders, boxes, whole-number triples and the distance to the horizon. You put it on the coordinate
grid as the distance formula. You find out why $\pi$ is the same for every circle and where $\pi r^2$ comes from. And
you describe straight lines by their slope and equation.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [1. The Pythagorean theorem: three proofs](01-pythagorean-proofs.md) | Prove $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ by rearranging squares, by similar triangles and with Garfield's trapezoid, and use the converse to test for right angles |
| [2. Using Pythagoras](02-using-pythagoras.md) | Find missing sides, ladder heights, box diagonals and horizon distances, simplify square roots, and make Pythagorean triples |
| [3. Distance and midpoint](03-distance-midpoint.md) | Find the distance and the midpoint between two points, and use them to test triangles and circles |
| [4. Pi and the circumference](04-pi-circumference.md) | Explain why $\pi$ is the same for every circle, trap it between polygons, and use $C = \pi d$ |
| [5. Area of a circle, sectors and arcs](05-circle-area-sectors.md) | Show why the area is $\pi r^2$, and find the areas of sectors, segments and rings and the lengths of arcs |
| [6. Slope and the equation of a line](06-slope-line-equation.md) | Find slopes and equations of lines, and tell parallel and perpendicular lines from their slopes |
