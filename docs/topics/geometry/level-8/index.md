# Level 8: Proof, radians and vectors

*Geometry · level 8 of 8 · rough age 17 and beyond (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

The last level of geometry is where it meets university mathematics. It starts with proof, the way mathematicians
become certain, and then frees sine and cosine from triangles: angles measured in radians, the unit circle, and the
waves it draws. Vectors turn pictures into calculations, the dot product finds angles in any number of dimensions, and
the final lesson steps off the flat page onto a sphere, where triangles can have three right angles.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [1. How to write a geometric proof](01-geometric-proof.md) | Write two-column and paragraph proofs, use congruence, and prove the triangle angle sum and the isosceles triangle theorem |
| [2. Radians and the unit circle](02-radians-unit-circle.md) | Measure angles in radians, find exact values of sine and cosine for any angle, and prove identities such as $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ |
| [3. Graphs of sine and cosine](03-sine-cosine-graphs.md) | Sketch and read $y = A\sin(B(x - C)) + D$, and model wheels, tides and daylight with it |
| [4. Vectors: adding, scaling, length, components](04-vectors.md) | Add, subtract and scale vectors, switch between components and length-and-direction, and prove facts with vectors |
| [5. The dot product and projections](05-dot-product.md) | Find the angle between vectors, test for perpendicular, project one vector onto another and compute work |
| [6. Beyond Euclid: geometry on a sphere](06-sphere-geometry.md) | Use great circles and Girard's theorem, compute flight distances on Earth and Mars, and describe non-Euclidean geometry |
