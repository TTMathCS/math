# Circles, triangles, squares and rectangles

*Geometry · level 1, lesson 1 of 5*

## A question to start

Here is a riddle. I am a shape. I have 3 straight sides and 3 corners. You can turn me upside down, stretch me
tall, or squash me flat, and I keep my name. What am I?

You may have said "a triangle" right away. Good! But here is the surprising part. A triangle can look very
different. It can be tall and thin like a party hat. It can be low and wide like a slice of pizza lying down. It
can stand on its point. And it is **still a triangle**. Why? Because a shape's name does not come from how big it
is or which way it points. It comes from **counting**. In this lesson you will learn to count two things, sides and
corners, and then you will be able to name shapes like a real mathematician.

## The idea

### Four shapes to know

Look at these four shapes. You see them every day.

- A **circle** is round, like a coin, a clock or the top of a cup.
- A **triangle** has 3 sides, like a slice of sandwich cut from corner to corner.
- A **square** has 4 sides that are all the same length, like a cracker or a sticky note.
- A **rectangle** has 4 sides too, like a door, a book or a phone.

Geometry is the part of math about shapes. Mathematicians do not just look at a shape and guess its name. They
look at its parts.

### Sides and corners

A **side** is a straight edge of a shape. Run your finger along it. It does not bend.

A **corner** is where two sides meet. It is a pointy place. If you were an ant walking along the edge of the shape,
a corner is where you would have to turn.

Let us count the triangle. Put your finger on one corner (the dots). Walk along a side to the next corner. That is
side 1. Walk to the next corner. That is side 2. Walk back to where you started. That is side 3. So a triangle has
**3 sides and 3 corners**.

Now count the square: 4 sides and 4 corners.

Here is a good question. Why does a triangle have the **same** number of sides and corners? Think of the ant
again. The ant walks a side, then turns at a corner, then walks a side, then turns at a corner. Walk, turn, walk,
turn. Every side ends at a corner. So when the ant gets back home, it has walked the same number of sides as it
has turned corners. That is true for every shape with straight sides: **the number of sides and the number of
corners always match**.

### The circle is different

A circle has **no straight sides** and **no corners**. It is one smooth curve that goes round and round. The ant
walking on a circle never has to make a sharp turn. It just keeps gently turning a little bit all the time.

There is one more special thing about a circle. Find its middle point. Every point on the circle is the **same
distance** from the middle. That is why a wheel is a circle: the middle of the wheel (where the axle goes) stays
the same height as the wheel rolls. A square wheel would go bump, bump, bump!

### Squares and rectangles

A **square** has 4 straight sides and 4 corners. Its sides are all the **same length**. Its corners are all
**square corners**.

What is a square corner? It is the corner of a sheet of paper, of a book, of a door. It is not sharp and pointy, and
it is not wide and flat. You can test a corner with the corner of a piece of paper: if they match exactly, it is a
square corner.

A **rectangle** has 4 straight sides and 4 square corners. But its sides do **not** have to be all the same
length. Usually two sides are long and two sides are short.

Here is a fun fact that surprises many grown-ups. A square follows all the rules of a rectangle: 4 sides, 4
square corners. So **a square is a special kind of rectangle**, the kind with all sides the same. Every square is
a rectangle. But not every rectangle is a square, because most rectangles have long sides and short sides.

### Turning a shape does not change its name

Look at this square. Now turn it so it stands on one corner.

length, and 4 square corners. It is the **same square**, just turned. Turning a shape, or moving it, or flipping
it over, never changes its name.

## Worked examples

**Example 1. Name the shape.** A shape has 4 straight sides. Two sides are long and two are short. All 4 corners
are square corners. What is it?

1. Count the sides: 4. So it is not a triangle, and it is not a circle.
2. Check the corners: all square corners. So it is a rectangle or a square.
3. Check the sides: they are not all the same length (two long, two short).
4. So it is a **rectangle**, not a square. A door is a good example.

**Example 2. Count the shapes in a picture.** How many of each shape can you find in this house?

1. The big part of the house: 4 sides, all the same, square corners. **1 square.**
2. The roof: 3 sides, 3 corners. **1 triangle.**
3. The door: 4 sides, two long and two short, square corners. **1 rectangle.**
4. The windows: 4 equal sides, square corners. **2 squares.**
5. The sun: round, no corners. **1 circle.**

So the picture has 3 squares, 1 triangle, 1 rectangle and 1 circle. That is 6 shapes in all. (Did you count the big
square of the house as well as the windows? It is easy to miss the biggest shape!)

**Example 3. A shape with 6 corners.** Sam draws a shape with straight sides. He counts 6 corners. How many sides
does it have?

1. Remember the ant: walk a side, turn at a corner, walk a side, turn at a corner.
2. Every side ends at a corner, and every corner starts a new side.
3. So the number of sides is the same as the number of corners: **6 sides**.

A shape with 6 sides has a name too: a **hexagon**. You can see hexagons in a honeycomb. You will meet many more
shapes like this in level 2.

## Try it

1. How many sides does a triangle have? How many corners?
2. I have no corners and no straight sides. What am I?
3. Is a slice of pizza (cut from the middle) a triangle? Look closely at the side at the crust.
4. A shape has 4 sides that are all the same length and 4 square corners. What is it? Can you give it a second
   name too?
5. Find 3 rectangles in your room. Check their corners with the corner of a piece of paper.
6. Mia says: "This triangle is upside down, so it is not a triangle any more." Is she right?
7. A shape has 5 corners. How many sides does it have?
8. Draw a shape with 4 sides that is **not** a rectangle. What did you change?

??? note "Answers"
    1. 3 sides and 3 corners. Count with your finger: walk along a side, stop at a corner, three times, and you are
       back where you started.
    2. A circle. It is one smooth round curve, with no straight parts and no sharp turns.
    3. Almost, but not quite. Two sides are straight, but the crust is a **curved** side. A true triangle has 3
       straight sides. So a pizza slice is "nearly a triangle". A sandwich cut from corner to corner is a real
       triangle.
    4. A square. Its second name is **rectangle**, because it has 4 sides and 4 square corners, which is all a
       rectangle needs. A square is a special rectangle.
    5. Some ideas: a door, a book, a window, a picture frame, the screen of a tablet, a drawer. Their corners match
       the corner of the paper exactly.
    6. No. Turning a shape does not change its name. It still has 3 straight sides and 3 corners, so it is still a
       triangle.
    7. 5 sides. The number of sides always matches the number of corners, because every side ends at a corner.
       (A shape with 5 sides is called a **pentagon**.)
    8. One way: draw 4 sides but make the corners not square, like a shape that leans over, or like the right-hand
       shape in the "square corners" picture. Another way: make one side much shorter so the top is narrower than
       the bottom. You changed the **corners**. A rectangle must have 4 square corners.

## Common mistake

**Thinking a turned shape is a different shape.** Many children (and some grown-ups) say a square standing on
its corner is "a diamond, not a square", or that an upside-down triangle "is not a triangle". But a shape's name
comes from counting its sides and corners and checking them, not from which way it points. Turn the page and look
again: nothing about the shape changed.

**Thinking a square is not a rectangle.** A rectangle needs 4 sides and 4 square corners. A square has both. So
a square is a rectangle, just a special one with all its sides the same length.
