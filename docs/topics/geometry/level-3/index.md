# Level 3: Angles and lines

*Geometry · level 3 of 8 · rough age 9-10 (a guide for parents; anyone can start anywhere)*

An angle is an amount of turning, and once you can measure turning in degrees, a whole world of shapes opens up.
This level starts with turns you already know (quarter, half and full turns), gives them numbers with a protractor,
and then uses them to sort lines, triangles and four-sided shapes into families. It ends with coordinates: two
numbers that pin down any point on a grid.

| Lesson | You will be able to |
| --- | --- |
| [What an angle is](01-what-angle-is.md) | See an angle as a turn, and name right, acute, obtuse, straight and reflex angles |
| [Measuring angles with a protractor](02-measuring-angles.md) | Measure and draw angles in degrees, read the right scale, and explain why a full turn is 360° |
| [Parallel and perpendicular lines](03-parallel-perpendicular.md) | Recognise, test and draw parallel and perpendicular lines, and use the ∥ and ⊥ signs |
| [Triangles by sides and by angles](04-triangles-sides-angles.md) | Name any triangle by its sides and its angles, and explain why no triangle has two right angles |
| [The quadrilateral family tree](05-quadrilateral-family.md) | Sort squares, rectangles, rhombuses, parallelograms, trapezoids and kites, and say which is a special case of which |
| [Coordinates: finding points on a grid](06-coordinates-grid.md) | Read and plot points (along, up), measure along grid lines, and find a missing corner of a shape |
