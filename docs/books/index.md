# Math, branch by branch

Each book follows one branch of mathematics from its first idea to the questions mathematicians are still working
on. Read a book in order: every chapter needs only the ones before it.

**Books or Topics?** The [Topics](../topics/index.md) shelf is a ladder of short lessons by age, for children and
teens. A book is one continuous course for a reader who wants the whole road. Its first chapters move faster and
point to the matching Topics lessons for anyone who wants them slower; its later chapters go well past high school,
through university mathematics to the frontier.

## How a chapter works

- **It opens with a box** saying what you will learn, which chapters you need first, and which sections are
  beginner, intermediate, advanced or expert. Skip the harder sections on a first read and come back later.
- **Every idea comes with its reason.** Each theorem is proved, at the level of the chapter; where a proof is too
  long for the page, the chapter says so, gives the idea, and names where the full proof is.
- **Worked examples** show every step.
- **Check yourself** ends each chapter: questions from easy to hard, each with a full worked answer folded under it.
  Try first, then open it.
