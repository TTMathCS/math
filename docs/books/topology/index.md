# Topology

*A book in 20 chapters about shape when stretching is allowed: from the bridges of Königsberg, Euler's formula and
the Möbius strip to open sets, compactness and connectedness, the classification of surfaces, the fundamental group
and homology, knots, and the Poincaré conjecture. Each chapter starts from a question, proves what it claims, works
examples step by step, and ends with questions whose answers are folded underneath.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter needs only the ones before it; the **Next** link at the end of every chapter walks you
  through.
- **Levels inside a chapter.** The box at the top of each chapter says which sections are beginner, intermediate,
  advanced or expert. On a first read, skip the harder sections and come back later.
- **Pictures first.** Part I needs no algebra, only drawings and counting. Part II uses the language of sets
  ([Logic and foundations](../logic/index.md), chapters 5 and 6) and builds on the metric spaces of
  [Calculus and analysis](../calculus/index.md), chapter 20.
- **Groups from Part IV.** Algebraic topology turns shapes into groups; the [Algebra](../algebra/index.md) book's
  group chapters are the background.
- **Rubber, not rigid.** In [Geometry](../geometry/index.md), lengths and angles matter. Here only what survives
  bending and stretching counts, which is why a coffee cup and a doughnut are the same.

## Three ways in

- **For the fun of shapes:** chapters 1 to 3, then 10 and 18. Euler, the Möbius strip, the four colour theorem,
  every surface there is, and knots.
- **For a first university course:** chapters 4 to 9 (point-set topology), then 12 to 14.
- **For the big theorems:** chapters 13 to 17, then 19 and 20.
