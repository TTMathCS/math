# Probability

*A book in 20 chapters about chance: from coin flips and the birthday problem to Bayes' theorem, the bell curve,
Markov chains, Brownian motion and the mathematics underneath them. Each chapter starts from a question, proves what
it claims, works examples step by step, and ends with questions whose answers are folded underneath.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter needs only the ones before it; the **Next** link at the end of every chapter walks you
  through.
- **Levels inside a chapter.** The box at the top of each chapter says which sections are beginner, intermediate,
  advanced or expert. On a first read, skip the harder sections and come back later.
- **Calculus from Part III.** Parts I and II need only school algebra and the counting of the
  [Combinatorics](../combinatorics/index.md) book's first chapters. From chapter 9 on, integrals appear.
- **Slower help.** The first chapters point to the matching lessons on the [Topics](../../topics/probability/index.md)
  shelf, which teach the same ideas more gently, for younger readers.
- **Simulate.** A few lines of Python can flip a million coins; many chapters show how, so you can check a result
  against experiment.

## Three ways in

- **Starting out:** read chapters 1 to 7. They are the probability most people ever need, with the reasons written
  out.
- **You know calculus:** read chapters 9 to 12 for the normal curve and the central limit theorem, then 13 to 16 for
  random processes.
- **You want the foundations:** chapter 17, then 18 to 20.
