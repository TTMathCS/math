# Algebra

*A book in 22 chapters, from "think of a number" to the reason no formula solves every equation of degree five, and
on to the algebra mathematicians use today. Each chapter starts from a question, explains every new word, proves
what it claims, works examples step by step, and ends with questions whose answers are folded underneath.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter needs only the ones before it; the **Next** link at the end of every chapter walks you
  through.
- **Levels inside a chapter.** The box at the top of each chapter says which sections are beginner, intermediate,
  advanced or expert. On a first read, skip the harder sections and come back later.
- **Slower help.** The first chapters point to the matching lessons on the [Topics](../../topics/algebra/index.md)
  shelf, which teach the same ideas more gently, with pictures, for younger readers.
- **Do the questions.** Try each one before opening its answer.

## Three ways in

- **Starting out, or coming back after years away:** read chapters 1 to 9. That is all of school algebra, with the
  reasons written out.
- **You know school algebra and want to see why it works:** start at chapter 9 or 10, then go straight through to
  chapter 20, where the question of chapter 11 (why is there no formula for degree five?) is finally answered.
- **You have met groups before:** start at chapter 17 and use the glossary to look back.

Linear algebra (vectors, matrices, eigenvalues) gets its own book on this shelf; this one touches it only where
groups and modules need it.
