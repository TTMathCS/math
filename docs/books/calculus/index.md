# Calculus and analysis

*A book in 22 chapters about change and accumulation: from the speed of a falling stone and the area under a curve
to derivatives, integrals, series, differential equations, calculus in several variables, and then the careful
analysis underneath it all, the real numbers, uniform convergence, metric spaces, Lebesgue integration and Fourier
series. Each chapter starts from a question, proves what it claims, works examples step by step, and ends with
questions whose answers are folded underneath.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter needs only the ones before it; the **Next** link at the end of every chapter walks you
  through.
- **Levels inside a chapter.** The box at the top of each chapter says which sections are beginner, intermediate,
  advanced or expert. On a first read, skip the harder sections and come back later.
- **Algebra first.** You need school algebra and the idea of a function; the [Algebra](../algebra/index.md) book
  covers functions, exponentials and series if you want them first.
- **Slower help.** The first chapters point to the matching lessons on the [Topics](../../topics/change/index.md)
  shelf ("Change"), which teach the same ideas more gently, for younger readers.
- **Two passes.** Parts I to V are calculus: how to use it and why it works. Part VI goes back and builds it
  properly from the real numbers up, which is what mathematicians call analysis. Some proofs promised early are
  completed there.

## Three ways in

- **Starting out:** chapters 1 to 11. They are a full first course in calculus, with the reasons written out.
- **You know school calculus:** skim 1 to 5, then read 6, 12 to 14 and Part V.
- **You want the rigour:** Part VI, chapters 18 to 22, going back to earlier chapters for the examples.
