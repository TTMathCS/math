# Statistics

*A book in 20 chapters about learning from data: from reading a graph honestly to confidence intervals, regression,
Bayesian methods, causal inference and the replication crisis. Each chapter starts from a question, proves what it
claims, works examples step by step, and ends with questions whose answers are folded underneath.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter needs only the ones before it; the **Next** link at the end of every chapter walks you
  through.
- **Levels inside a chapter.** The box at the top of each chapter says which sections are beginner, intermediate,
  advanced or expert. On a first read, skip the harder sections and come back later.
- **Probability behind it.** Parts I and II need only school algebra. From Part II on, the
  [Probability](../probability/index.md) book supplies the distributions, the law of large numbers and the central
  limit theorem; from Part III on, calculus appears.
- **Slower help.** The first chapters point to the matching lessons on the [Topics](../../topics/statistics/index.md)
  shelf, which teach the same ideas more gently, for younger readers.
- **Honest data.** Every data set is either a well-known real one, described as it was published, or clearly marked
  as made up for the example.
