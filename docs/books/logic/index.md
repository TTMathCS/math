# Logic and foundations

*A book in 20 chapters about reasoning itself: from true and false, truth tables and how to write a proof, to sets
and infinity, formal logic, Turing machines and what computers cannot do, and Gödel's theorems on the limits of
proof. Each chapter starts from a question, proves what it claims, works examples step by step, and ends with
questions whose answers are folded underneath.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter needs only the ones before it; the **Next** link at the end of every chapter walks you
  through.
- **Levels inside a chapter.** The box at the top of each chapter says which sections are beginner, intermediate,
  advanced or expert. On a first read, skip the harder sections and come back later.
- **Nothing needed to start.** Part I needs no algebra at all, only patience with careful words. It is also the best
  place to learn how to read and write proofs before any other book on this shelf.
- **Slower help.** The first chapters point to the matching lessons on the [Topics](../../topics/logic/index.md) shelf
  ("Logic and puzzles"), which teach the same ideas more gently, for younger readers.
- **Honest about the famous results.** Gödel's and Turing's theorems are often quoted to mean more than they say. The
  chapters state exactly what was proved and what was not.

## Three ways in

- **To think and argue clearly:** chapters 1 to 4. They are the logic every other book on this shelf uses.
- **For infinity:** chapters 5 to 8, Cantor's road from counting to the axioms of set theory.
- **For the limits of proof and computation:** chapters 13, 14, 17 and 18, going back to Part III when a word is
  new.
