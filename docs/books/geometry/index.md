# Geometry

*A book in 21 chapters, from points, lines and angles to the curved space of general relativity and the shape of
the universe. Each chapter starts from a question, draws it, proves what it claims, works examples step by step, and
ends with questions whose answers are folded underneath.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter needs only the ones before it; the **Next** link at the end of every chapter walks you
  through.
- **Levels inside a chapter.** The box at the top of each chapter says which sections are beginner, intermediate,
  advanced or expert. On a first read, skip the harder sections and come back later.
- **Slower help.** The first chapters point to the matching lessons on the [Topics](../../topics/geometry/index.md)
  shelf, which teach the same ideas more gently, for younger readers.
- **Draw along.** A ruler, a compass and paper make every proof easier to follow.

## Three ways in

- **Starting out, or coming back after years away:** read chapters 1 to 8. That is all of school geometry and
  trigonometry, with every reason written out.
- **You know school geometry:** start at chapter 9 or 10, then read chapters 12 to 18 for the classical depth and
  the geometries Euclid never imagined.
- **You know calculus and want curved space:** read chapters 15 to 17, then 19 to 21.

The [Algebra](../algebra/index.md) book is the companion: chapter 14 here uses its chapter 19, and the symmetry
groups of chapter 10 are studied there in chapters 13 to 16.
