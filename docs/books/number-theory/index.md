# Number theory

*A book in 21 chapters about the whole numbers: from "which numbers divide which" to the Riemann hypothesis,
elliptic curves and the proof of Fermat's last theorem. Number theory is full of questions a child can ask and the
best mathematicians cannot yet answer; this book shows which is which. Each chapter starts from a question, proves
what it claims, works examples step by step, and ends with questions whose answers are folded underneath.*

## How to read it

- **In order.** Each chapter needs only the ones before it; the **Next** link at the end of every chapter walks you
  through.
- **Levels inside a chapter.** The box at the top of each chapter says which sections are beginner, intermediate,
  advanced or expert. On a first read, skip the harder sections and come back later.
- **Slower help.** The first chapters point to the matching lessons on the
  [Topics](../../topics/number-theory/index.md) shelf, which teach the same ideas more gently, for younger readers.
- **Compute.** A calculator or a few lines of Python turn every example into an experiment; most chapters show how.

## Three ways in

- **Starting out:** read chapters 1 to 6. They are the core every later chapter uses.
- **For codes and computers:** chapters 3 to 7 and 12, then the [cryptography book](https://cryptography.akadian.ca/book/).
- **You know some algebra and calculus:** read chapters 13 to 15 for the primes and the Riemann hypothesis, and 17 to
  21 for the modern subject. The [Algebra](../algebra/index.md) book's chapters on groups, rings and fields are the
  companion.
